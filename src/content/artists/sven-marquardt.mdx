Most people know Sven Marquardt as a face at the front of a queue: the tattooed, pierced figure who works the door at Berghain, Berlin's most mythologised club. That is a fraction of the picture. Marquardt is a trained photographer from East Berlin whose black-and-white portraits of the city's subcultures have been shown from Turin to Manila to Sydney, and who has been documenting Berlin's counterculture since the 1980s, years before the club that made him famous existed.

## East Berlin, DEFA and a Photographic Education

Marquardt was born on 3 February 1962 in East Berlin and grew up in the GDR, the son of a motorway builder and a medical-technical assistant, according to German Wikipedia. In 1982 he began training as a photographer and cameraman at the state film studio DEFA. After early publications in the East German papers Sonntag and Das Magazin, he spent 1985 to 1986 as an assistant to the photographer Rudolf Schäfer, then worked as a fashion photographer for the magazine Sibylle until the end of the decade.

He was part of the punk, new wave and art scene around Prenzlauer Berg, and the photographer Helga Paris is described as his mentor in his Fotografiska Berlin listing. In 1988 he joined the GDR's visual artists' association. German Wikipedia describes him as a photographic documentarian of the East German subculture, with pictures that describe the inside and outside of his existence in the big city.

## After the Wall: Ostgut and Berghain

The fall of the Wall did not make life easier for his work. Marquardt later told Die Zeit that he withdrew from photography because he felt he had nothing left to say. In the 1990s he began working the door for his brother Oliver Marquardt, who DJs as Jauche, at parties in the club Ostgut, a role Fotografiska dates to the late 1990s. When Ostgut closed in January 2003, he returned to taking pictures.

The successor to Ostgut was Berghain, which opened in 2004 in a former East Berlin heating plant. English Wikipedia names Marquardt as its head bouncer, and German Wikipedia says he still watches the door on most weekends, usually supported by other doormen. What the club will not do is explain itself: Berghain publishes no entry criteria, which [RTS.FM's explainer on who runs Berghain](/blog/who-runs-berghain-ownership-operators) notes in setting out what is and is not public about the operation. Marquardt himself has described the process as subjective rather than rule-based, as our [report on the Hollywood comedy about being turned away at Berghain](/blog/berghain-hollywood-comedy-film) relays. Treat the online lore about dress codes and secret rules as folklore, not policy.

The club's standing is its own story. [Berghain fell to 21st in DJ Mag's 2026 Top 100 Clubs poll](/blog/berghain-unvrs-dj-mag-top-100), a result we examined as a split between underground rooms and hyperclubs. ## The Photography: Portraits, Books and Exhibitions

Marquardt's best-known work is black-and-white portraiture. A Time Out listing for his Sydney show Future's Past describes portraits taken over several decades, documenting the Prenzlauer Berg scene and techno culture from the 1980s onward, many of them of people he knows.

His books include Zukünftig vergangen, a retrospective of photographs from 1984 to 2009 (Mitteldeutscher Verlag, 2010), Heiland (2011) and Wild verschlossen (2015). His autobiography, Die Nacht ist Leben, written with Judka Strittmatter, came out from Ullstein in 2014. Exhibitions listed on German Wikipedia include Götterdämmerung in Turin in 2014, Future's Past in Barcelona and Manila in 2016, Black Box with the DJ [Marcel Dettmann](/artists/marcel-dettmann) at the Polygon Gallery in Vancouver in 2018, Stageless at C/O Berlin in 2020, and the New York portrait series NYC/22, shown in Berlin in 2023. In April 2016, SBS reported his first Australian exhibition, Fotografien, opening at The Substation in Newport near Melbourne before moving on to Sydney.

## Collaborations, Film and Fashion

In September 2023, Marquardt presented 11+1 with the denim brand G-Star RAW during Berlin Art Week: 12 portraits of Berlin nightlife figures, cast largely from the street, plus a made-to-measure denim collection. Mixmag quoted him calling it "my homage to the club culture in Berlin."

He has also moved through film. Rosa von Praunheim made a short portrait, Der härteste Türsteher Berlins, in 2012, and David Dietl's Berlin Bouncer and Annekatrin Hendel's Schönheit & Vergänglichkeit, both from 2019, followed. In 2023 he appeared as Klaus in John Wick: Chapter 4, leading the title character to a fictional Berlin club.

## 2025 and 2026

On 11 September 2025, Marquardt's multimedia exhibition Disturbing Beauty ran for one night at the Haus der Visionäre in Berlin, with four large black-and-white portraits, video installations and live piano, according to Kaltblut Magazine, which says it had earlier been shown in New York, Montreal and Mexico City. Fotografiska Berlin listed an in-conversation talk with him for 15 January 2026, held in German and tied to its Helga Paris exhibition, which includes a portrait of Marquardt from his teens. Berghain, meanwhile, remains in the headlines through projects such as the Searchlight comedy developed around the door, and Fotografiska's own event listing describes him as having worked as a doorman since the late 1990s.

## FAQ

### How old is Sven Marquardt?

Sven Marquardt was born on 3 February 1962, according to German Wikipedia and Google Arts & Culture, which makes him 64 as of November 2026.

### Where is Sven Marquardt from?

He was born in East Berlin and grew up in the GDR. He works in Berlin.

### What nationality is Sven Marquardt?

German. German Wikipedia describes him as a German photographer and doorman, born in what was then East Berlin.

### Who is Sven Marquardt?

Sven Marquardt is a Berlin photographer and the head doorman of the club Berghain. He trained at the East German film studio DEFA, worked as a fashion and portrait photographer in the GDR, and later became one of the best-known faces of Berlin nightlife.

### Is Sven Marquardt the owner of Berghain?

No. Sources describe him as the club's head doorman, not an owner. English Wikipedia names him as head bouncer, and the legal notice on the club's own site, as reported in our explainer on who runs Berghain, does not list him.

### Does Sven Marquardt decide who gets into Berghain?

He is described as the head of the door and, according to German Wikipedia, works it most weekends with the support of other doormen. The club has never published entry criteria, and Marquardt has said the process is subjective, so no outside list of rules can be confirmed.

### What books has Sven Marquardt published?

His titles include Zukünftig vergangen (2010), Heiland (2011), the autobiography Die Nacht ist Leben with Judka Strittmatter (Ullstein, 2014) and Wild verschlossen (2015).

### What films has Sven Marquardt appeared in?

He features in Rosa von Praunheim's 2012 short Der härteste Türsteher Berlins, the 2019 documentaries Berlin Bouncer and Schönheit & Vergänglichkeit, and John Wick: Chapter 4 (2023), where he plays Klaus.

### What are Sven Marquardt's most recent projects?

In September 2025 he showed Disturbing Beauty at the Haus der Visionäre in Berlin, and Fotografiska Berlin scheduled a talk with him for 15 January 2026.
