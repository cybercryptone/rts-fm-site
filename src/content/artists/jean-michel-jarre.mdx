Jean-Michel Jarre has spent half a century proving that electronic music can be staged at the scale of a city. His 1976 album Oxygène, recorded in a converted kitchen in Paris, became one of the best-selling instrumental records ever made, and the open-air concerts that followed turned skylines into stages. In 2026 he is back at the centre of the conversation: Oxygène is 50, BMG has taken over his recorded catalogue, and he opened Amsterdam Dance Event's 30th edition.

## From Lyon to the Groupe de Recherches Musicales

Jarre was born on 24 August 1948 in Lyon, according to English and German Wikipedia and the MusicBrainz database. He is the son of the film composer Maurice Jarre, who left for Hollywood when his son was about five, and he was raised mainly by his mother and maternal grandparents. English Wikipedia describes a boy who struggled with classical piano but was drawn to Paris jazz clubs, and to the painter Pierre Soulages, whose layered canvases gave him the idea that a composer could work like a painter with frequencies.

He studied at the Lycée Michelet in Vanves, took harmony, counterpoint and fugue lessons with Jeannine Rueff, and in 1967 played guitar in a band called The Dustbins. In 1969 he joined the Groupe de Recherches Musicales in Paris, led by Pierre Schaeffer, the pioneer of musique concrète. The tape loops and found sounds of that environment, along with his early synthesizer experiments, shaped everything he later did.

## Oxygène, Motors and Dreyfus

Jarre's 1972 album Deserted Palace was an experimental record intended for film and television use, and from 1972 to 1975 he mainly wrote music and lyrics for other artists at the Disques Motors label run by Francis Dreyfus. Oxygène was assembled in a makeshift studio in his Paris apartment, with EMS VCS 3 and EMS Synthi AKS synthesizers and a Scully eight-track recorder, and it was turned down by several labels before Dreyfus agreed to release it. It came out in France in December 1976 on Disques Motors and internationally in 1977 through Polydor.

"Oxygène (Part IV)" reached number four on the UK singles chart, the album topped the French chart, and Wikipedia gives an estimated 18 million copies sold as of 2016, a figure that RTS.FM's [Oxygène at 50 article](/blog/jean-michel-jarre-oxygene-50-years-ade) treats as an estimate because other sources cite lower totals. Equinoxe followed in 1978 on Disques Dreyfus, the label Francis Dreyfus created next.

## Sound: Sequences, Samples and Lasers

The Jarre sound is melodic and sequence-driven. Wikipedia quotes one description of Oxygène as "bouncy, bubbling analog sequences and memorable hook lines", and a Qobuz retrospective cited in our Oxygène coverage frames it as the more romantic alternative to Kraftwerk's colder sound. Wikipedia lists Moby and Brian Canham of Pseudo Echo among the artists it influenced. RTS.FM's piece on [the best ambient albums of 2026](/blog/best-ambient-albums-2026) points to Oxygène as a reference point for listeners tracing the genre's popular roots.

He was never only a synth player. On Zoolook (1984) he built tracks from fragments of human voices speaking in many languages, an early case of sampling driving an entire album, and Oxygène 7-13 (1997) returned to the analogue instruments of the 1970s, dedicated to his mentor Schaeffer. That record also pulled him into dance culture: The Orb's "Toxygene", commissioned as a remix of "Oxygène 8", reached number four on the UK singles chart in February 1997.

## Concerts the Size of Cities

Jarre's second career is the open-air spectacle. Dates from his Wikipedia biography and official site:

- **14 July 1979:** a free Bastille Day concert at Place de la Concorde in Paris, reported at more than one million people.
- **Autumn 1981:** five concerts in Beijing and Shanghai, the first by a Western musician in China after Mao.
- **5 April 1986:** Rendez-Vous Houston, reported at about 1.5 million spectators.
- **8 and 9 October 1988:** Destination Docklands in London.
- **14 July 1990:** La Défense in Paris, reported at about two million.
- **14 July 1995:** the Eiffel Tower, held for the 50th anniversary of UNESCO.
- **6 September 1997:** Moscow, reported at about 3.5 million, which Wikipedia records as a world record for an outdoor concert audience.
- **31 December 1999:** The Twelve Dreams of the Sun at the Giza plateau in Egypt.

These attendance figures are organiser and Wikipedia figures, and Wikipedia is not always consistent with itself, so treat them as estimates. Jarre has also served as a UNESCO Goodwill Ambassador, is a Commander of the Legion of Honour (21 June 2021) and received the MOJO Lifetime Achievement Award in 2010 and the Stephen Hawking Medal for Science Communication in 2017.

## Electronica, Oxymore and the Dance Floor

In the 2010s Jarre turned collaborator. Electronica 1: The Time Machine (2015) paired him with acts including Gesaffelstein, M83, Tangerine Dream, John Carpenter and Armin van Buuren, and Electronica 2: The Heart of Noise (2016) added Pet Shop Boys, Hans Zimmer, Yello and Gary Numan. Oxymore, released on 21 October 2022 by Sony Music and Menart Records as a tribute to Pierre Henry, came with remixes from Brian Eno, Nina Kraviz, Armin van Buuren and Martin Gore, tying his catalogue to [Nina Kraviz](/artists/nina-kraviz) and the club world.

His links to techno run mainly through admiration. As RTS.FM's Oxygène coverage reports, [Jeff Mills](/artists/jeff-mills) told Dazed in 2016 that he grew up listening to Jarre, and Jarre called Mills an architect of electronic music. We found no documented technical lineage from Oxygène to Detroit or Berlin techno, and the article says so.

## 2025 to 2026: Samarkand, BMG and Oxygène at 50

His recent run has been busy. He performed at the closing ceremony of the 2024 Summer Paralympics on 8 September 2024, and his official biography lists a remastered Zoolook for its 40th anniversary on 21 February 2025, with a new track, "Moon Machine". German Wikipedia records a show at Stuttgart's Jazzopen on 11 July 2025, and the photo above comes from a June 2025 concert in Budapest. On 1 November 2025 he performed at Registan Square in Samarkand, his first concert in Uzbekistan, tied to the 43rd UNESCO General Conference, according to announcements from his own site and Sonic State. His 2026 listing includes a Special Summer Live Tour in Europe.

In July 2022 BMG bought his music publishing catalogue, and on 17 September 2026 Music Week reported that BMG would also license his recorded catalogue and future work on an exclusive basis, adding to its existing rights in the recordings of his first three albums. A book, *Machines by Jean-Michel Jarre: A History of Electronic Music* (Thames & Hudson), was announced for October 2026.

The anniversary release is due on 20 November 2026 from BMG, including a half-speed vinyl cut, a Blu-ray with the album's first Dolby Atmos mix, and new writing, as RTS.FM's [Oxygène at 50 report](/blog/jean-michel-jarre-oxygene-50-years-ade) details. On 21 October 2026 Jarre opened ADE with "Oxygène & Beyond" at AFAS Live as the festival's guest of honour, a show covered in our [ADE 2026 anniversary guide](/blog/ade-2026-30th-anniversary). A 3voor12 review, summarised in the [ADE 2026 wrap-up](/blog/ade-2026-wrap-up-30th-edition), praised the stylish opening and the laser harp in the encore, but found the music hard to dance to and the Oxygène 4 remix a low point. His official site lists a longer "Oxygène & Beyond" tour for 2027 and 2028.

## FAQ

### How old is Jean-Michel Jarre?

Jean-Michel Jarre was born on 24 August 1948, according to English and German Wikipedia and MusicBrainz, which makes him 78 as of November 2026.

### Where is Jean-Michel Jarre from?

Jarre was born in Lyon, France, and made Oxygène in his Paris apartment in 1976.

### What nationality is Jean-Michel Jarre?

French. Wikipedia describes him as a French composer, performer and record producer.

### Who is Jean-Michel Jarre?

Jean-Michel Jarre is a French composer and electronic music pioneer, born in Lyon on 24 August 1948, best known for the 1976 album Oxygène and for large outdoor concerts with lasers, projections and fireworks.

### What is Jean-Michel Jarre's best-known album?

Oxygène (1976). It was released in France in December 1976 on Disques Motors, reached number one in France, and is estimated by Wikipedia to have sold 18 million copies as of 2016.

### What label is Jean-Michel Jarre on?

Oxygène came out on Disques Motors, with Polydor handling international release. In 2022 BMG bought his publishing, and on 17 September 2026 it announced an exclusive licence for his recorded catalogue and future work, with the Oxygène 50th anniversary edition due on 20 November 2026.

### What is Jean-Michel Jarre doing in 2026?

He opened Amsterdam Dance Event on 21 October 2026 at AFAS Live, toured Europe in summer 2026 according to his official site, and is releasing the Oxygène 50th anniversary edition on 20 November 2026.

### Who is Jean-Michel Jarre's father?

The film composer Maurice Jarre, who left for the United States when Jean-Michel was a young child. They did not meet again until Jarre was 18, according to English Wikipedia.

### What does Jean-Michel Jarre's music sound like?

Melodic, sequencer-led instrumental electronic music built around analogue synthesizers, with a cinematic quality. His later work, from Zoolook onward, adds sampled voices, and his live shows are known for lasers and large-scale visuals.
