Terry Lee Brown Jr. is the name Norman Feller gave to a project that ended up defining a genre. Since 1995, that alias has carried some of German house music's most influential records, starting with an album that critics on two continents singled out as a genuine turning point for tech house, a term the artist himself is regularly credited with helping to establish through his own sound.

## From DJ Booth to Studio Alias

Feller started DJing in 1989 and spent the first half of the 1990s releasing tracks under a handful of different names before committing to music full time in 1994. The following year he built Terry Lee Brown Jr. as a dedicated house-oriented project, a vehicle distinct enough from his other work that it let him develop a sound entirely his own rather than splitting his identity across aliases. The name itself came together almost by accident: he liked "Terry" because it read as gender-neutral, picked "Lee" without much deliberation, and borrowed "Brown" from an American friend's surname, before Alexander Hendorf, the founder of the Mannheim label Plastic City, suggested tacking on the "Jr."

## Brother For Real and Tech House's Arrival

That alias found its signature record almost immediately. "Brother For Real," released in spring 1996, fused techno's loops and machine sounds into house music's groove and tempo, with detours into jungle, trance and easy listening that kept it from sitting neatly inside any one scene. Muzik, the UK's leading dance music magazine at the time, named it one of the top ten albums of 1996 alongside releases from DJ Shadow, Underworld and Faithless, and Chicago house pioneer Marshall Jefferson reviewed it with a verdict as blunt as it gets: "He is God." The record's American and British reception surprised critics who assumed, from the name, that its producer was American rather than German.

## Plastic City and the Mannheim Scene

Feller's home base has stayed the Mannheim area, and Plastic City, the label Hendorf founded there in 1993, became his long-term outlet, home to Terry's Café and Deep Train, two of the label's longest-running compilation series, alongside a run of Terry Lee Brown Jr. albums and singles that stretched across the following decades. Mannheim's electronic scene had its own institutional anchor forming at the same time: [Time Warp](/festivals/time-warp) grew out of Steffen Charles's Mannheim record shop nights that same year, 1993, part of the same city-wide wave of techno and house infrastructure that Plastic City helped build from the studio and label side.

## A Name That Outlasted the Trend Cycle

Tech house has cycled through several stylistic phases since 1996, and plenty of producers who helped originate it have either stopped releasing or shifted identities entirely. Terry Lee Brown Jr. has done neither, continuing to release through Plastic City and other outlets while remaining, on Discogs and Resident Advisor alike, one of the few original tech house names still credited under the same alias three decades on.

## FAQ

### Who is Terry Lee Brown Jr.?

Terry Lee Brown Jr. is the recording alias of German producer Norman Feller, created in 1995 and best known for the 1996 album "Brother For Real," widely credited as a foundational tech house record.

### Is it "Terry Lee Brown Jr." or "Terry Lee Brown Junior"?

Both spellings are in circulation. Discogs and his own releases generally credit him as "Terry Lee Brown Jr.," while Resident Advisor and some other platforms use the fully spelled-out "Terry Lee Brown Junior."

### Where does the name Terry Lee Brown Jr. come from?

Norman Feller chose "Terry" for its gender-neutral sound and "Brown" from an American friend's surname; Plastic City founder Alexander Hendorf suggested adding "Jr." to complete the name.

### What is Terry Lee Brown Jr.'s connection to Plastic City?

Plastic City, the Mannheim label Alexander Hendorf founded in 1993, has been Feller's primary outlet for decades, releasing his albums alongside long-running compilation series like Terry's Café and Deep Train.
