Rick Wade has spent more than three decades proving that Detroit's underground did not stop at techno. While the city's better-documented producers built their reputations on the Belleville Three's machine-funk lineage, Wade carved out a parallel, quieter career in deep house, steady kick drums, sparse keys, soul and disco samples folded into a melancholy, unhurried groove, work that earned him the kind of "producer's producer" reputation that rarely makes headlines but rarely goes out of style either.

## From Buchanan to Ann Arbor

Wade grew up in Buchanan, a small farming town in southwestern Michigan close enough to the Indiana border to pick up Chicago radio, where house music broadcasts reached him well before Detroit's own scene did. He got his start DJing in the mid-1980s at Ann Arbor's Nectarine Ballroom, the same venue where Jeff Mills was making his name on the techno side of the room, and built a following through "Journey to the Land of House," a mix show he hosted on the University of Michigan's WCBN that carried his name through Detroit and the wider Michigan scene well before he had a record out.

## Harmonie Park Records

Wade's studio career and his label history are the same story. Detroit producer Dan Bell encouraged him to start releasing the house music he had been making, and in 1993 the two of them formed Harmonie Park together, with Bell handling much of the business side so Wade could focus on production. The label became one of the most respected Detroit deep house imprints of its era and has stayed active for over three decades since, with Wade also releasing as Big Daddy Rick and Dr. Low-Tech and putting out full-length albums including "The Good, the Bad and the Deep" in 2008, "Requiem for a Machine Soul" in 2009 and "Golden Harvest" in 2015, the last of which leaned into instrumental hip-hop and downtempo territory.

## A Fixture of Detroit's Festival and Club Circuit

Wade has stayed rooted in Detroit's house scene through decades of lineup changes around him, including a slot on the Made in Detroit stage at [Movement](/festivals/movement-detroit) in 2009, the festival built on Hart Plaza to celebrate the city's own electronic music history. He has kept up a steady release pace since, with tracks like "Groove Tool" surfacing on Detroit-focused labels as recently as 2025, and he still plays Detroit venues alongside the city's newer generation of house and techno producers.

## FAQ

### Who is Rick Wade?

Rick Wade is a Detroit deep house DJ and producer who grew up in Buchanan, Michigan, and co-founded Harmonie Park Records with Dan Bell in 1993.

### What is Harmonie Park Records?

Harmonie Park is the Detroit deep house label Wade founded with Dan Bell in 1993, one of the city's most respected house imprints and still active more than thirty years later.

### What does Rick Wade's music sound like?

His productions are built on steady house tempos, punchy but understated drums, and sparse, melancholy melodies drawn from soul and disco, a formula he developed early in his career and has refined ever since.

Yes. He played the Made in Detroit stage at Movement in 2009, the Hart Plaza festival built around celebrating Detroit's own electronic music history.
