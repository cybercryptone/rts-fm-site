Lola Palmer has spent two decades building a reputation on selection rather than spectacle, a deep house and tech house DJ whose name keeps turning up on the credits of some of the scene's more discerning bookers, from Sonar Barcelona to RTS.FM's own studios. Her rise traces an unusually direct line from a Crimean weekend hobby to a full-time career built on the kind of warm, groove-first sets that reward repeat listening over instant impact.

## From Razdolnoe to Full-Time DJing

Palmer was born in Razdolnoe, a town in Crimea, Ukraine, and started DJing around 2004, initially just for the pleasure of sharing music, playing records at local bars on weekends rather than chasing bookings. That changed around 2012, when she moved into DJing full-time, cutting her teeth with regular sets for RTS.FM, the Kyiv-founded radio and video platform, and building a following through consistent, well-selected online mixes rather than a single breakout record. She has since played across France, Italy, Greece and Georgia, and RA has described her as one of Europe's better deep techno and deep house selectors, a reputation that also earned her a slot at Sonar, the long-running Barcelona festival known as much for booking depth as headline names.

## A Deep-Rooted RTS.FM Relationship

Palmer's connection to RTS.FM goes further than a single archive upload. The channel's own back catalogue holds five separate Lola Palmer DJ sets, all recorded between July 2010 and October 2011, each of which has individually passed 10,000 views, an unusually high number of sets for one artist on a channel with thousands of uploads. That kind of repeat booking, five distinct dates across roughly fifteen months, points to something close to an informal residency rather than a one-off appearance, and it places her among a small group of artists RTS.FM returned to again and again during that period.

## Sound, Labels and Collaborations

Palmer's own description of her sound sticks close to what she plays: deep, groove-driven house and tech house built around atmosphere over aggression. On the production side, she has worked alongside two of her own stated influences, Terry Lee Brown Jr and The Timewriter, veteran tech-house producers known for the Driftwood and Tonika labels, and that relationship led to a "What You Feel" EP with Terry Lee Brown Jr remixes on Ready Mix Records. Her releases have also landed on MixCult, the St. Petersburg-rooted label and radio project where she has held a residency, on Berg Audio with the "Stranger" / "Classic Dub" and "Elements" releases, and on the UK's Acunadeep label, where she paired with producer V I F for the joint "Salt Drop" project.
