Ask anyone who caught Jeff Mills working three decks and a drum machine at once, and they will tell you it never looked like ordinary DJing. It looked like something being assembled live, under pressure, with no safety net. That reputation was not built overnight. Mills has been doing this since Detroit's earliest techno broadcasts in the 1980s, and he is still doing it now, which puts him in a category of dance music figures that is smaller than the genre likes to admit.

## Detroit Roots and the Wizard

Mills was born on June 18, 1963, in Detroit, and came up in the same city, the same era, that produced the Belleville Three, the trio whose story anchors [Movement festival's own 20th anniversary and Detroit's history as techno's birthplace](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years). Before he was known as a producer, Mills was known as a radio mixer. As "the Wizard," he hosted a nightly show, first on WDRQ and later WJLB, where he built a local following on beat juggling, rapid scratching and mixes that jumped between New Wave, industrial, electro and the emerging Detroit and Chicago house and techno sounds. The speed and precision of those broadcasts became part of Detroit radio lore well before Mills had a record out under his own name. The Detroit lineage he came out of runs through [Juan Atkins](/artists/juan-atkins), [Derrick May](/artists/derrick-may) and [Kevin Saunderson](/artists/kevin-saunderson).

## Underground Resistance

In the late 1980s, Mills co-founded [Underground Resistance](/artists/underground-resistance) with "Mad" Mike Banks, with [Robert Hood](/artists/robert-hood) among the early members, a group that positioned itself explicitly outside the mainstream music industry and treated techno as a political project as much as a musical one. Wikipedia says Mills left in 1991 (Discogs gives 1992) and relocated to New York, then Berlin, where he became a resident at Tresor, and began building a solo career on his own terms.

## Axis Records and the Minimal Turn

Mills founded [Axis Records](https://www.axisrecords.com/) in 1992 (the label's own site gives that year), and the label became the vehicle for the sound he is now most associated with: stripped-down, hypnotic, relentlessly functional techno built around repetition rather than melody. Axis spawned sub-labels, including Purpose Maker and Tomorrow, that let Mills separate his rawer, DJ-tool material from more conceptual, album-length work. His Tresor residency tied Detroit's originators to the Berlin scene, and Frankfurt's Omen, the club at the center of [Cocoon Recordings' own 25-year story](/blog/cocoon-recordings-25-years), counted Mills among the DJs it hosted during its 1988 to 1998 run.

## Sound and Style

Mills built a technical reputation that is unusual even among techno's more skilled selectors. He is known for working with more than two decks at once, and for playing a Roland TR-909 drum machine live over the mix, a technique he described to [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/jeff-mills-roland-tr-909-tony-allen/). His 1995 Liquid Room set in Tokyo used two turntables and two reel-to-reel tape machines. That approach produces a sound that is fast, sparse and mechanically precise, techno stripped to kick, hat and a handful of moving parts, which is exactly what minimal and hypnotic techno as genre terms are usually describing. His catalogue extends well past club records. He scored a new soundtrack for Fritz Lang's silent film Metropolis, released in 2000, later performed a live four-turntable cinemix for Lang's earlier silent feature Woman in the Moon (1929), recorded Blue Potential, a 2006 live album with the Montpellier Philharmonic Orchestra, and released Tomorrow Comes the Harvest, a 2018 EP with Afrobeat drummer Tony Allen on Blue Note. In April 2017 the French Order of Arts and Letters promoted him to Officier, with Jack Lang presenting the award, according to [FACT](https://factmag.com/2017/05/02/jeff-mills-awarded-frances-order-arts-letters-medal), which also notes he had been made a Knight in 2007. His work with [Jean-Michel Jarre](/artists/jean-michel-jarre) is covered in our [Oxygene retrospective](/blog/jean-michel-jarre-oxygene-50-years-ade).

## Career Highlights and the Next Generation

Mills' guest bookings, including [Movement Detroit](/festivals/movement-detroit) in 2007, 2014 and 2026, read like a map of techno's most important rooms, including a residency-era relationship with clubs across Germany, one of which was Munich's BLITZ, the Ilian Tape-affiliated venue that closed in 2026 after [nine years as one of the country's most respected small techno rooms](/blog/blitz-munich-closure), with Mills among the guest names that appeared on its programming history.

His relevance has not dimmed with age. In July 2026, Mills was billed for the Junction 2 x fabric weekend in London, where he is listed in Room 01 at [Junction 2 x fabric by Night](/blog/nina-kraviz-jeff-mills-fabric-junction-2), and [Nina Kraviz](/artists/nina-kraviz) was announced for the same day, a pairing that read as a direct line between Detroit's founding generation and the artists carrying techno forward today, the same artists tracked across [Nina Kraviz's own eventful 2026](/blog/nina-kraviz-2026-recap). Few DJs from Mills' original cohort still headline rooms alongside performers thirty years their junior on equal footing. Fewer still do it while remaining recognizably, unmistakably themselves behind the decks.

## FAQ

### How old is Jeff Mills?

Jeff Mills was born on 18 June 1963, according to English and German Wikipedia, which makes him 63 as of November 2026.

### Where is Jeff Mills from?

Jeff Mills was born in Detroit, Michigan, and is an American DJ and producer. The Detroit techno scene he came up in is covered above.

### What is Jeff Mills' real name?

Jeff Mills is the name he records and performs under, and we found no separate stage name. Searches for "Jeffrey Mills" refer to the same artist, but we have not found a source that gives Jeffrey as a formal birth name. His known aliases are The Wizard and True Faith.

### How do you spell Jeff Mills' name?

The Detroit artist is Jeff Mills. People also search for "Jeffrey Mills" and the misspelling "Jeff Mils"; both lead to the same artist covered on this page.

### What genre does Jeff Mills play?

Detroit techno and minimal techno, built on long, rhythm-driven sets and releases on Axis Records.

### Who is Jeff Mills?

Jeff Mills is a Detroit-born DJ, producer and composer, born June 18, 1963, who co-founded Underground Resistance and later founded Axis Records. He is regarded as one of the foundational figures of Detroit techno and a pioneer of the minimal and hypnotic techno style.

### What was "the Wizard"?

The Wizard was Mills' identity as a radio mix show host in Detroit in the 1980s, first on WDRQ and later WJLB, known for fast, technical beat juggling and scratching across New Wave, industrial, electro and early Detroit techno.

### What is Axis Records?

Axis Records is the label Mills founded in 1992 to release his own minimal, stripped-down techno productions, along with sub-labels including Purpose Maker and Tomorrow for different strands of his output.

### Was Jeff Mills part of Underground Resistance?

Yes. Mills co-founded Underground Resistance in the late 1980s with "Mad" Mike Banks, alongside Robert Hood in the early lineup, before leaving the collective in the early 1990s (Wikipedia says 1991, Discogs 1992) to pursue a solo career through Axis Records.

### What makes Jeff Mills' DJ sets distinctive?

Mills is known for working with more than two decks at once and for playing a live Roland TR-909 drum machine over his mixes. His fast, mechanically precise style has few direct peers in techno.

### Is Jeff Mills still touring?

Yes. He toured the Live at the Liquid Room 30th anniversary show from November 2025, was touring Asia in late May 2026, was billed for MUTEK Montreal in August 2026, and Songkick lists a 13 November 2026 date in Mexico City. Check each venue for current dates.

### What is Jeff Mills' latest album?

The Trip To Vega, released on Axis Records on 19 June 2026, is his latest solo album. The Gamma Rays EP followed on 30 October 2026, and the second Tomorrow Comes The Harvest album, The Forbidden Planet, is announced for 20 November 2026.

### What is Jeff Mills' best-known track?

"The Bells", released on Purpose Maker in the mid-1990s, is widely treated as his signature track and was remastered for Axis's Director's Cut series in 2019.

### What is Live at the Liquid Room?

It is a 1996 mix album recorded at Liquidroom in Tokyo on 28 October 1995, in which Mills mixed on two turntables and two reel-to-reel machines at high speed. Axis marked the 30th anniversary with a tour and book, CD and cassette editions.

### Has Jeff Mills won any awards?

France's Order of Arts and Letters promoted him to Officier in 2017, after naming him a Knight in 2007, per FACT.

### What labels does Jeff Mills run?

Axis Records, founded in 1992, along with its sub-labels Purpose Maker and Tomorrow. Recent Axis series include the Expressionist Series and the Millsart EPs.

### Is Jeff Mills the same person as Underground Resistance?

No. Mills is one of UR's co-founders and left the collective in the early 1990s. Read the [Underground Resistance profile](/artists/underground-resistance) for the full history.
