Rhadoo is one of the people who turned a small Bucharest scene into a sound other cities copied. Born Radu Bogdan Cilinca, the Romanian DJ and producer is one third of the trio behind the Arpiar label, a regular on the long-set circuit, and a figure the press describes as a first-generation pioneer while he himself says very little in public.

## Early Life and Path Into Music

According to [XLR8R's history of Romanian house and techno](https://xlr8r.com/features/a-deep-dive-into-the-present-and-history-of-romanian-house-and-techno/), Rhadoo was born in 1975 in Galați and belongs to the first generation of DJs that emerged after the 1989 revolution. [Bandcamp Daily's 2026 scene report](https://daily.bandcamp.com/scene-report/romanian-minimal-techno-scene-report) says he began spinning in the 1990s.

XLR8R says he met the booking agent Catalin Ghinea at Club A in Bucharest in 1990 and played tapes there two nights a week during that decade. In the early 2000s he had a weekly radio show and travelled around Romania to play. In 2002 he was in Ibiza with Petre Inspirescu, where, XLR8R reports, the pair spent their days hunting for minimal records because they could not afford to go out. Wikipedia's page on the [2003 MTV Romania Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Romania_Music_Awards_2003), held on 5 June 2003 at Sala Polivalentă in Bucharest, lists Rhadoo as the winner of Best DJ.

## Arpiar and the RPR Trio

Rhadoo, Raresh and Petre Inspirescu run the label Arpiar, written [a:rpia:r] by the label itself. XLR8R says the trio also DJ together as RPR, short for Rhadoo, Pedro and Raresh, and that the label was formed in 2006 or 2007. Our [Raresh profile](/artists/raresh) covers the same label from his side, including why sources disagree on the founding year.

Bandcamp Daily says the trio were coached by [Ricardo Villalobos](/artists/ricardo-villalobos), who was enjoying a surge of popularity in Bucharest at the time and released a handful of his records on Arpiar. The label's own site, [arpiar.ro](https://www.arpiar.ro/), is a bare contact page, in keeping with the low profile the three keep. For the wider story of how Arpiar, the agency Sunrise and a beach festival fed one another, see our [history of Romanian minimal techno](/blog/rominimal-history-romanian-minimal-techno).

## Sound and Style

Discogs describes him as a Romanian techno, tech-house and minimal DJ, producer and remixer, and that range is audible in his discography, which runs from tech-house remixes in the mid-2000s to the patient, swung records the Romanian scene became known for. XLR8R's 2015 feature on the scene notes that some of his releases are pure techno, a reminder that the label "minimal" fits him loosely. Bandcamp Daily frames the wider Romanian style as a blend of folk and classical elements in slow dance music, which is as close to a definition as the scene itself offers.

It is a sound built for long nights rather than peak-time drops. His influence is visible beyond Romania: our [Traumer profile](/artists/traumer) notes that the Paris producer's shift around 2016 drew on the Bucharest scene around Rhadoo, Raresh and Petre Inspirescu.

## Labels and Releases

The release that gets named most often is the 12-inch *Dor Mit Oru*, issued on Cadenza in April 2008. Discogs lists four tracks, "Bau", "Baisbe Alt Bass", "Elan" and "Slagare", spread across two Cadenza catalogue numbers, 22 and 25, and Bandcamp Daily singles it out as a Cadenza release of original songs. In October 2013 he mixed *fabric 72* for the London club's long-running series, which Discogs lists with 17 tracks and Bandcamp Daily calls revered. His *Arhiva EP* appeared on Understand in 2012, a label run by his Our Own partner Cezar Lazăr, and Discogs also lists a *Semantics EP* on Nervmusic Records in 2019.

Beyond records, XLR8R says he co-founded Our Own, a distributor and booking agency, with [Cezar Lazăr](/artists/cezar-lazar). XLR8R also notes that Rhadoo has not spoken publicly and avoids the media, so interviews are close to nonexistent and this profile sticks to releases and reported facts.

## Career Highlights

- Best DJ at the 2003 MTV Romania Music Awards, according to Wikipedia.
- A co-founder of Arpiar with Raresh and Petre Inspirescu, 2006 or 2007 depending on the source.
- *Dor Mit Oru* on Cadenza in 2008 and *fabric 72* in 2013, both documented on Discogs.
- Described by XLR8R in 2015 as a leading pioneer of the first generation who still plays regularly.
- Cited by Bandcamp Daily as having played Nowadays, Horst Festival and Waking Life.

He also sits close to the story of Sunwaves. XLR8R's 2015 piece says the promoter Catalin Ghinea, whom Rhadoo met in 1990, co-owns the festival, and our [Sunwaves page](/festivals/sunwaves) traces the beach festival at Mamaia from 2007 to its last Romanian edition in 2025.

## FAQ

### How old is Rhadoo?

Rhadoo was born in 1975, according to XLR8R, which makes him 50 or 51 depending on his birthday (age 50 or 51 as of October 2026). He has not published an exact date of birth that we could find.

### Where is Rhadoo from?

Rhadoo is Romanian. XLR8R says he was born in Galați and built his career in Bucharest, where he played at Club A from 1990.

### What genre does Rhadoo play?

Rhadoo plays minimal techno and minimal house in the Romanian style, with tech-house and techno elements. Discogs describes him as a techno, tech-house and minimal DJ, producer and remixer.

### What is Rhadoo's real name?

His real name is Radu Bogdan Cilinca, as given by Bandcamp Daily, XLR8R and Discogs. He records and performs as Rhadoo.

### What is Arpiar?

Arpiar, written [a:rpia:r], is a Romanian label run by Rhadoo, Raresh and Petre Inspirescu. XLR8R says it was formed in 2006 or 2007, and its members also DJ together as RPR.

### Has Rhadoo released on Cadenza?

Yes. His 12-inch Dor Mit Oru came out on Cadenza in April 2008, according to MusicBrainz and Discogs, with four original tracks across two catalogue numbers.

### Who runs Our Own?

XLR8R says Our Own is a distributor and booking agency that Rhadoo founded with Cezar Lazăr. Cezar also runs the label Understand.
