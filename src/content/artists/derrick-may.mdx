Derrick May is one of three people who can fairly be said to have invented techno, and the only one whose name is permanently attached to a single piece of music: "Strings of Life", the 1987 record that carried Detroit's synthetic soul into British clubs and, from there, the rest of the world. He built the label that released it, Transmat, and spent the next four decades as a DJ who treats a set as a piece of theatre, which is a large part of why his name still sits near the top of festival posters for the genre he helped create.

## Early Life and Path Into Music

According to the [Detroit Historical Society](https://detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/may-derrick), May was born in Detroit in 1963 and raised mostly by his mother. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_May_(musician)) and [German Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_May) give his birth date as 6 April 1963. At Belleville High School, in the suburbs west of the city, he met Juan Atkins and Kevin Saunderson, and the three traded mixtapes built from Parliament-Funkadelic, Kraftwerk, Gary Numan and the late-night radio shows of The Electrifying Mojo. The Historical Society notes that May's mother moved to Chicago while he was still in school, and his visits there exposed him to the city's young house scene, an influence that would shape the way he played records. Out of those friendships came the trio's DJ collective, Deep Space Soundworks, and eventually the group the world now calls the Belleville Three, whose story is told in more detail in our look at [Detroit techno's origins and the Movement festival](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years).

## Transmat and Strings of Life

May's first record, "Let's Go", came out in 1986 on [Juan Atkins](/artists/juan-atkins)' Metroplex label, after which he started his own, Transmat, according to the Detroit Historical Society. Transmat's [own site](https://transmatrecords.com/) dates the label to 1986 and presents its early run as a blueprint for the genre. Most of May's best-known material appeared in the following three years under the name Rhythim Is Rhythim: "Nude Photo", "It Is What It Is", "Kaos" and "Strings of Life", according to Wikipedia's account of his [career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_May_(musician)). That page notes that Frankie Knuckles named "Strings of Life" and that the record landed with particular force in Britain during the 1987 to 1988 house explosion.

The record's reputation rests on a choice that was unusual for dance music at the time: a rhythm track with no bassline carrying a piano and string figure that sounds closer to a film score than to a drum machine workout. May has described his own approach as "Hi-Tek Soul", and Wikipedia records his definition of it as ["George Clinton meeting Kraftwerk in an elevator"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_May_(musician)). The same entry cites AllMusic's John Bush on the sound itself, a balance of streamlined percussion with string samples and a warmth picked up from time spent in Chicago, around DJs such as Ron Hardy and Frankie Knuckles.

## The Music Institute

In May 1988 a downtown Detroit after-hours club called the Music Institute opened its doors. According to [Red Bull Music Academy's history of the club](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/05/music-institute-nightclubbing), the Friday-night residents were May and D-Wynn, who replaced Kevin Saunderson early on, and they played the drum-driven, forward-looking music that the local labels Transmat, KMS and Metroplex were making in their warehouses. The club is often described as the first room anywhere to give the sound a dedicated platform. It also features in our piece on [DJ fees and the underground](/blog/dj-fees-underground-promoters-squeeze-2026), which notes that May later said the residents played for free for almost a year before fighting for a weekly wage.

## Labels and Mentorship

Transmat is as much a talent incubator as a catalogue. The label's site credits it with nurturing a second generation of Detroit producers, naming Stacey Pullen as Silent Phase, [Carl Craig](/artists/carl-craig) as Psyche, Kenny Larkin as Dark Comedy and James Pennington as Suburban Knight. Its sub-label, Fragile, is described there as a second outlet that also fed the global techno boom, and Wikipedia reports that Craig and Pullen themselves distinguished the two labels, Transmat for original creation and Fragile for work built on sampling. The Historical Society adds that May took long breaks from recording and had largely stopped making new music by 1991, while continuing to perform and to guide Transmat's evolution.

## Movement and a Public Voice

May directed what is now known as Movement for two years. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_May_(musician)) says he took over the riverfront festival in 2003 and renamed it Movement, replacing the earlier Detroit Electronic Music Festival title, and the Historical Society says he remains closely aligned with the event. The festival's later history, including Kevin Saunderson's stewardship and the Paxahau era, is covered on our [Movement Detroit festival page](/festivals/movement-detroit).

In a 2019 [Mixmag interview](https://www.mixmag.net/feature/derrick-may-interview), given ahead of a performance with the City of London Sinfonia at the Southbank Centre, May said he grew up in a middle-class household raised by a single mother and rejected the idea that Detroit's techno pioneers came from poverty. He also credited [Jeff Mills](/artists/jeff-mills) with getting to orchestral techno first and criticised festivals and promoter-driven businesses for harming local club culture. It is a useful reminder that the music's founders have always been vocal about the economics of the scene they started.

## Discography Notes

Wikipedia lists his albums as Innovator (1996) and Mysterious Traveller (2002), the latter made with System 7, alongside DJ mixes including The Mayday Mix (1997), Derrick May x Air (2010) and Fact Mix 339 (2012). He also appears in several documentaries on the genre, including High Tech Soul (2006). Discogs, which lists him with aliases including Rhythim Is Rhythim, Mayday and Long Ago, remains the most complete [catalogue](https://www.discogs.com/artist/650) of his releases.

## FAQ

### Who is Derrick May?

Derrick May is an American producer, DJ and label owner from Detroit, one of the Belleville Three who created techno in the early 1980s. He founded Transmat Records and is best known for "Strings of Life", recorded as Rhythim Is Rhythim.

### How old is Derrick May?

Derrick May was born on 6 April 1963, according to Wikipedia, German Wikipedia and Discogs, which makes him 63 (age 63 as of October 2026).

### Where is Derrick May from?

He was born in Detroit, Michigan, and went to high school in the nearby suburb of Belleville, where he met Juan Atkins and Kevin Saunderson.

### What genre does Derrick May play?

Derrick May plays techno, specifically the Detroit style he calls Hi-Tek Soul, which combines machine rhythms with strings, soul and funk influences.

### What is Derrick May's real name?

Derrick May records and performs under his own name. He has also released music as Rhythim Is Rhythim, Mayday and Long Ago.

### What is Derrick May's most famous track?

"Strings of Life", released in 1987 as Rhythim Is Rhythim on his own Transmat label, is his best-known record and is widely regarded as a foundational techno track.

### What labels does Derrick May run?

He founded Transmat in 1986 and its sub-label Fragile. Transmat has released music by Carl Craig, Stacey Pullen, Kenny Larkin and James Pennington among others.

### Who are the Belleville Three?

The Belleville Three are Juan Atkins, Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson, three friends from Belleville High School outside Detroit who are credited with creating techno in the 1980s.
