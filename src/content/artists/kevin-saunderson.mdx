Kevin Saunderson is the member of the Belleville Three who took Detroit techno furthest into the pop charts without leaving the underground behind. With Inner City he scored international dance hits; as Reese he recorded a bassline that drum and bass producers kept reusing for a decade; and through KMS Records he has kept a Detroit label running since the mid-1980s. If [Derrick May](/artists/derrick-may) is techno's romantic and [Juan Atkins](/artists/juan-atkins) its architect, Saunderson is the one whose records crossed over most visibly into the charts.

## Early Life and Path Into Music

Saunderson was born in Brooklyn, New York, and grew up in Belleville, Michigan, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Saunderson) and the [Detroit Historical Society](https://detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/saunderson-kevin). The two sources give different birth dates for him, so only the year, 1964, is stated here. At Belleville High School he met Atkins and May, and the Historical Society says he learned the landscape of electronic music from the two older colleagues and others in their circle such as Eddie Fowlkes. While studying at Eastern Michigan University he joined Deep Space, the DJ collective started by Atkins and May, spinning under the name Master Reese. Wikipedia says he was inspired to make his own music after watching the long process of Atkins and May completing "Let's Go". His first single, "Triangle of Love", came out under the alias Kreem on Atkins's Metroplex label. The wider origin story, including the radio show that shaped the trio, is in our piece on [Detroit techno and Movement](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years).

## KMS Records and Inner City

The Historical Society says Saunderson formed KMS Records in 1985, naming it after his initials, Kevin Maurice Saunderson. Discogs lists KMS Records and the production company KMS Productions as his imprints.

Inner City came next. Wikipedia reports that in 1987 Saunderson recorded a backing track at home but needed a vocalist, and that the Chicago house producer Terry Baldwin suggested Paris Grey, who flew to Detroit and wrote the lyrics to "Big Fun". The UK entrepreneur Neil Rushton then included the track on Virgin's compilation Techno: The New Dance Sound of Detroit, and it was released as a single. Wikipedia's article on [Inner City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_City_%28band%29) lists "Big Fun" in 1988 at number 8 in the UK and number 1 on the US dance chart, and "Good Life" at number 4 in the UK, with the group's debut album Paradise following in 1989. The same article reports that Inner City had nine UK top 40 hits. A new incarnation of the group has included his son Dantiez Saunderson since 2017, per that entry.

## The Reese Bassline

Saunderson's most durable contribution may be the one that sounds least like a signature. According to Wikipedia, the bassline of "Just Want Another Chance", released in 1988 under his Reese pseudonym on Incognito Records, became hugely influential in jungle and drum and bass. It was played on a Casio CZ-1000 using phase distortion synthesis, and the article cites later records built on it, among them Renegade's "Terrorist" (Moving Shadow, 1994) and Alex Reece's "Pulp Friction" (Metalheadz, 1995).

## Sound and Style

Wikipedia quotes AllMusic's John Bush on Saunderson's techno, describing it as a dense rhythmic assault of samples and heavy percussion from his first production, while the Inner City records were slicker, house-inspired tracks supporting vocalists. He has run other aliases and projects alongside them. E-Dancer is the one Wikipedia describes as more underground, and its first album, Heavenly, came out in 1998. [Carl Craig](/artists/carl-craig)'s Planet E label has also released Saunderson's music, with a Faces & Phases compilation in its discography, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_E_Communications).

## Movement and Detroit

Saunderson has performed at Detroit's annual electronic music festival since its start in 2000, and organized the 2005 edition, according to the Historical Society. Wikipedia's article on Derrick May notes that Saunderson and Ade' Mainor produced a festival called Fuse-In in 2005. By the time of the 20th anniversary in 2026, as covered in our [Movement retrospective](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years), he was on the programme in a collaborative set with Dantiez, and his KMS label was represented on the lineup. More on the festival's history is on the [Movement Detroit page](/festivals/movement-detroit).

## Selected Releases

Wikipedia and Discogs together point to the main entry points: the Inner City albums for the chart-facing side, the 1997 compilation Faces & Phases for an overview of his most important releases, and the E-Dancer material for the harder side. A recent E-Dancer release with Dantiez is listed on his [Bandcamp page](https://kevinsaunderson.bandcamp.com/).

## FAQ

### Who is Kevin Saunderson?

Kevin Saunderson is an American DJ and producer, one of the three Belleville High School friends known as the Belleville Three who created Detroit techno. He founded KMS Records and co-founded the group Inner City.

### How old is Kevin Saunderson?

Kevin Saunderson was born in 1964, which makes him about 62 (age 62 as of October 2026). Sources disagree on his exact birth date, so only the year is given here.

### Where is Kevin Saunderson from?

He was born in Brooklyn, New York, and grew up in Belleville, Michigan, near Detroit, where he met Juan Atkins and Derrick May at high school.

### What genre does Kevin Saunderson play?

Kevin Saunderson plays techno and house. His Inner City records are house-influenced dance-pop, while his Reese and E-Dancer work is harder techno.

### What is Kevin Saunderson's real name?

His full name is Kevin Maurice Saunderson. He records under his own name and under aliases including Reese, E-Dancer and Kreem.

### What is Kevin Saunderson's most famous track?

"Big Fun" by Inner City, recorded with vocalist Paris Grey, is his best-known release, reaching number 8 in the UK and number 1 on the US dance chart in 1988.

### What is the Reese bassline?

It is a bass sound from Saunderson's 1988 track "Just Want Another Chance", released as Reese, which became a staple of jungle and drum and bass.

### What is KMS Records?

KMS Records is the Detroit label Saunderson formed in 1985, named after his initials.
