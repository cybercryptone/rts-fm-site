Motor City Drum Ensemble is the recording name of Danilo Plessow, a German producer, DJ and label owner whose name pays tribute to Detroit's techno and soul lineage despite his having grown up nowhere near it. Since launching the project in 2007, Plessow has built one of house music's more quietly authoritative catalogues, built on crate-dug samples, warm analogue production and a habit of treating labels as carefully as records.

## From Schwäbisch Gmünd to MCDE

Plessow was born around 1985 and raised in Schwäbisch Gmünd, a small town roughly an hour outside Stuttgart with, by his own account, no clubs and no underground scene to speak of. He started on drums at six, began composing on a computer at around ten or eleven, and worked his way into music largely by sampling his older brother's jazz collection. That combination, a drummer's sense of groove and a crate-digger's ear, has defined his production style ever since. He named the project Motor City Drum Ensemble as a nod to Detroit's outsized influence on the house and techno he grew up on, while also gesturing at Stuttgart's own identity as a car-manufacturing city.

## Raw Cuts and a Run of Labels

MCDE's early singles for Compost and Four Roses in 2007 mixed tech-house with funk and Afrobeat influences, but it was the Raw Cuts series, launched the following year on his own label, that established his reputation: stripped-down, disco and soul-sampling house tracks built for DJs rather than radio. He has since run several imprints alongside his solo output, including MCDE Recordings (a house and techno-focused label operated jointly with Pablo Valentino's FACES Records), Four Roses, and Space Grape, a more recent outlet built around live, band-based recording rather than sample-based production, working with acts including Jéroboam.

## Mixes, Curation and Live at RTS.FM

Plessow's discography beyond his own productions includes a DJ-Kicks mix for !K7 in 2010, Selectors 001 for Dekmantel, an unmixed collection of house and disco rarities, and a contribution to the long-running Fabric mix series. In 2016 he stepped outside the booth to curate a full day of the Nuits Sonores festival in Lyon, a booking that let him bring disco and soul figures like Leroy Burgess onto a bill built mostly around club music. RTS.FM's own archive holds one of MCDE's earliest widely circulated recorded sets, a DJ session filmed at the channel's St. Petersburg studio in October 2009, since then one of the channel's most-watched uploads from that era.

## A Career Built on Sourcing

What separates Plessow from many of his house and disco-edit contemporaries is how much of his catalogue rests on hands-on record digging rather than sample packs or presets, a working method he has talked about at length in interviews over the years as the foundation of both his production and his DJ sets. That approach, paired with a genuinely broad palette that runs from Afrobeat to disco to straight house, has kept MCDE's music in circulation well past the usual shelf life of dance records, with Raw Cuts tracks still turning up in DJ sets more than a decade after their original release.
