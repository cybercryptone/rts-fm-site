Electric Indigo has spent more than three decades doing two things that rarely sit together: playing techno records in clubs and composing electroacoustic music for concert halls and festivals. Behind the name is the Vienna-born Susanne Kirchmayr, who is also the person who started female:pressure, the network that has argued since 1998 for more women in electronic music. This profile traces her path from jazz radio to Hard Wax, her own label, and the archive of sets she recorded for RTS.FM.

## Early Life and Path Into Music

Susanne Kirchmayr was born on 15 December 1965 in Vienna, according to [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Kirchmayr), and the [Austrian Musiklexikon](https://www.musiklexikon.ac.at/ml/musik_K/Kirchmayr_Susanne.xml) gives the same date. The stage name combines her favourite colour with her affinity for electronic music, as both [German](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Indigo) and English Wikipedia put it.

She started DJing in Vienna in 1989, according to her [official site](http://indigo-inc.at/). Wikipedia describes those first years as a mix of jazz, funk and radio DJing before she moved toward Detroit techno and Chicago house. From 1993 to 1996 she lived in Berlin and worked at the record shop Hard Wax. In a [2015 interview with Stray Landings](https://straylandings.co.uk/_content/interviews/2015-07-22-grainfreeze/) she recalled a shop that got busy whenever new records arrived, when scarce physical copies meant waiting your turn behind the VIP customers.

## Sound and Style

The [Austrian Musiklexikon](https://www.musiklexikon.ac.at/ml/musik_K/Kirchmayr_Susanne.xml) describes her club music as techno with syncopated rhythms, strong basslines and sparing melody. Her own site separates two modes: club-oriented work, and immersive electroacoustic compositions that range from experimental ambient to broken beats. She also performs audiovisual concerts that pair her music with abstract, colourful visuals.

In the Stray Landings interview she described a method built around language: spoken recordings in many languages are transformed through granular synthesis into increasingly abstract sound. Pieces she discussed there include "Chiffres", built from recordings of the numbers zero to twenty in more than twenty languages and dialects, and "Morpheme". She said she works in Ableton Live with Max for Live devices, including one built with Robert Henke's help. That research-led side explains why her works have premiered at festivals including Wien Modern, Musikprotokoll, Heroines of Sound and [CTM](/festivals/ctm-festival), according to her site.

## female:pressure

In 1998 Kirchmayr launched female:pressure, an international platform and database for women working in electronic music. In a [2019 interview with Music Export Austria](https://www.musicexport.at/21-years-femalepressure-a-talk-with-electric-indigo/), she is described as co-founder alongside programmer Andrea May, with the network starting from the absence of women on festival line-ups. The network now includes female, queer, non-binary and transgender artists.

Its best-known project is the FACTS survey, which counts the share of women, trans and non-binary artists on festival line-ups. According to that interview, the 2013 and 2017 surveys found a share under 10 percent before 2015 and about 17 percent by 2017, a figure still described as very low. The network's own site lists further FACTS editions for 2020, 2022 and 2024. In 2009 female:pressure received an Honorary Mention at Prix Ars Electronica, according to Kirchmayr's site. Member counts vary by source and by what is being counted: the 2019 article cites about 2,000 database entries from more than 70 countries, while the 2015 Stray Landings piece cites over 12,000 members in 60 countries.

## Labels and Releases

In 2003 she founded her own label, indigo:inc recordings, per Wikipedia. Its catalogue includes the *Six-Trak* EPs and reworks of 2003 and 2004. Earlier she released on Disko B, including "Wolkenkratzer" with Richard Bartz in 1993, which The Gap ranked 13th in a 2020 list of the 100 most important Austrian pop songs, according to German Wikipedia. Other credits in the Wikipedia discographies include collaborations with David Carretta on International DJ Gigolos and Pornflake.

Her album *5 1 1 5 9 3* came out in March 2018 on Imbalance Computer Music, which Music Export Austria treats as her debut album. Her site lists later releases including *Registers* (Ostgut Ton, 2018), *Ferrum* (Editions Mego, 2020), *Volcanite* (Gradient, 2020), *Morpheme* (fals.ch, 2021), *Brittle* (Ventil, 2022), *Mod16* (Illegal Alien, 2023), *One Thing* (30D, 2024) and *Vienna Percussion* (Art Bei Ton, 2025). The site also lists a May 2026 remix of dadub on Opal Tapes.

## Career Highlights

German Wikipedia says she played the main stage of the Detroit Electronic Music Festival in 2002, the event that [later became Movement](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years). She was a TV presenter in Vienna around 2000, and in 2004 she began the collaboration colophony circuit with Mia Zabelka and Dorit Chrysler.

Her recognitions include the Outstanding Artist Award for Music from Austria's culture ministry in 2012, the Frauenring-Preis in 2015 and the Austrian Kunstpreis for Music in 2020, as listed on German Wikipedia and on her site. Her site says she has performed in 46 countries since 1989.
