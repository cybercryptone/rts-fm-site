Burning Man Project's first statement, posted on September 4, 2026, said a Black Rock City participant in his mid-50s had a medical emergency and was pronounced deceased at the onsite emergency center. It gave no name and no cause. Two more deaths were reported within days, and on October 16 the Washoe County Regional Medical Examiner's Office released causes of death for all three men. Those are the confirmed facts, stated plainly, because plain facts are what this story deserves.

The loss arrived during a difficult week for the 2026 event. Gates into Black Rock City closed twice in a matter of days, first for high winds and blowing dust, then again after overnight rain turned the playa's clay lakebed into thick mud. Because the news broke in the same 48 hour window, a lot of headlines ran the death and the weather chaos side by side. That juxtaposition is understandable. It is also, according to everything officials have actually stated, not the same thing as a connection.

## What Happened at Black Rock City on September 3?

According to the Burning Man Project's own [official announcement](https://journal.burningman.org/2026/09/news/official-announcements/participant-passes-away-at-2026-burning-man-event/), a participant "experienced a medical emergency, immediately received lifesaving measures, and was transported to the onsite center for emergency care where he was pronounced deceased." The statement describes him only as a participant in his mid-50s. It does not name him, and it does not speculate about what caused the emergency.

That restraint is deliberate and standard. The statement said the participant's name would not be released until his family had been notified, a practice followed by nearly every public agency handling a sudden death. When the news first broke, no cause of death had been published by the organization, local media, or the Pershing County Sheriff's Office, which holds jurisdiction over the Black Rock Desert (later developments are covered below). Early coverage from [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/burning-man-attendee-dies-festival-grounds-rcna596203) and [TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/09/04/burning-man-attendee-dies-at-event/) confirms the same limited set of details: mid-50s, medical emergency, lifesaving measures administered, transport to onsite care, pronounced dead. Nothing more was confirmed publicly at that point, and out of respect for the family, we did not fill that gap with guesswork.

## What Has Been Confirmed Since the First Statement?

Quite a lot, and it changes the shape of the story. Burning Man Project has added updates to its own [announcement page](https://journal.burningman.org/2026/09/news/official-announcements/participant-passes-away-at-2026-burning-man-event/), which now carries the headline "Participants Pass Away at 2026 Burning Man Event."

- **September 5:** a second participant, Craigh Mann, 60, was found dead by campmates in his camp at about 3 PM.
- **September 10:** the organization named a third participant, Armando Ramirez, 51, who had a serious medical emergency in the early hours of Friday, September 4, received lifesaving measures on site, and died during transport to Reno.
- **The investigation:** Burning Man says it understands the Pershing County Sheriff's Office has announced an investigation into all deaths at the event during Sheriff Jerry Allen's tenure, and that it is not aware of any outcomes. At the time, the organization said the three deaths "all appear to be from natural causes" while deferring to the medical examiner.

The sheriff's office separately identified the man who died at Rampart on the evening of September 3 as Sampson Tshombe, according to [News 4 Reno](https://mynews4.com/news/local/third-death-at-burning-man-confirmed-by-the-pershing-county-sheriffs-office-burning-man-festival-gerlach-black-rock-city-burners-burn-washoe-county-sheriffs-office-sampson-tshombe-craig-mann-burning-man-project-deaths-investigation). Deputies reported no outward signs of foul play in his case or Mann's, and said autopsy and toxicology results would take six to eight weeks.

Those results arrived on October 16. According to [News 4's report on the medical examiner's findings](https://mynews4.com/news/local/medical-examiner-releases-causes-of-death-for-three-men-tied-to-burning-man-festival-pershing-county-pershing-county-sheriffs-office-investigation-drugs-crime-reno-nevada-rampart-medical-center), the Washoe County Regional Medical Examiner's Office ruled as follows:

- **Tshombe Sampson, 62** (the name order differs between sources): hypertensive and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, manner natural.
- **Armando Ramirez, 51:** toxic effects of methamphetamine and MDMA, manner accident.
- **Craig Mann, 60:** combined cocaine and methamphetamine toxicity, manner accident.

One thing is still unresolved. Burning Man's first statement described a participant in his mid-50s, and its page does not say which of the three men that statement concerned. The man who died at Rampart on September 3 was 62 according to the medical examiner's report, so the age does not match cleanly. We are not going to guess how that discrepancy arose. The weather has not featured in any of this: none of the causes the medical examiner listed is weather related, and no official has linked either gate closure to any of the deaths.

## Why Are the Death and the Weather Being Reported Together?

Because they happened in the same week, not because officials linked them. That distinction matters, and it is worth being direct about it for anyone searching for clarity rather than speculation.

### The Wind, the Dust, and the Rain

The same week Black Rock City lost a participant, its gates closed twice. First came a Thursday evening dust storm with high winds strong enough to halt entry. Then, as reporting from [SFist](https://sfist.com/2026/09/04/man-in-his-50s-dies-at-burning-man-gates-stay-closed-due-to-rain-and-mud/) lays out, overnight rain on Friday soaked the alkaline lakebed and turned it into the kind of cement-like mud that has stranded vehicles at past events. Organizers told incoming travelers to hold in Reno and told people already on the playa to avoid driving. Our companion piece, [Inside the Wet Playa Protocol: Why Burning Man 2026's Gates Closed Twice](/blog/wet-playa-protocol-burning-man-2026-gate-closures), goes deeper into how those closures actually worked and why Black Rock City treats a wet playa as a serious operational hazard, separate from any single incident.

### What the Official Statement Actually Says, and Doesn't

A report from [Karmactive](https://www.karmactive.com/burning-man-2026-death-medical-emergency-rain-mud-official-statement/) put the distinction as plainly as it can be put: the death and the weather closures happened at the same time, and that timing does not, on its own, establish that one caused the other. Burning Man's statement calls the event "a medical emergency." It does not mention wind, dust, rain, mud, heat, or any environmental factor at all, and the medical examiner's later findings list cardiovascular disease and drug toxicity rather than anything environmental. No credible reporting has produced an official statement linking the deaths to the weather. Anyone claiming otherwise is speculating past what has actually been confirmed.

## How Common Are Deaths at Black Rock City?

More common than many first-time attendees might assume, and rarely tied to any single dramatic cause. Pershing County Sheriff Jerry Allen told Fox News Digital that his office typically sees about one death per year at the festival, as [reported by KOLO](https://www.kolotv.com/2026/09/08/third-burning-man-attendee-dies-way-reno-area-hospital-sheriff-says/), which makes three deaths in 2026 a heavier year than usual. With attendance regularly running past 70,000 people camped for a week or more in a remote alkaline desert, the event carries the same baseline mortality risk any temporary city of that size would, layered on top of extreme daytime heat, cold nights, dust exposure, physical exertion, and a location roughly two hours from the nearest full-service hospital in Reno.

Causes over the event's history have included cardiac events, accidents involving vehicles and art cars, falls, and, in at least one widely covered case in 2025, a homicide, which was itself the first confirmed homicide in the event's nearly four decade run. None of that history implies a pattern connecting this year's deaths to any single factor. It reflects, instead, what happens when a city's worth of people gathers in a harsh environment: most years bring loss of some kind, and the causes vary.

## What Is Rampart, and How Does Onsite Medical Care Work?

Rampart is Black Rock City's onsite emergency medical facility, and it is a genuinely serious piece of infrastructure, not an improvised first aid tent. It is a Nevada-licensed emergency care facility staffed around the clock by emergency physicians, nurses, pharmacists, and paramedics, with on-site X-ray, a laboratory, and a full pharmacy. Contracted providers such as Royal Ambulance, which has [publicly described its role](https://www.royalambulance.com/burning-man) supporting Black Rock City's Emergency Services Department, report handling well over a thousand patients across an eight day event, most of them treated and discharged on site rather than transported to Reno. Coverage of the event's medical operations from outlets like [EMS1](https://www.ems1.com/mass-gathering/articles/behind-the-scenes-at-burning-man-TrgT5uyyp2IoLCAe/) describes a mass-gathering medicine operation built for exactly the scenario the official statement describes: a medical emergency, immediate lifesaving measures, and transport to definitive onsite care.

That infrastructure existing does not guarantee every outcome. It does mean the response described in Burning Man's own statement, that the participant "immediately received lifesaving measures" before being brought to onsite emergency care, fits a system built and staffed for exactly that purpose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Has the person who died at Burning Man 2026 been identified?

Partly. Burning Man's first statement withheld the name until family notification. Burning Man Project has since named Craigh Mann and Armando Ramirez on its announcement page, and the Pershing County Sheriff's Office identified the man who died at Rampart on September 3 as Sampson Tshombe (reported as Tshombe Sampson in the medical examiner's findings). The organization has not said which of the three its first statement described.

### What was the cause of death?

Burning Man's first statement described the event only as "a medical emergency" and gave no cause. On October 16 the Washoe County Regional Medical Examiner's Office released causes for all three men who died: hypertensive and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (natural) for the man who died at Rampart on September 3, and drug toxicity (accident) for the other two. See the section above for details.

### Was the death caused by the rain, mud, or weather at Burning Man 2026?

There is no official confirmation of that. The first death and the two gate closures for weather happened in the same week, which led to overlapping media coverage, but officials have not stated any causal link between any death and the wind, dust, or rain that affected the event, and the medical examiner's published causes are not weather related.

### How many people die at Burning Man in a typical year?

Pershing County Sheriff Jerry Allen has said his office typically sees about one death per year at the festival. Three men died in connection with the 2026 event, and causes over the years have varied rather than pointing to a single recurring factor.

### What medical resources are available at Black Rock City?

Rampart, the event's Nevada-licensed onsite emergency care center, operates 24 hours a day during the event with emergency physicians, nurses, paramedics, and pharmacists, along with on-site imaging and a pharmacy, so that most patients can be treated without transport to hospitals in Reno.

Our thoughts are with the families of all three men and everyone in the Black Rock City community affected by these losses.
