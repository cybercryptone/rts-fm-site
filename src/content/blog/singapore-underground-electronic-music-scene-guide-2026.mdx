Singapore's underground electronic music scene in 2026 is small, dense, expensive, tightly regulated and regionally connected. Its centre of gravity is a handful of rooms in and around the central business district: the venues run by the nightlife company [Thugshop](https://www.thugshopsg.com/venue), the music and arts space [RASA](https://ra.co/clubs/252093) at Raffles Place, the label-and-party network around Darker Than Wax and the new Lifted Trax record shop, and a freshly renovated, much more commercial Zouk. The year's big shifts are all dated: Headquarters, the Boat Quay techno club, closed on 30 May 2026 after ten years; Zouk reopened on about 17 June 2026; Lifted Trax opened on 5 August 2026; the Boat Quay and Clarke Quay 4am liquor-hours pilot that began on 18 August 2025 was made permanent from 18 August 2026; and the Work Permit (Performing Artiste) scheme closed to new applications on 1 June 2026, though the government's own announcement does not mention DJs.

This is a desk-research field guide by the RTS.FM editorial desk, not a travelogue. We have not visited these venues for this piece, and nothing here is first-hand. We read the scene's press, promoter and venue pages, label Bandcamps, official regulators and Resident Advisor's Singapore listings, then wrote down what the sources say, where they disagree, and what we could not confirm. RTS.FM is an artist-run, non-commercial internet radio station and label that has broadcast underground house and techno since 2006, so we also asked a narrower question near the end: which Singapore projects share that ethos? The method, the limits and every discrepancy we resolved are listed in the final section.

## How did Singapore's club scene get here?

Singapore's electronic club culture grew around one venue, Zouk, which opened in March 1991, and the independent scene that exists now was largely built as a reaction to the commercial nightlife that followed. That is the consensus of the sources we read, though several of them are insiders and promoters, and we flag the origin claims as such.

[Wikipedia's Zouk entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zouk_(club)) records that founder Lincoln Cheng opened the club in three restored 1919 warehouses on Jiak Kim Street, modelled on his Mediterranean and Ibiza travels. [FZINE](https://www.fzine.com/culture/read-one-writers-fondest-memories-of-zouk-club-in-singapore) calls it the birthplace of Singapore's EDM scene and the first club to play house, which is an editorial claim, not independent research. Darker Than Wax co-founder Dean Chew puts it plainly in a [Club77 interview](https://www.club77.com.au/news/from-singapore-to-new-york-via-perth-dean-chew-on-spreading-the-darker-than-wax-gospel-and-southeast-asias-vibrant-club-scene): "Zouk was the epitome of club culture in Singapore." Electronic Beats, in a [2014 archive piece](https://www.electronicbeats.net/singapores-club-scene), says the club scene began when Zouk became its anchor venue.

The cleanest way to hold the history is a dated timeline. Where sources disagree, the row says so.

Two texture notes sit behind that table. First, the 1990s underground was real but tiny: [Electronic Beats says](https://www.electronicbeats.net/how-berlins-diy-club-culture-inspired-a-rave-scene-in-singapore/) it was "a small underground electronic music community", particularly drum and bass, and a [Mixmag Asia essay](https://mixmag.asia/feature/techno-is-not-what-you-think-it-means-to-most-singaporeans) argues that "techno" meant Eurodance and the local "manyao" remix genre to most Singaporeans for years. Second, the 2000s tilted commercial. Promoter Kavan Spruyt told [Electronic Beats in 2014](https://www.electronicbeats.net/singapores-club-scene) that he linked a shift toward commercial music to a drug crackdown around 2004 and 2005, and a [2018 Electronic Beats feature](https://www.electronicbeats.net/how-berlins-diy-club-culture-inspired-a-rave-scene-in-singapore/) describes the later independent wave, including Darker Than Wax, Ice Cream Sundays, Ujikaji and Headquarters, as a deliberate reaction to bottle-service culture, borrowing Berlin's DIY model. For the Berlin scene it borrowed from, see our [Berlin clubs guide](/blog/best-berlin-clubs-2026-guide).

After COVID the revival was real but fragile. [Time Out reported in March 2023](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/nightlife/stayin-alive-singapores-nightlife-scene-is-struggling-to-survive) that promoters had more than doubled since 2019 to about 20, with six or seven house and techno parties on some weekends, while Canvas, Nineteen80 and Rails were gone.

### Thugshop: the commercial operator that sets the tempo

Thugshop is a nightlife and hospitality company, not a collective. Its own site calls it a self-owned business and lists its venues as Higher Ground (a Boat Quay rooftop bar and diner), Tuff Club, MDLR and Exit Reality. We include it because, in practice, it has become the main stage for techno and house in the city, and because its pricing sometimes behaves like a community project. Its tenth-anniversary party on 28 June 2025 charged S$10 on the door and S$10 for drinks, [per Life In Arpeggio](https://www.lifeinarpeggio.com/blog/thugshop-10-year-anniversary-glass-dome-techno-house-music-parties-singapore).

[Tuff Club](https://www.thugshopsg.com/venue) began as a three-month pop-up in March 2018, according to [RA club news](https://ra.co/clubs/149322), and was later made permanent. It sits on the 19th floor of an office building and is fitted with a Funktion-One system. MDLR started as "Thugshop's new pop-up space at 62 Cecil Street". RA had listed 51 events there in 2026 by mid-October, and it hosts promoters including Kings of Bass, FOMOHOMO and the GIVE. nights. Exit Reality, on an upper floor of a Boat Quay shophouse at 66B, is billed by Thugshop as a boutique electronic sanctuary. It is a 100-capacity room with an unlit dance floor, and the Thugshop Telegram schedule shows nightly sets from 10.30pm to 3am with local residents. One point of care: HQ was at 66 Boat Quay, so Exit Reality sits at or near the old HQ site, but no source we read says one replaced the other, and we do not claim it.

Thugshop's recurring series include Never Tooo Loud (techno; the listed autumn dates are in the calendar below) and GIVE. x Thugshop, which marked its first anniversary with STORM on 22 August 2026. Its line-ups run from the in-house residents (sho&tell, Joshua Dillon, VAIBS, Vinnie Stew, Meliah) to Rebekah, Matrixxman and Natascha Polke in September 2026. Grazia describes Thugshop as formerly The Council, but we could not find a document setting out the corporate relationship between the two, so we treat it as lineage rather than a legal fact.

### RASA: the money and the ambition

[RASA](https://www.fzine.com/culture/rasa-singapore-nightlife) opened in January 2025 in Republic Plaza, with Taiwan's Organik Festival on its first weekend. It has two rooms, The Hall (a lounge) and The Floor (a dance room), across about 6,100 square feet, designed by Berlin's Studio Karhard, whom the coverage identifies as the Berghain designers, with a custom TPi sound system. For why sound systems now headline, see [our piece on hi-fi systems as festival attractions](/blog/hifi-sound-systems-festival-lineups-despacio-sunflower). The founder is Kavan Spruyt, a veteran of The Vault and Kyo who also runs the Midnight Shift label. Cindy Tan leads marketing and music, Efy Ramdan is the designer, and Wild Pearl's Chris Shearmon curated a Wednesday series.

It is a private venture and not a non-commercial one; other promoters, including Collective Minds Asia, rent the room. FZINE's December 2024 visit reported that club nights are "not its main purpose". Its 2026 programme, by RA's past-events pages and Mixmag Asia, includes DJ Stingray 313 (11 July), Rrose (18 July), JakoJako (24 July), Denis Sulta (1 August), Perc (7 August), Henning Baer and Phil Berg (9 August), Ken Ishii (14 August), Tommy Four Seven (15 August, [S$35](https://mixmag.asia/read/singapore-rasa-presents-tommy-four-seven-events)), Goldie b2b dBridge (3 October), Lil' Louis (9 October), Patrice Baumel (30 October), Roman Flugel (6 November) and Lee Burridge (7 November). Residencies and co-promoters include Sivilian Affairs, Darker Than Wax, Progressions and The Deep End, EN, CIEL, Mismatch and Blackout Agency. A [Mixmag Asia opening report](https://mixmag.net/read/singapore-rasa-club-music-arts-venue-news) is the best primary account of the launch.

### Zouk: reopened, repositioned, not underground

Zouk closed in phases from January 2026 for a multi-million-dollar renovation. [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/nightlife-venues-zouk-capital-and-phuture-to-shut-for-a-multi-million-dollar-renovation-till-mid-2026-010526) reported Capital closing on 17 January, with Phuture and Mainroom following at the end of March. It reopened with an event on about 17 June 2026, "The Next Chapter", with Iman Fandi live, according to Elle; the [Honeycombers](https://thehoneycombers.com/singapore/zouk-singapore-renovation-reopens-june-2026/) reopening piece also dates it to June. The refit brought modular acoustic walls, combined capacity of up to 3,500, 12-metre LED walls and a VIP sofa section. [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/zouk-singapore-kicks-off-multi-million-dollar-transformation-ahead-of-35th-anniversary) reports that Zouk is repositioning as a "multi-dimensional lifestyle platform" for brand activations. Sources disagree on the lease: Honeycombers says an extension with CapitaLand to 2031, while Marketing-Interactive says the lease expired in May 2026 with a renewal starting in June.

ZoukOut, its Sentosa beach festival, has a patchier record. It paused in 2019 (announced 28 August 2019, with attendance reportedly falling from about 40,000 in 2017 to about 20,000 in 2018, [per the Straits Times via Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/zouk-group-presses-pause-on-zoukout-this-year)), skipped 2020 and 2021, returned on 3 and 4 December 2022 and was promoted in 2023 with Alesso and FISHER. We found no source on any edition from 2024 to 2026. Our verdict: Zouk matters historically and as a signal of where money is going, not as an underground address.

### Headquarters and The Council: the club that closed on its own terms

Headquarters opened in May 2016 on Level 2 of 66 Boat Quay, a basement-style room with a caged DJ booth and red lasers. It was the flagship of The Council, which [Bandwagon reports](https://www.bandwagon.asia/articles/the-council) was formed in January 2016 by Clement Chin and Eileen Chan, found the shophouse in April, and faced venue, permit and licensing hurdles, with police "frequently" shutting early parties. Mixmag Asia's 2020 guide describes The Council as "a multi-layered institution". [RA's club page](https://ra.co/clubs/119385) lists Rebekah, TIN MAN, DJ Boring, Rampue, Mendy Indigo and Sunju Hargun among past guests.

The closure was announced on Instagram on 8 May 2026. [FZINE](https://www.fzine.com/culture/headquarters-club-singapore-closing) and [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/headquarters-the-red-lit-boat-quay-club-is-closing-after-10-years-050926) both say no reason was given in the announcement. A later [Mixmag Asia feature](https://mixmag.asia/feature/headquarters-hq-singapore-closure-10-years-thugshop) reports that it was a deliberate choice rather than a rent or licensing problem, that the team turned down offers to buy the club and brand, and that the walls would be archived digitally, with a Tanjong Pagar Distripark pop-up to follow. The Thugshop team told Mixmag Asia that "Ten years is a good time. It's a good story." The final night, "This Was The Place: 10 Years of HQ", ran for ten hours with ten b2b sets from the residents, from 6pm at S$35 online and S$40 on the door. It is one of the few Singapore closures that came with a plan. Compare it with the pattern in our [Europe club closures tracker](/blog/europe-club-closures-tracker).

### Collectives and crews

- **Sivilian Affairs** is artist-run, curated by DJ Sivanesh Pillai and Lilian Hautemulle, with house, disco and techno at an intimate 100 to 200 capacity according to [VICE in 2022](https://www.vice.com/en/article/pandemic-singapore-underground-techno-party-night-life/). Its Celestial Seasonings nights run at RASA (25 October 2025, 27 March and 25 September 2026), and Chocolate City follows there on 16 October 2026. Sivanesh told [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/nightlife/stayin-alive-singapores-nightlife-scene-is-struggling-to-survive) in 2023 that the first thing that comes to mind is "the lack of venues."
- **Ice Cream Sundays** is an artist-run daytime collective (Daniel O'Connor, Jake "Toppings" Camacho, Meltem Acik, with residents Muto and Bongomann). The idea dates from 2015 and parties from 2016, and 2026 is its tenth-anniversary run, with events at Haw Par Villa, Gillman Barracks, the National Gallery and Tanjong Pagar Distripark. It co-organised **Rainbow Disco Club Singapore** on 3 and 4 October 2026, the Japanese festival's first Singapore edition, in three sessions: a day session in the Distripark carpark, a night session at Cargo39 (10pm to 3am) and a recovery session at RASA. Kuniyuki played live alongside Antal, Daryl Knows, Muto, DJ Nobu, Xiaolin, Kin Leonn and Kindergarchy, with tickets from S$45 to S$138, according to [Mixmag Asia](https://mixmag.asia/read/singapore-rainbow-disco-club-singapore-events). FZINE gave a slightly different venue split across the two days, so treat the detail as approximate. The collective told [Mixmag Asia](https://mixmag.asia/read/rainbow-disco-club-singapore-debut-ice-cream-sundays-day-to-night-events): "We have some incredible DJs and electronic musicians in Singapore."
- **Kings of Bass** is a bass-music promoter (drum and bass, jungle, UKG) that has run since 2020, founded by people active in Australian drum and bass since 1999. It is small and commercial and works closely with Thugshop; its [RA page](https://ra.co/promoters/109531) lists General Levy (3 October) and Messie (23 October) at MDLR.
- **FOMOHOMO** is a queer nightlife collective working with international and local DJs and drag; its [RA page](https://ra.co/promoters/119743) lists HE.SHE.THEY with Bimini at MDLR on 19 June 2026, RUNWAY at Host on 10 and 15 October and HAIR RAISER with Gottmik on 24 October.
- **Degenerave** (since 2024) curates hard, dark techno, industrial and schranz, including EVE at Tuff Club on 6 November 2026, per its [RA page](https://ra.co/promoters/146708).
- **Manifest SG** (since the start of 2019) books local techno and house talent first. Its [RA page](https://ra.co/promoters/118560) lists a seventh-anniversary pool party on 7 March 2026 and past nights at Behind The Green Door and RASA.

### Wild Pearl, The Glass Hut and other uncertain rooms

Two of the scene's most interesting small rooms may or may not be operating, and we say so rather than guess. **Wild Pearl** was a tiny music studio and listening room on Pearl's Hill Terrace founded by Chris Shearmon and Andy Lines, BYOB and not a bar, with a weekly Wednesday community night; [Life In Arpeggio](https://www.lifeinarpeggio.com/blog/wild-pearl-interview-local-clubs-music-studios-djs-radio-shows-singapore) put capacity at just under 40 in December 2022 and [RA](https://ra.co/clubs/221487) says 80. Andy Lines told that outlet: "There's no ego about the people that are in the room." RA shows no Wild Pearl events after December 2023, and the unit number differs by source, so its current status is unverified. Shearmon curated a Wednesday series at RASA in 2025. **The Glass Hut**, founded in 2020 by artist Dione Keh and guitarist Cruise Chen in the same building complex, paused in August 2022 after an ultimatum from building management. Keh told [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/pandemic-singapore-underground-techno-party-night-life/): "Our goal was never to create a party space per se." We found no 2026 listing. Cherry Discotheque, Kilo Lounge and White Label Records also have status question marks; see the research section.

The wider commercial tier exists too. Pasir Panjang Power Station hosts ARTBAT (17 October), Intercell with Mall Grab (21 November) and Dom Dolla (27 November), promoted by Collective Minds Asia, while Marina Bay Sands venues ran Anyma, Claptone and Carl Cox during F1 race week in October, [per FZINE](https://www.fzine.com/culture/best-singapore-nightlife-parties). Drip Singapore, a large Orchard Road basement club that opened in July 2022, [announced in February 2026](https://mustsharenews.com/singapore-club-culture-youths/) that it was closing its Singapore outlet and moving to Kuala Lumpur; one online commentator blamed rents and alcohol rules, but that is opinion, not an operator statement.

## Which labels, shops and radio shows matter?

The label scene is small, artist-run and Bandcamp-led. The shop scene is just getting a new anchor. Radio, in the sense of a dedicated underground station, is the gap.

### Labels

**Darker Than Wax** was founded in 2011 by Dean Chew and Kevin Guoh, known as Kaye, to champion jazz-inflected house, broken beat and leftfield music from Southeast Asia. 2026 is its fifteenth anniversary. [Mixmag Asia's label spotlight](https://mixmag.asia/feature/label-spotlight-darker-than-wax-singapore) records the aim of four or more vinyl releases a year, plus radio shows and A&R, and Chew told Mixmag Asia that "the scene here has quietly been brewing since the 90s". The label's New York arm is run by Marco Weibel, and the [Bandcamp catalogue](https://darkerthanwax.bandcamp.com/music) includes vinyl LPs such as KEI-LI. Its anniversary programme includes the DTW 15 Anniversary Space nights at RASA (17 July with Arga from Seoul; 18 September with Querico from Tokyo) and BREW at Ida's on 24 October. The co-owned listening bar Offtrack, at 34 North Canal Road, opened in early 2022 with two restored Klipsch La Scala speakers, the model associated with David Mancuso's Loft parties, according to [Mixmag Asia](https://mixmag.asia/read/offtrack-opens-singapore-klipsch-feed-your-soul-local); its current status is unverified.

**Midnight Shift** was founded by Kavan Spruyt and Debbie Chia. Its output on [Bandcamp](https://mnshift.bandcamp.com) includes the Mr. G "Temasek" EP (6 June 2016), Deadbeat x Om Unit, Prequel Tapes and dBridge, the Rivet LP, Umwelt, Nothus and Xhin. Two facts are disputed across sources: the founding year (2009 per Bandcamp Daily, 2012 per Life In Arpeggio) and the base (the Bandcamp page says Singapore, while Life In Arpeggio and Bandcamp Daily describe it as primarily Berlin-based, and RA calls it an electronic label and events organisation from Singapore). The [Life In Arpeggio interview](https://lifeinarpeggio.com/blog/midnight-shift-interview-local-techno-music-record-labels-singapore) says vinyl was pressed after the Berlin move, and Spruyt's own recipe is modest: "persistence and taking the time to keep on releasing".

**Integrity Records** is Eddie Niguel's house and techno label, launched in 2016 with the Back2Basics EP; release 13 was Miles Ellis's The Pier EP on 13 March 2020. It is [Bandcamp-led](https://integrityrecords.bandcamp.com/artists), and Niguel heads the mastering studio TBR Mixmaster, credited by Life In Arpeggio with mastering tracks for Amelie Lens and KiNK. **Ujikaji Records**, founded in 2010, is an independent label, event organiser and mail-order store focused on experimental work from Southeast Asia, with LPs, CDs and cassettes; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujikaji) notes that the name means "experiment" in Malay. **Evening Chants**, founded in 2018 by Nigel Lopez and Jasmine Ho, focuses on experimental, ambient and new age music. Its [CLOT Magazine interview](https://clotmag.com/news/insight-evening-chants-record-label-building-on-beautifully-strange-music) curator Nigel Lopez describes "a very small yet tight-knit community", and, importantly, says there are no cassette duplication services or vinyl pressing plants in Singapore or the region. Bandcamp Daily adds that vinyl test pressings take longer to approve and shipping costs more from Singapore. That is the same bottleneck we describe in our reporting on [pressing plant capacity](/blog/vinyl-pressing-plant-capacity-crisis) and on [vinyl's 2025 record year](/blog/vinyl-sales-record-2025), made more acute by distance.

Also in the file: **Syndicate**, an audiovisual collective and label (Safuan Johari, Jonathan Kiat) for experimental beats, ambient and glitch, whose LP Recluse is listed on vinyl ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/electronic-labels-singapore-feature)); we could not open Syndicate's own Bandcamp page, so current status is unverified. **SoulMatters Recordings** (Gerald Ang, Shawnn Lai, Amjad Shah) released the techno and tech house Silence EP in 2017. **Super Enjoy** is run by Dexter Colt. For how labels like these fare over time, see [our study of independent label survival](/blog/independent-label-survival-study).

### Record shops

**Lifted Trax** is the news. It opened on 5 August 2026 at 25 Lorong Telok, #03-01, on the Boat Quay and Clarke Quay side. [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/darker-than-waxs-dexter-colt-just-opened-a-record-shop-on-lorong-telok-081226) reports hours of Wednesday and Thursday 12 to 7, Friday and Saturday 12 to 8, and Sunday 12 to 6; check before visiting. Owner Dexter Colt, the Canadian-born Jordan Glick who has lived in Singapore for about 20 years, also runs Darker Than Wax and Super Enjoy. [Bandwagon](https://www.bandwagon.asia/articles/lifted-trax-is-singapore-s-new-record-store-for-deep-digging-discovery) describes second-hand dance and electronic stock, ambient, jazz, soul and disco, a wall for Asian artists and labels, collection buying, shipping across Asia, zines and turntable accessories, and plans for in-store events, club nights and eventually its own labels. Colt calls record shops among the most important spaces for community building in local scenes.

Older options, with caveats: **Red Point Record Warehouse** (Kapo Factory Building, 80 Playfair Road) held about 50,000 records and a S$5 second-hand dance section in a 2017 [Mixmag Asia crate-digging feature](https://mixmag.asia/feature/9-djs-spill-secret-spots-to-go-crate-digging-asia), and opens at weekends, though hours may have changed since. **The Analog Vault** was praised by Bandwagon in 2020 for the best catalogue of hip-hop and electronic titles in the country, and **Roxy Records and Trading** at Excelsior Shopping Centre for cheap 1990s house 12-inches, both per [Bandwagon's 2020 vinyl guide](https://www.bandwagon.asia/articles/bandwagon-s-guide-to-vinyl-shopping-in-singapore); their current status needs checking. **White Label Records**, the Ann Siang Road shop-bar-community space that opened in September 2018, is reported closed by two sources we opened, though RA still lists it; we do not list it as somewhere to buy. For vinyl-led bars, Time Out's guide of 7 July 2026 lists [Appetite, Slow Boat, RPM, Little Island Brewing (Waxy Wednesday) and Choice Cuts Pizza and Records](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/music/the-best-listening-bars-in-singapore), and notes that Vertigo 26 and Simply Analog by Tin Box have closed.

### Radio: the honest answer is no

We found no dedicated Singapore underground radio station operating in 2026. What exists is a trail of ended and part-time projects. Lush 99.5 stopped transmitting on 31 August 2017. Sonar Radio ran online from 5 May 2011 to 2013. Panoptkn, a weekly bedroom-studio livestream styled as a local Boiler Room, began in 2011. Singapore Community Radio launched in 2017, relaunched on 15 September 2020 with a new team and shows on Twitch and Mixcloud Live, and we could not verify its status after 2020. Wild Pearl ran radio-style Wednesday sets at RASA, and Darker Than Wax has run weekly and monthly shows. Sharon Seet of The Analog Vault told [FZINE](https://www.fzine.com/culture/singapore-music-scene-creatives-chantal-tan-preeti-nair-sharon-seet) of "a lack of radio stations playing more eclectic types of genres". We also found no Singapore-resident NTS show. That gap is one reason the scene might care about an independent stream.

## What rules and costs shape a night out in Singapore?

Three layers of permission, early closing hours, a heavy drug-law regime and high costs define what an underground night can be. This section is reporting, not legal advice. Check the current text of any regulation before acting on it.

### Licences: planning, liquor and entertainment

Per a Ministry of Home Affairs reply to Parliament dated 9 July 2018, nightlife operators need URA planning permission (a change of use), plus a liquor licence and a Public Entertainment licence from the Singapore Police Force. Clubs, bars and discos fall under the SPF's Category 1 conditions. MHA said the venues are regulated under the Public Entertainments Act and that it consulted the Singapore Nightlife Business Association when reviewing the Act. [The reply is here](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-review-of-licensing-regime-to-ensure-singapore-continues-to-be-an-attractive-location-for-work-and-play-by-mr-k-shanmugam-minister-for-home-affairs-and-minister-for-law/). We did not verify licence fees, hour tiers or the URA planning classes for nightclubs, and a March 2025 parliamentary answer that surfaced in a search summary could not be opened.

### Age, noise and dancing

The Category 1 conditions we read (dated 1 February 2020, and possibly revised since) say the licensee must not sell liquor to, or allow liquor consumption by, anyone under 18, and that patrons under 16 may not be in premises with dancing outside restaurant hours. Dancing by patrons needs Licensing Officer approval. Entertainment must stop at the licensed hours. Sound must not exceed 65 dBA at the boundary or 55 dBA at the nearest dwelling after 10.30pm with windows closed, and a noise-metering regime with a Class 1 meter, CCTV and kept recordings can be imposed. Individual clubs often set a 21+ door policy, but that is house policy, not the legal minimum.

Here is an inference of ours, not a finding of any source: with noise limits that tight, it is unsurprising that RASA sits in Republic Plaza, Tuff Club on the 19th floor of an office building and MDLR at 62 Cecil Street, and that The Glass Hut struggled in a mixed-use factory building. VICE's 2022 report, for its part, said current legislation prevents events in proper warehouse spaces.

### Hours: the Boat Quay and Clarke Quay pilot

Clarke Quay liquor hours were restricted from 2013 after violent crime (3am on weekdays, 4am on weekends and eves of holidays), per the MHA's 2018 reply. On 7 August 2025, the government announced a one-year pilot from 18 August 2025 allowing liquor hours to be extended to 4am on Thursday and Friday nights in the Boat Quay Historic District, the Upper Circular Road area and the Clarke Quay Conservation Area, and allowing new nightlife uses in parts of that district, [per the MHA release](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/joint-press-release-on-revitalisation-measures-at-boat-quay-historic-district-upper-circular-road-area-and-clarke-quay-conservation-area/). Yahoo reports this ended a 16-year moratorium on new bars and clubs in Boat Quay. The results, from the MHA's [August 2026 formalisation notice](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/formalisation-of-revitalisation-measures-at-boat-quay-historic-district-upper-circular-road-area-and-clarke-quay-conservation-area/): URA approved 37 new nightlife uses, SPF approved 92 extensions, law and order stayed stable, and the measures became permanent from 18 August 2026. Extra security is paid for by Singapore River One, at about S$20,000 a month for five officers. One discrepancy: Time Out wrote Friday and Saturday in August 2025, while the MHA and Yahoo say Thursday and Friday. We follow the MHA, which is the primary source.

What this does and does not mean for the underground: Boat Quay hosts Exit Reality, but the CBD towers housing RASA, Tuff Club and MDLR are not in the named precincts. Elsewhere, licensed hours are set per venue under the licence. We found no source saying whether Exit Reality uses the extension. Michelle Koh of Singapore River One described the aim as "making Circular Road social again", and Singapore Nightlife Business Association president Danny Loong called the formalisation a good step while making proposals on late-night transport costs, per [Yahoo News Singapore](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/loosened-nightlife-rules-boat-quay-093914689.html). Separately, the public-drinking ban from 10.30pm to 7am, under the Liquor Control (Supply and Consumption) Act, with stricter zones in Little India and Geylang, makes the street after midnight a dry one, while drinking inside licensed premises is unaffected.

### Foreign DJs: the Performing Artiste permit closes

The Ministry of Manpower stopped accepting new applications under the Work Permit (Performing Artiste) scheme from 1 June 2026, after enforcement found syndicates using non-operating outlets to hire performers. Existing permits run to expiry, with alternatives including entertainment service providers or regular work passes. The Work Pass Exempt route for short performances excludes bars, discos, lounges, nightclubs, pubs and hotels with a Category 1 PE licence. MOM's text does not mention DJs, and we did not verify how visiting club DJs are handled in practice. International bookings at RASA and the Thugshop rooms continue through autumn 2026, but we cannot say by which route.

### Drug law and enforcement

The Central Narcotics Bureau states that consuming a controlled drug carries a minimum of one year and a maximum of ten years in prison and/or a fine of up to S$20,000, and that citizens and permanent residents are liable for consumption overseas, per [CNB's Misuse of Drugs Act page](https://www.cnb.gov.sg/drug-information/misuse-of-drugs-act). That page gave no trafficking tiers, and we did not confirm trafficking penalty thresholds from an official page, so we publish none.

On enforcement, CNB-led operations check patrons inside nightspots with saliva test kits, in use since January 2023. On 18 and 19 June 2026, CNB with the SPF and the Health Sciences Authority checked about 130 people at five nightlife establishments, saliva-tested 61, and arrested one 26-year-old foreign woman for suspected drug abuse, as part of a week in which 76 people were arrested island-wide, according to [CNB](https://www.cnb.gov.sg/mediaroom/news/76-arrested-in-islandwide-anti-drug-operation/). An earlier operation on 31 March and 1 April 2023 at two venues around Orchard Road and Jalan Sultan checked about 240. We found no source for routine urine tests at club doors. A summary we could not verify says urine tests follow after an arrest at CNB, which is a different thing. For a contrast with a policy that tests substances rather than people, see [our NSW pill-testing report](/blog/nsw-festival-drug-checking-trial); nothing comparable exists in what we read for Singapore.

The licence conditions also oblige operators to take steps against sale, consumption or trafficking of controlled drugs on premises and to report offences to police. The scene's own view is wry. Horizon99, the Berlin-inspired collective [profiled by Electronic Beats](https://www.electronicbeats.net/how-berlins-diy-club-culture-inspired-a-rave-scene-in-singapore/), said of its nights that "We do not have the drugs", while Kyo's owner Godwin Pereira told [the same site in 2014](https://www.electronicbeats.net/singapores-club-scene) that performing without drugs is "a bigger challenge".

### What it costs, and who is squeezed

The numbers are sector-level because underground-scale rents are not documented. NTU reported that operating revenue for pubs, nightclubs, discos, dance clubs and karaoke lounges fell from a S$674.7m peak in 2015 to S$284.7m in 2022, citing alcohol costs, early closing and after-midnight transport. NTU also notes that public transport ends by midnight and taxi fares double after midnight. The Straits Times, read via [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/05/02/039troubling-times039-nightlife-business-operators-say-that-a-growing-quiet-is-falling-over-singapores-once-thriving-hot-spots), added rent, manpower and alcohol duties, 3,047 F&B closures in 2024, a typical city cocktail at S$28, Clarke Quay bars down to under half of the 30 to 40 once there, and Keong Saik Road venues that struggle to win late-night licences after switching to restaurant licences during the pandemic. Bar owner Naz Arjuna put it bluntly: "We're on the last legs of a dying lifestyle." We did not find a rent figure for an underground club, and we dropped two unverified search-snippet figures (a Boat Quay shophouse listing and venue-hire minimum spends) rather than print them.

For comparison with another expensive city, see [why the UK keeps losing its nightclubs](/blog/uk-nightclub-closures-licensing-reform), where licensing, rates and rents are the same kind of three-way squeeze, and [our data on underground DJ economics](/blog/underground-dj-economics-data). For an analysis of how licensing can fall unevenly on particular communities, our report on [London's REMEL study](/blog/remel-black-music-events-london-licensing-study) is the nearest parallel. We found no equivalent Singapore study.

## What makes Singapore's scene distinct?

Five things stand out in the reporting: size, regulation, a promoter culture split between commercial and DIY, queer scenes that organise cautiously, and a regional rather than local horizon.

**Size and venue scarcity.** An island city-state means few rooms and a shared talent pool. Chia said in 2014 that the same photographers and designers get reused, and Kevin Ho wrote in [Mixmag Asia](https://mixmag.asia/feature/techno-is-not-what-you-think-it-means-to-most-singaporeans) in 2021 that Singapore is "not exactly a mecca of dance music culture". Sivanesh's line about the lack of venues is borne out by the closure list: Kyo went on a break in June 2017 to focus on its Kuala Lumpur branch, Kilo Lounge's Tanjong Pagar site closed on 31 December 2019 citing rising operating costs and a business that was "not financially sustainable", and Canvas announced its closure on 9 February 2021 after seven years.

**Regulation as aesthetic.** Spruyt and Chia told [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/electronic-labels-singapore-feature) in 2018 that Singapore's tough stance on local laws had stunted creativity, while Syndicate's Kiat said "I think the greatest constraint is in our minds." Both can be true, and the early closing hours, sound limits and heavy drug penalties above give them their basis. The Council's founders described police closing early parties. Bussy Temple kept early raves off social media. One sourced consequence is a scene that organises carefully and, on the evidence of the CBD tower rooms, indoors.

**Bottle service versus DIY.** HQ's founders wanted to shift perception away from bottle-service clubbing. Eileen Chan told [Electronic Beats](https://www.electronicbeats.net/how-berlins-diy-club-culture-inspired-a-rave-scene-in-singapore/) that "Every community should develop and define their own club culture." RA club news records a tribute night for her at Tuff Club on 5 August 2023. Zouk's present repositioning for brand activations is the opposite pole, which is why we cover it as context rather than as part of the underground.

**Queer scenes after Section 377A.** Parliament passed the repeal on 29 November 2022, in force from January 2023 per Wikipedia, alongside a constitutional amendment leaving the definition of marriage to Parliament. FZINE describes today's queer party scene as "post-Covid, post-377A", and the evidence in our sources is of growth: FOMOHOMO's MDLR and Host nights, 66 Chambers' self-description as welcoming to LGBTQ+ communities, and the Agni parties. Mixmag Asia notes that queer events in Singapore and Malaysia still face legal challenges, including government approvals. We found no source measuring the effect of repeal on club venues, so we do not claim one.

**A regional horizon.** Dean Chew told [Club77](https://www.club77.com.au/news/from-singapore-to-new-york-via-perth-dean-chew-on-spreading-the-darker-than-wax-gospel-and-southeast-asias-vibrant-club-scene) that exchanges between Bangkok, Jakarta, Vietnam and Australia have grown since COVID and described Bali as booming. Muttiah Ashnim's Into The Deep ran events across six Southeast Asian countries, and Bussy Temple has played Vietnam's Arcan. Collective Minds Asia stages ticketed shows across Singapore, Hong Kong, Bangkok, Jakarta and Kuala Lumpur, and the Sunda Festival's Philippines takeover on 29 March 2025 shows regional flows going both ways. Two things we could not source: Kuala Lumpur as a specific touring hub for Singapore DJs, and the share of audiences who are expats or tourists. Many of the people profiled here are international in biography, such as the Canadian-born Dexter Colt, the Yokohama-born Aya and the Bangalore-born Aman Anand, which suggests an international scene, but not a number.

Government music policy is thin. The National Arts Council's Presentation and Participation (General) Grant now includes Music Recording Projects, and from 16 April 2026 the cap is S$100,000 per applicant per financial year, open to individuals, organisations and collectives, per the [NAC page](https://www.nac.gov.sg/support/funding-and-schemes/presentation-and-participation-grant). We found no electronic-specific scheme. Separately, on 8 May 2026 the Singapore Tourism Board signed a three-year agreement with Universal Music Singapore to use music for destination marketing. How that reaches the underground, if at all, is unreported.

## What we make of it

This section is RTS.FM's opinion. It states no new facts, and each point ends with the strongest counter-argument against it.

1. **Singapore's best underground assets are people and objects, not buildings.** Rooms close, relocate and change hands, while labels, record shops and residents persist, and the scene's recent history is a list of rooms that did not last. The most durable-looking things in this guide are Darker Than Wax's catalogue, Midnight Shift's releases and a record shop that has just opened. *The counter-argument: labels and shops also depend on rooms to build audiences, and without venues the community may thin out.*
2. **Thugshop is the pragmatic centre, and its commercial status matters less than its pricing and residents.** We would rather a commercial operator runs a S$10 anniversary and gives locals the closing night than that the scene waits for a purely non-commercial model that nobody can afford. *The counter-argument: a single operator concentrating the best small rooms creates a dependency, and community-priced nights are a choice that can be reversed.*
3. **Regulation shapes the sound as much as taste does.** Tight noise limits, short hours and heavy penalties produce small, careful, indoor, well-organised nights, and the result can be disciplined and intimate. *The counter-argument: constraint is not a creative advantage, and the artists we quoted say as much when they talk about rules and lost venues.*
4. **The gap in radio is an opening, but not an obvious one for RTS.FM to fill.** A scene that has had no dedicated underground station since Lush 99.5 has an audience for one, and an artist-run stream could serve it without competing with anyone. *The counter-argument: the history is of short-lived stations, which suggests the problem is economics rather than awareness, and we should not assume demand we have not measured.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Singapore's techno scene like?

Small and dense. A handful of venues share a limited talent pool (Time Out counted about 20 promoters in 2023), with techno concentrated in Thugshop's rooms, RASA and one-off nights, and a smaller but strong house, broken beat and leftfield side around Darker Than Wax. Many Singaporeans associated the word techno with Eurodance for years, so the independent scene is a minority taste.

### Where to go for techno in Singapore?

For techno specifically, the rooms to check are Tuff Club, MDLR and Exit Reality (all run by Thugshop), RASA at Raffles Place, and the Friday series at 66 Chambers. Degenerave and Manifest SG run harder or more local nights. Check Resident Advisor's Singapore listings, because programmes change weekly and December had no underground events listed in mid-October.

### Are clubs in Singapore open late?

Many rooms run to about 3am, and it depends on the licence. In the Boat Quay and Clarke Quay areas, liquor hours can be extended to 4am on Thursday and Friday nights, and this became permanent from 18 August 2026. Elsewhere hours are set per venue. Public transport ends around midnight and public drinking is banned from 10.30pm to 7am, which affects how people get home.

### Are drugs tested at Singapore clubs?

Not at the door as far as we could find. The Central Narcotics Bureau runs operations inside nightspots using saliva test kits, such as one on 18 and 19 June 2026 that checked about 130 people, tested 61 and arrested one person. We found no source for routine urine tests at club doors. Consuming a controlled drug can bring 1 to 10 years in prison and a fine of up to S$20,000, including abroad for citizens and permanent residents.

### What happened to Headquarters?

Headquarters, the Boat Quay techno club that opened in May 2016, closed on 30 May 2026 after ten years. Its announcement gave no reason, according to FZINE and Time Out, while a later Mixmag Asia feature reported that the closure was a deliberate choice and that offers to buy the club and brand were declined. The operators, Thugshop, held a ten-hour closing night with ten b2b sets.

### Is Zouk still open?

Yes. Zouk closed in phases from January 2026 for a renovation and reopened at Clarke Quay with an event around 17 June 2026. It now has a combined capacity of up to 3,500 and is positioning itself as a lifestyle and brand-activation venue, so it is far from an underground room. Its ZoukOut beach festival returned in 2022, and we found no confirmed editions after 2023.

### Who are the best Singapore DJs?

We do not rank DJs, but the names that recur across sources include Xhin, Kavan Spruyt, Debbie Chia, Haan, Aya, Dexter Colt, Kaye, Sivanesh, Zig Zach, Intriguant and the queer collective Bussy Temple. Muttiah Ashnim is a Singapore-rooted techno artist now based in Germany. The right answer depends on whether you want techno, house, electronica or bass.

### Where to buy vinyl in Singapore?

Lifted Trax at 25 Lorong Telok opened on 5 August 2026 and sells second-hand and new dance, ambient, jazz, soul and disco records. Older options include Red Point Record Warehouse, The Analog Vault and Roxy Records and Trading, but their current status and hours need checking. Darker Than Wax and Ujikaji sell releases through Bandcamp. There are no vinyl pressing plants in Singapore, according to Evening Chants.

### What are the age rules?

Under the police licensing conditions we read, venues may not sell or allow liquor to anyone under 18, and under-16s may not be in premises with dancing outside restaurant hours. Many venues set their own door policy, often 21 and over, which is house policy and not the legal minimum.

### Are there queer-friendly nights?

Yes. FOMOHOMO runs queer nights and drag events at MDLR, Tuff Club and Host, Bussy Temple is a long-running queer rave collective, the Agni parties centre South Asian queer people, and 66 Chambers describes itself as welcoming to LGBTQ+ communities. Section 377A was repealed in November 2022 and came into force in January 2023, though queer events can still face approval challenges.
