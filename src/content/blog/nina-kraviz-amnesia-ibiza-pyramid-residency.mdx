Every Sunday from June 14 through October 4, 2026, with a single week off on July 19, Amnesia's doors open at 23:00 for Pyramid, the party that has quietly become the most argued-about night on the island. It is not the biggest production in Ibiza, it does not have a pyrotechnics budget to rival the superclubs down the road, and its own promotional language leans hard on a single idea: the dancefloor, not the DJ booth and not the VIP tables around it, is the point. Nina Kraviz has spent her 2026 summer as one of the artists building that argument from the inside, curating and headlining across the season rather than treating Ibiza as one tour stop among fifty.

## What Is Pyramid, and Why Does It Matter at Amnesia in 2026?

Pyramid is Amnesia's Sunday night residency, running the full length of the club's summer season and split across two rooms with distinct identities, the Terrace leaning into minimal house and forward-thinking techno, the Main Room carrying the harder techno programming, according to [Amnesia's own party page](https://www.amnesia.es/en/party-info/pyramid). The party launched in 2018 as a reaction against the commercialization that had crept into Ibiza clubbing, explicitly pulling inspiration from the island's rawer 1990s club culture, and its language has stayed consistent ever since: "music first, bodies together, freedom through dance." The site is blunter about it than most club marketing bothers to be: "This is not about watching. It's about dancing."

That framing carried real weight in 2025, when Pyramid was named Best Techno Party in Ibiza at the Vicious Music Awards, an honor covered by [Welcome to Ibiza](https://welcometoibiza.com/en/agenda-ibiza/noticias/ibiza-brilla-en-los-vicious-music-awards-2025/) as part of a wider night celebrating the island's music scene. Coming into 2026, [Ibiza Spotlight's overview of Amnesia's full weekly schedule](https://www.ibiza-spotlight.com/magazine/2026/05/amnesia-ibiza-week-overview) places Pyramid squarely in the techno, tech house and minimal lane on Sundays, distinct from the pop-leaning Rememberland or the reggaeton-driven BRESH that fill other nights of the club's calendar.

## What Is Amnesia's History, and Why Does the Venue Carry This Kind of Weight?

Amnesia is not a new build chasing an underground reputation, it earned one across half a century. The club opened in 1976 inside a converted finca, a traditional Ibicencan farmhouse that had belonged to the same family for five generations before it was leased to Antonio Escohotado, a Madrid philosopher who, together with Manolo Sáenz de Heredia, first opened the space as El Taller del Olvido, "The Workshop of Forgetting," before landing on the Greek-derived name Amnesia, as detailed in the venue's own history and on [Wikipedia's entry for the club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesia_(nightclub)). DJ Alfredo's residency there through the 1980s is widely credited with inventing what the world now calls Balearic beat, the loose, genre-blind mixing style that gave Ibiza its first real musical identity.

Amnesia turns 50 in 2026, and [DJ Mag's retrospective on the anniversary](https://djmag.com/features/celebrating-50-years-of-amnesia-ibiza-clubbing-institution) points out something unusual for a venue this influential: the building itself has stayed structurally almost untouched since it opened, aside from the addition of a roof. Across five decades it has hosted a string of residencies that effectively wrote Ibiza's clubbing history in real time, from Cream and Matinée to Foam & Diamonds and Sven Väth's long-running Cocoon nights. Pyramid inherits that room, and that history, every Sunday night.

## How Is Pyramid Different from Ibiza's VIP-Driven Club Culture?

Ibiza's superclub economy has, for two decades, run substantially on bottle service: tables sold at a premium, sightlines that favor who is sitting rather than who is dancing, and lineups built to justify the price of the table. Pyramid does not pretend this economy doesn't exist on the island, and it still sells VIP tables and early-access tickets like any other Amnesia night. What it sells differently is the framing. The party's own copy insists the dancefloor is "the soul of clubbing," not a backdrop for a table, and that positioning is exactly what the Vicious Music Awards recognized when it singled Pyramid out as the island's best techno party rather than its most visible one. It is a smaller, more specific claim than "biggest party in Ibiza," and it is the claim that has stuck.

## Who Are Pyramid's Curating Artists?

Pyramid's identity is shaped by three names who curate as well as headline across the season: Ricardo Villalobos, Richie Hawtin, and Nina Kraviz. All three come from the harder, more purist end of house and techno, and none of them built their careers on festival-mainstage spectacle.

### Ricardo Villalobos, Minimal Techno's Most Uncompromising Figure

Villalobos was born in Santiago, Chile, in 1970 and moved to Germany with his family at age three after Pinochet's coup, growing up to become, per his [Wikipedia biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Villalobos), one of minimal techno's defining figures. He began DJing professionally in 1998 after an unsuccessful early label venture, and hit his creative peak with epic-length, structurally loose albums like "Alcachofa" (2003) and "Thé Au Harem d'Archimède" (2004), records that helped define what minimal techno could sound like when stretched past dancefloor convention. His reputation for hours-long, hypnotic sets that build almost imperceptibly is part of why Pyramid's slower Terrace programming exists at all.

### Richie Hawtin, from Plastikman to Amnesia's Sunday Stage

Hawtin was born in Banbury, England, in 1970 and raised in LaSalle, Ontario, just across the river from Detroit, close enough to absorb the city's techno scene as lived culture rather than an import. Under his Plastikman alias he released the stark, foundational "Sheet One" in 1993, and, per [Wikipedia's account of his career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richie_Hawtin), co-founded Plus 8 Records in 1990 with John Acquaviva before launching M-nus in 1998. He has spent three decades as one of minimal techno's most technically rigorous performers, and his continued presence at Pyramid links Amnesia's Sunday night directly back to the Detroit-Windsor lineage the genre grew out of.

## What Did Nina Kraviz Actually Play at Pyramid in 2026?

On July 5, 2026, Kraviz played a set at Pyramid that ran one hour, 55 minutes and 54 seconds, moving through 31 tracks across tech house and techno, according to the full tracklist logged on [1001Tracklists](https://www.1001tracklists.com/tracklist/fnw6qd1/nina-kraviz-pyramid-amnesia-ibiza-spain-2026-07-05.html). It's the kind of set length that suits a residency rather than a festival slot: long enough to build a real arc rather than hit a greatest-hits sequence in 60 minutes.

Her biggest date of the season came on August 2, 2026, when she headlined alongside Richie Hawtin, backed by Fatima Hajji, Patrick Mason and Andres Campo, with Mha Iri, Mar-T, ONYVAA and Luca Donzelli filling out the rest of the bill, as listed on [Calrave's event page](https://calrave.com/en/events/pyramid-hawtin-kraviz-amnesia-ibiza/). That pairing of Kraviz and Hawtin on one night, both credited curators of the party rather than guest bookings, says something about how Pyramid structures its calendar: the people shaping the party's identity also headline it, repeatedly, across the season.

Kraviz's presence at Pyramid in 2026 also sits inside a broader pattern worth noting. She has regularly shared the season's bills with other leading women in techno, including Amelie Lens, Fatima Hajji and Clara Cluvé, a lineup logic that echoes the collaborative energy RTS.FM covered in [Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte's One Mind EP](/blog/amelie-lens-charlotte-de-witte-one-mind), where two of techno's most prominent women built a record together rather than competing for the same festival slot.

## Why Anchor a Summer Around One Residency Instead of Festival-Hopping?

A DJ at Kraviz's level could spend a summer chasing festival fees across three continents, and she does plenty of that too, as covered in [RTS.FM's roundup of her 2026 world tour dates](/blog/nina-kraviz-2026-world-tour-dates). But choosing to return to the same room on the same night, repeatedly, across a single season is a different kind of statement than a one-off festival headline slot. It builds a relationship with one crowd, one soundsystem and one club's history instead of a rotating audience that shows up once and moves on. For an artist whose broader 2026, including a new label and the shape of that world tour, is laid out in [RTS.FM's recap of Nina Kraviz's 2026](/blog/nina-kraviz-2026-recap), Pyramid reads less like a tour stop and more like a chosen home base. Ibiza has always rewarded artists who commit to a residency over artists who simply pass through, and Kraviz choosing Amnesia's Sunday slot, repeatedly, over a full summer says she is playing the island's longer game rather than its shorter one.
