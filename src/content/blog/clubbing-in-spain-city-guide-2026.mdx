Spain has more than one nightlife. It has a year-round city scene in Barcelona and Madrid, a seven-month island season in Ibiza, a half-mythologised rave heritage around Valencia, and a patchwork of smaller regional rooms that barely appear on the international radar. This guide covers all of them, with a focus on Barcelona, Madrid and Valencia, and it names the clubs, the recurring nights and the promoters that we could source.

To see where Spain's electronic nights actually happen, and how often, we collected Resident Advisor's public event listings for all 13 of its Spain areas on 24 October 2026 and counted what is listed from Saturday 24 October to Sunday 1 November. That week is unusual in two ways, and the guide says so: it includes Halloween weekend, and it falls at the start of the winter, when the big Ibiza clubs have been shut for a few weeks. RA lists only events that promoters submit to it, so it over-represents ticketed electronic nights and under-represents commercial, Latin and word-of-mouth parties. Treat every RA figure below as a snapshot of RA, not a census of Spanish clubbing.

**The short version:** on RA, Barcelona is Spain's busiest city by a distance and has something on every night; Madrid is a three-night week on RA, packed into Thursday to Saturday; Valencia has a strong heritage but a small RA footprint, which says more about where its promoters sell tickets than about the scene; and Ibiza is on its winter tail, with a handful of local rooms open. We link each claim to what we could verify and flag what we could not.

## Barcelona

#### The scene in one paragraph

Barcelona's club map runs from a multi-room Poblenou institution to an 80-capacity booth room in the Gothic Quarter, a ballroom that has stood since 1903 and a Funktion-One room inside the Poble Espanyol on Montjuïc, all within one city. The geography is clustered: Raval and the Gothic Quarter for small, loud rooms (Moog, Macarena, Les Enfants Brillants); Poble-sec for LAUT and Nitsa at Sala Apolo; Poblenou for Razzmatazz and the Bridge_48 studio collective; Montjuïc for INPUT and, in summer, La Terrrazza. The big beach-front clubs are a separate story (see the local debate section below). What follows is built from official club pages, RA's own Barcelona clubs guide, and press, and anything we could not source is flagged.

### Clubs to know

#### Razzmatazz

**Razzmatazz** is at Carrer dels Almogàvers 122 in Poblenou (second entrance for rooms 2 and 3 on Carrer de Pamplona 88, per the club's own site). Wikipedia dates the current venue to 2000, when the former Sala Zeleste, a concert room that moved to Almogàvers in 1986 and closed in 2000 after debts to Spanish social security, was renewed under new owners and renamed after a Pulp song. The club's site says "25 years connecting music and the city" and lists five numbered rooms; Wikipedia's infobox gives capacities of 2,100 (main hall), 940 (The Loft) and 200 (Lolita), which we could not cross-check on the official site. Its programme is organised as nights under the RAZZCLUBS banner: RazzClub (Friday and Saturday, a wide electronic and rap mix), Fuego (Friday), Human (Saturday: techno, house, electro, breaks, rave, industrial, synth and acid), Torax (Sunday: techno and house), plus midweek pop and hits nights (El Dirty on Wednesday, Mandanga on Thursday) and Common People on Sunday. RA's guide highlights Human as the Saturday series for multidisciplinary artists, live bands and local talent. Fury, the event behind the "3rd Anniversary" listings, is on the site for Sunday 1 November with rooms named Fury, Madhouse, Kinyxx and Hardcore Maniaks; we could not confirm who promotes Fury. Razzlloween on Friday 30 October showed a waiting list.

#### Sala Apolo and Nitsa Club

**Sala Apolo** sits on Avinguda del Paral·lel in Poble-sec. The venue's own site calls it one of the oldest dance halls in Europe and traces it to an amusement park on Paral·lel, later a ballroom, bingo hall, skating club and nightclub; it marked a 75th anniversary in 2018. Spanish Wikipedia says it was born in 1943 as a dance hall and gives a capacity of 2,900 across three rooms (not confirmed on the official site). The club night is **Nitsa**, which RA's guide describes as housed in the former theatre and a fixture of the city's electronic scene for over three decades, with big names on Fridays and Saturdays across two rooms; it lists Aphex Twin, Kenny Larkin and Carl Craig among past acts. Apolo's own agenda for 24 October was "Nitsa: Yung Prado All Night Long", and its other recurring brands include Diablada, Bresh, Nalgas Club, Milkshake, Oxido and El Dúplex. The site also announced a d&b Soundscape immersive system debuting 5 November.

#### Moog

**Moog** is at Carrer Arc del Teatre 3 in El Raval and bills itself as the first techno club in Barcelona. RA's guide gives "open since 1996" and says it runs 365 days a year, with pop-oriented sets upstairs and harder techno and house on the underground floor. The club's site shows it is part of the Mas i Mas group and lists hours of 23:59 to 05:00 Sunday to Thursday and 23:59 to 06:00 on Friday and Saturday. Its agenda runs fixed weekly nights: Carol Nyx on Sundays, Uroz on Mondays, Rubén Seoane on Tuesdays and Javi Gon on Saturdays. Listed event prices were mostly 5 to 10 euros, with headline nights up to 20 euros (for example DJ Hell and Reka at 20 euros).

#### Macarena Club

**Macarena Club** is at Carrer Nou de Sant Francesc 5 in the Gothic Quarter. RA's guide says it has kept its name since the 1920s, when it opened as a flamenco tablao, that capacity is 80, that it runs a Funktion-One system with the DJ booth in the middle of the floor, and that it is open seven days a week. Time Out dates its life as an electronic club to 2011 (older than RA's guide, which says seven days; Time Out says Thursday to Sunday, so treat the opening days as something to check). Music is techno and house.

#### INPUT High Fidelity Dance Club

**INPUT High Fidelity Dance Club** is inside Poble Espanyol on Montjuïc (Av. de Francesc Ferrer i Guàrdia 13). Its own site describes a Funktion-One system with 360-degree LED visuals and says it is open weekends. RA's guide describes an open-plan room for headsy house and techno with weekly international guests and no named residents. In the window its agenda included CULTO "It's A Ritual" (24 October) and a Halloween night by PRISM with Quelza. We could not source an opening year.

#### City Hall

**City Hall** is at Rambla Catalunya 2 to 4 in a former theatre. Its site says it is open every day of the year and programs international techno, hard techno, house and urban music, with a fixed weekday format: Monday Perreo, Tuesday Tiny Rave (hard bounce, schranz, industrial), Wednesday showcase, Thursday two rooms. It topped the RA window with 10 listings. We could not source an opening year or capacity.

#### LAUT

**LAUT** is a small club in a former carpentry workshop at Carrer de Vila i Vilà 63 in Poble-sec. A listing site gives 2017 as its opening year and Friday and Saturday midnight to 05:00 as hours (unverified on an official page, which would not load for us). RA's guide calls it a no-frills industrial room with a powerful system, programming dark techno, house and experimental sounds.

#### Bikini, La Paloma and others

**Bikini** (Sala Bikini) bills itself as both a nightclub and a concert venue. Its official site lists accounts for Bikini Club, live concerts, Tunnel and NOM, so NOM appears to be one of its in-house nights; we could not confirm who runs it, nor the address from that page. RA lists NOM presenting John Digweed at Bikini on Saturday 31 October.

**La Paloma** is on the Raval and Sant Antoni border. Monocle reports it opened as a dance hall in 1903, dancing stopped in 2007 after long-running noise complaints, and it reopened after 16 years; Mixmag and RA both date the reopening to the end of 2023. Mixmag gives capacity as 1,000. Owner Mercè March has run it since 1977 (Monocle). Mixmag lists regular nights from Glove Party, Polenta, Locobongo, Vida Club, Mentha, Eck Echo, Odd Club and Mainline Collective. RA's autumn 2025 programme included Louie Vega, Mr Scruff, Soul Clap and Yuksek.

**Les Enfants Brillants** is in El Raval on Carrer de Guàrdia, with an L-Acoustics system and a capacity of 400, programming what RA's guide calls classy minimal and tech house. **Studio Stereo** is, per RA's guide, an "audiophile social club" in a 19th-century Catalan estate with a Pequod Acoustics dancefloor and a separate listening room; RA places it in the Dreta de l'Eixample but also mentions La Rambla, so we would not quote an address.

**Bridge_48** is less a club than a Poblenou collective and studios complex: Mixmag (5 June 2024) says it launched in 2019, runs nine recording studios plus co-working and event space, and organises the annual Bridge Festival. **M7 Club** (7 RA listings) and **Row14** could not be verified from any source we could open.

### What to avoid and the local debate

The city has a record of fighting nightlife in its dense centre. In 2019 Barcelona declined to renew the beachfront concessions of Shôko, Pachá, Carpe Diem and Opium, ending 21 June, with the premises earmarked for a CSIC marine-sciences expansion, and opened safety sanctions on three of them (Time Out, 7 May 2019). In November 2023 the city suspended two decrees that had capped bar hours (2am) and terraces (11pm) around Plaça George Orwell and Carrer Joaquim Costa for noise; the council cited procedural defects and promised a new anti-noise plan and eight more sound meters (Ara). La Paloma's closure from 2007, which lasted 16 years, followed long-running noise complaints (Monocle).

## Madrid

### The scene in one paragraph

On Resident Advisor's map, Madrid is Spain's second electronic city, behind Barcelona, and it is built around contrasts: basement rooms off Gran Via and Calle Alcala at one end, and at the other Fabrik, the largest macro-club in the Community of Madrid, sitting in an industrial estate in Humanes de Madrid. The city has a long club memory (Specka has been open since 1989, Mondo Disko says it has run since 1999, Fabrik opened in 2003) and a newer layer of small specialist rooms booking hard techno, house, disco and queer or kink nights side by side.

One caveat before the numbers. Everything below that cites Resident Advisor refers to RA listings, 24 Oct to 1 Nov 2026. RA only shows events that promoters submit, so it over-represents electronic, ticketed nights and under-represents commercial, Latin and mainstream clubs. The window also includes Halloween weekend (Fri 30 and Sat 31 Oct), which skews any week.

### A bit of history: the Movida, and its limits

Madrid's nightlife mythology starts with La Movida Madrilena, the countercultural wave that followed Franco's death in 1975. Wikipedia gives its traditional starting point as a tribute concert to "Canito" on 9 February 1980 at the civil engineering school of the Technical University of Madrid, and says it lasted until the middle of the 1980s. It drew on punk, synth-pop and dada, and is tied to the Socialist mayor Enrique Tierno Galvan, whose city government took an open approach and subsidised artistic projects. Pedro Almodovar is its best-known international face. Cult rooms named in the Spanish article include Rock-Ola, La Via Lactea, Marquee and El Penta.

The honest limit: the Movida was a rock, pop and synth-pop moment, not a club-music one, and none of the sources we opened draw a line from it to Madrid's techno and house scene. We have deliberately not claimed a direct lineage.

### Clubs to know

Prices, hours and door rules come from venue pages or third-party listing guides checked in late September and October 2026, and they change by event. Treat them as indicative.

**Fabrik** is the heavyweight. Spanish Wikipedia says it opened in 2003 on the M-413 in Humanes de Madrid, with a 5,000-capacity main room (a third-party guide says 6,000 for the venue overall), seven stages after the 2023 addition of Area 19, and a 43-room hotel on site since 2019. The techno brand CODE has run since the opening and elrow has had a residency since 2018, per the same article. DJ Mag readers placed Fabrik second in the world in 2011 and 17th in 2024; the third-party guide adds 14th in 2025 and 8th in 2026, which we could not confirm on DJ Mag itself. From that guide: events are mainly on Saturdays and special dates, doors open between 18:00 and 22:00 depending on the event, some parties run more than 12 hours, entry is 18+, EU citizens need a national ID card or passport, non-EU visitors a passport (driving licences are not accepted), and official shuttle buses run from Pintor Rosales 2 by the Templo de Debod. CODE's 23rd anniversary on 11 October 2026 was priced at EUR 50 general, EUR 90 VIP and EUR 150 backstage.

**Mondo Disko** (Mondo) calls itself an electronic music nightclub "since '99", and its site footer names DANCE WITH MACHINES, SL. Its nights are currently listed at Sala But, Calle de Barcelo 11, plus the open-air Terraza Jowke in Alcorcon for its Mondo Open Air and Mondo Krystal events; the site also names Club Cocó. A listing guide gives Thursday, Saturday and holiday eves, 23:59 to 06:00 (some afterparties to 08:00), 18+, and EUR 23 for Sala But nights on 10 and 11 October 2026. Mondo Open Air on 3 October 2026 (15:00 to 23:00) was EUR 35, with shuttle buses from Atocha. An older ticketing page still places Mondo Disko at Sala Coco, Calle Alcala 20, so the brand has moved between rooms; check the address before you go. RA lists Mondo three times in the window.

**Sala Coco** is the Calle Alcala room that ticketing pages link to Mondo Disko, and RA lists two HYBRID nights there in the window. We could not open a page for the room itself, so its history and the details of HYBRID stay out of this guide.

**Specka** is the elder statesman: a listing guide gives Calle Orense 26 (basement), says the club has run since 1989 and calls itself the longest-running electronic music discotheque in Spain. Its sound moved from post-punk, new wave and EBM through trance to techno and electro, on a Funktion-One system. Club nights are Fridays and Saturdays from about 23:30 or midnight, 18+, tickets roughly EUR 6 to 30.

**The Bassement** is at Calle de Galileo 26 in Chamberi: about 1,000 square metres in two areas with four bars, open mainly Fridays and Saturdays from midnight to 06:00, re-entry from 02:30 with a hand stamp. **LAB theCLUB** sits in the attic of Chamartin station, with access from Calle Agustin de Foxa 40, per its own site. A third-party guide says it was formerly Macumba. The site lists Mathame on Friday 30 October, La Malquerida presents Roger Sanchez on Sunday 1 November, Pendulum DJ set on 6 November and Claptone on 7 November, and says Saturdays belong to the Space of Sound series.

**berlinClub** runs inside Cafe Berlin at Costanilla de los Angeles 20. A guide says club nights began in 2012, the cafe reopened in a 500 square metre space in March 2016 after closing its old room in January 2016, and capacity is 254. ### Festivals and the calendar

Madrid's festival layer sits mostly outside electronic music. Wikipedia lists Mad Cool (pop, rock, indie, some DJs) on 8 to 11 July 2026 at the Iberdrola Music site in Villaverde, and says Dcode (indie, rock, pop and electronic, Complutense University campus) began in 2011; we did not confirm Dcode's dates. ### Rules, doors and the Madrid Arena shadow

On licensing, the Community of Madrid said in a 30 April 2022 press release that under a new order discotheques and dance halls keep a 17:00 opening and 05:30 closing, with restaurant-licensed discotheques allowed to open from 12:00. We could not confirm whether that order has changed, and several listings above show nights to 06:00 and afterparties to 08:00. We cannot reconcile those from the sources we opened, so check any specific night.

Door norms vary: Specka and The Bassement say 18+, LuLa 21+, El Sotano admits 16+ to concerts. ID rules differ too, from Fabrik (passport or national ID, no driving licences) to Mondo (passport, national ID or driving licence recommended).

A Halloween-weekend guide to Madrid should mention the Madrid Arena. On 1 November 2012, at an electronic Halloween party with Steve Aoki on the bill, a crowd crush at the municipal arena killed five young women. Police found about 16,791 people inside a venue with a stated maximum of 10,600, per Spanish Wikipedia, which attributes the crush mainly to overcrowding and errors by security and police. The same article notes that, two months later, Local Police closed parts of Fabrik on 31 December 2012 as a precaution. We have not sourced a list of the rules that followed.

## Valencia

### Why it deserves a close look

Valencia has the most mythologised club history in Spain and a modern scene that is smaller on RA and harder to read. Two surviving anchors, Barraca (in Sueca) and Spook Club (in Pinedo), sit outside the centre and run on a shuttle-bus, Saturday-night model, while newer rooms such as Oven and Killing Time operate in and around Ruzafa. In 2026 the city also made nightlife a political fight over noise.

### A short history of the Ruta del Bakalao

The Ruta del Bakalao, also called Ruta Destroy or "La Ruta", was a weekend circuit of discotheques around Valencia. English Wikipedia dates it to roughly 1982 to 1996 and describes a 30 km circuit along the CV-500, the El Saler road, running from Friday to Monday. Its start is fuzzy: Spanish Wikipedia says the seed of the Ruta "has no clear origin date", points to late-1970s groups such as La Banda de Gaal in 1979, and says the word "bakalao" came from a record shop, Zic Zac, in 1983. "Ruta Destroy" is credited to Vicente Pizcueta, a Barraca director, who publicly called it the "Valencian Festival".

The anchors, per the two Wikipedia articles:

- **Barraca**: opened 6 December 1965 in a traditional Valencian barraca house at Les Palmeres in Sueca; Carlos Simo took over its booth in 1982 as programming moved from post-punk and synth-pop towards house and techno-pop.
- **Chocolate**: a former rice mill near Barraca, dark, gothic and industrial; sources disagree on dates (English Wikipedia says 1982, another guide 1980), so we leave the year open.
- **Spook Factory**: opened 1984 on the site of a former disco; techno and EBM.
- **Puzzle** (1986, synth-pop then acid house) and **ACTV** (1986, Malvarrosa beach, new beat and acid house).

The music drifted through the late 1980s and early 1990s towards EBM, techno and eventually a faster, simpler "bakalao" or makina sound; Spanish Wikipedia says ecstasy arrived around 1987 to 1988 and that by 1991 more than 30,000 young people were attending.

The comedown: English Wikipedia says that in late 1993 a police operation called "Operation Bakalao" put more than 100 Guardia Civil officers on the CV-500, with checkpoints, searches and helicopters. Spanish Wikipedia lists loss of drug purity, door violence and political and media pressure (including coverage after the Alcasser case) among the causes, with a steep decline from 1995 and the good atmosphere over in 1996, the year Spook Factory closed. Both are open-source summaries and partly uncited.

Afterwards, Spanish Wikipedia says, the scene deflated, then recovered from the early 2000s through new promoters. Spook Factory reopened in 2005 as Spook Club Vlc and took that name again in September 2008. Puzzle closed for good on 31 October 2011. Barraca is still open after about 50 years, Chocolate opens only occasionally, and the Friends after-hours at Bananas (summer 2004) was shut for good in 2006.

### Regulation and the Ruzafa debate

The sharpest sourced story is the "zona acusticamente saturada" (ZAS), or acoustically saturated zone, for Ruzafa. El Espanol reports that the city announced on 21 April 2026 that it would declare Ruzafa a ZAS at the plenary session on 30 April; ESdiario reports it was approved on 30 April 2026 with the votes of the governing PP and Vox. Valencia Plaza reported in 2024 that a 2020 Superior Court of Justice ruling required the noise study behind it. Per El Espanol the measures cover 18 streets plus a surrounding "respect zone", suspend new terraces and extensions, block new background-music licences in certain venues, close convenience stores from 22:00 to 08:00, and set discotheque closing at 03:30 Sunday to Thursday and 04:30 on Fridays, Saturdays and holiday eves, with Fallas and certain holiday nights exempt. We could not open the final published text, so check the legal status of those hours.

Reaction split the city. The Asociacion Empresarial de Discotecas de Valencia and a Ruzafa hospitality association called it a "cortina de humo" (smokescreen) after a court ruling against festivals at the Ciudad de las Artes y las Ciencias, per El Espanol. A spokesman for the Coordinadora de Ocio y Hosteleria de los Barrios argued noise comes mainly from traffic and infrastructure; Compromis and the PSPV-PSOE called the plan ineffective. Whether it touches Barraca and Spook, which sit outside the city, our sources do not say. We found no sourced general closing time for discotheques across the Comunitat Valenciana, so we give none.

### Practical facts: hours, rules, prices, transport

**Hours.** In December 2024 the Ibiza nightlife employers' association AEON (Noches de Ibiza) said discotheques currently run from 16:00 to 06:00 the next day, and asked for a new schedule of 22:00 to 06:00 with daytime leisure ending at 20:00. That was a request, not a rule. AEON also said daytime leisure in hotels and restaurants has no regulated hours or capacity even though the Consell d'Eivissa has competence (Noudiari, 19 December 2024).

**The 2024 decree.** In May 2024 the Balearic government toughened its tourism-excess rules. According to The Local and Euronews: shops in specified tourist zones may not sell alcohol from 21:30 to 08:00 (the Balearic government said this extended an existing rule), street drinking in those zones is banned except on terraces and licensed areas, fines run from EUR 500 to EUR 1,500 (up to EUR 3,000 for disruptive behaviour), and party boats may not operate within one nautical mile of the designated areas or pick up passengers there. On Ibiza the zone is Sant Antoni de Portmany (only part of the West End strip); on Mallorca it covers Palma, Calvia (Magaluf) and Llucmajor. Bars, clubs and restaurants can keep serving. Effective 11 May 2024 and due to run to at least December 2027. It builds on the 2020 ban on happy hours, two-for-one drinks and pub crawls.

**A licence freeze, proposed.** In 2026 the Consell d'Eivissa proposed freezing new nightclub licences island-wide as part of its tourism areas intervention plan (PIAT). First Vice President Mariano Juan said it would not close existing clubs. Most municipalities except Sant Josep have restricted new openings locally for about 15 years. The report describes a proposal only; we found no report of approval.

**Prices.** Ibiza clubs sell tickets by night and line-up, so there is no flat door price. Data points we can source: Pacha's charity party from EUR 7 and New Year's Eve from EUR 60, with VIP bookings from EUR 350 (Pacha site); Akasha roughly EUR 10 to EUR 15 off peak; TIMEmACHINE EUR 3 to EUR 5.

**Getting around.** The Discobus is the cheap club link: per Ibiza Spotlight's July 2026 guide, the D1 runs San Antonio, UNVRS, Amnesia, Pacha and Ibiza Port, the D3 runs San Antonio, DC10 and Ushuaïa/Hï, and new in 2026 are the D2 (Playa d'en Bossa, Ibiza Town, Pacha) and the D4 (Ibiza, Santa Eulalia, Es Cana). A single costs about EUR 4 to EUR 5 and full service ends in late September, with a lighter service in October. Pacha and UNVRS run free shuttles with proof of a ticket. Taxis: about EUR 16 from Ibiza Town and EUR 32 from San Antonio to Hï, and EUR 32 to EUR 37 from Ibiza Town to Akasha. In winter, assume a taxi or a car.

### Seasonality: what exists now

The structural difference from Ibiza is that Mallorca has no single opening and closing weekend. Resort and beach venues are summer businesses (Sandbeds tags BCM, Social Club and the Cala Rajada rooms as summer or April to October), while a handful of Palma and Platja de Palma rooms trade all year. In late October the island therefore runs on two things: Halloween, which is the one date every venue wants (BCM on the 31st, AMOK on the 30th and 31st, Barraco in Deia and Hotel OD at Port Portals on RA), and the year-round rooms with a regular Friday and Saturday calendar. After that the next dated peaks we can source are Christmas Day and New Year's Eve at BCM. For an electronic fan the practical advice is to treat Mallorca in winter as a weekend city-club destination, built around Palma and AMOK, rather than a festival-style island.

## Spain-wide: hours, rules, festivals and safety

### Closing hours are regional, then municipal

Spain has no single national closing time. Autonomous regions set the framework and town halls can extend or cut it.

- **Catalonia.** During the exceptional Christmas extension (21 Dec 2025 to 6 Jan 2026), a Departament d'Interior order let discotheques and dance halls close at 05:45 Sunday to Thursday and 06:45 on Fridays, Saturdays and holiday eves, according to Diari de Tarragona. The article says the extension added up to 45 minutes, which implies ordinary hours of 05:00 and 06:00 (our arithmetic, not stated in the article).
- **Valencia region.** Consumoteca (updated 23 Dec 2025) gives discotheques and dance halls 17:00 to 07:30, with a 60-minute extension on listed dates including 31 October.
- **Madrid region.** The framework is Order 1562/1998. The Community of Madrid said in a 30 April 2022 press release that discotheques and dance halls keep a 17:00 opening and 05:30 closing; several current listings show nights to 06:00 and after-parties to 08:00, which we cannot reconcile, so check any specific night.
- **Balearics.** Hours are set largely by councils. The Calvia ordinance, published in the BOIB in 2020, caps discotheques at 06:00.

### Afters, tardeo and age

"Afters" (after-hours sessions that start when licensed clubs close) are a real part of Madrid and Ibiza nightlife and sit in a grey legal zone: Spanish press has covered unlicensed after-hours in Malasana and Chueca with neighbour complaints and enforcement. Licensed daytime sessions are growing: El Periodico noted in June 2025 that "tardeo" (late-afternoon drinking and dancing) was spreading to venues with and without Interior permits. The one sourced door norm is age: 18 is Spain's minimum age to buy or consume alcohol (Wikipedia). We found no reliable source for typical entry or drinks prices across the country, so we give prices only for specific nights cited above.

### Rules and debates

The regulatory fight in 2026 is mostly about noise and tourism. Valencia's Ruzafa noise zone and the Balearic 2024 decree are covered in their city sections. In Barcelona, ARA reported in November 2023 that the city suspended two decrees that capped bar and terrace hours around Plaça George Orwell, and Time Out reported in 2019 that the beachfront club concessions were not renewed. For the European picture of how often such decisions close rooms, see [our club closures tracker](/blog/europe-club-closures-tracker).

### Safety

After a wave of reports of spiking by needle in summer 2022, Spanish regions published protocols: Catalonia's included HIV prophylaxis and urgent tests (20Minutos, 31 July 2022), Mossos had received 23 complaints (El Punt Avui, 2 August 2022), and Confidencial Digital reported in August 2024 a summer without such complaints after added security. Cadena SER and Diario de Ibiza reported on 13 October 2026 around 90 drug-possession complaints filed in Sant Antoni during discotheque closing parties. We could not find a source confirming current drug-checking services at Spanish clubs or festivals, so we describe none.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Spanish city has the most electronic events?

Barcelona, on Resident Advisor's listings. On 24 October 2026 RA showed 567 upcoming events for Barcelona and 388 for Madrid, and no other Spanish area had more than 60. RA lists only events that promoters submit, so it over-represents ticketed electronic nights.

### What time do clubs close in Spain?

It depends on the region and the town. Catalonia's Christmas order of 2025 let discotheques close at 05:45 on Sunday to Thursday and 06:45 on Friday, Saturday and holiday eves, which implies ordinary hours of 05:00 and 06:00. The Valencia region lists discotheques from 17:00 to 07:30. Madrid said in 2022 that discotheques close at 05:30, although some current listings show later nights. The Calvia ordinance on Mallorca caps discotheques at 06:00.

### When does the Ibiza season start and end?

In 2026 the big clubs opened between 4 April (Es Paradis) and 9 May (Amnesia), and closed in the first half of October, with Amnesia's Closing Festival on 10 October. No club has published official 2027 opening dates, and one ticketing site forecasts the weekend of 23 to 25 April 2027, labelled as a forecast.

### What was the Ruta del Bakalao?

A weekend circuit of discotheques around Valencia, running along the CV-500 from Friday to Monday, roughly from 1982 to 1996 according to English Wikipedia. Barraca, Spook Factory, Chocolate, Puzzle and ACTV were its anchors. A police operation on the road in late 1993 and a decline in the mid-1990s ended its heyday. Barraca and Spook Club still open today.

### Is Valencia's club scene small?

Not necessarily. RA lists only 52 upcoming events for Valencia, but that counts events promoters put on RA. Barraca and Spook sell tickets on Xceed and other platforms. What the RA week shows is a Saturday-led scene concentrated in a handful of venues.

### Which clubs should I try in Barcelona?

For electronic music: Razzmatazz (the Human night on Saturdays), Sala Apolo (Nitsa), Moog (techno, open every night), Macarena Club (an 80-capacity room), INPUT (Funktion-One, open weekends), LAUT and City Hall. Check each club's agenda: Nitsa, Human and Moog's weekly hosts change by night.

### How old do you have to be to go clubbing in Spain?

Spain's minimum age to buy or consume alcohol is 18, and clubs set their own rules. Specka and The Bassement in Madrid say 18+, LuLa 21+, and Fabrik wants a passport or national ID card (not a driving licence). Check the venue before you go.

### When are Sonar and Primavera Sound in 2027?

Sonar runs 17 to 19 June 2027 and Primavera Sound's main days are 3 to 5 June 2027, with a free opening day on 2 June and the closing party Primavera Bits on 6 June.
