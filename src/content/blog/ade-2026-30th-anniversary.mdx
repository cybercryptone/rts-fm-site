Amsterdam Dance Event turns 30 this October, and the timing has produced a coincidence that says more about electronic music's current moment than either anniversary does on its own. The week ADE marks three decades as the genre's biggest annual gathering, October 21 through 25, 2026, is the exact same week Resident Advisor's own 25th anniversary world tour rolls through the city. Two institutions, a festival format and a media outlet, both old enough now to be celebrating themselves, landing in the same five square miles of Amsterdam within days of each other.

That's not a coincidence anyone manufactured. ADE has run every October since 1996, and RA scheduled its Amsterdam stop for October 22 specifically because the city fills up with the entire industry that week anyway. But it's a useful marker of where the genre actually is in 2026. There's now enough electronic music history that its landmark institutions are old enough to have institutional memory of their own, stacking anniversaries on anniversaries in a way that would have made no sense twenty years ago, because none of this infrastructure was old enough yet.

This piece covers what ADE's 30th actually contains, from a first wave lineup announcement through a second that pushed the confirmed artist count past 1,500, to the anniversary-specific programming built around it, and why the RA connection is more than a scheduling footnote.

## How big is ADE's 30th anniversary edition?

ADE has never worked like a normal festival with one site and one gate. Since 1996 it's run as a citywide takeover, club nights and parties spread across every warehouse, church, theatre, and pop-up space Amsterdam has to offer, with a daytime professional conference layered on top. The 30th anniversary edition keeps that format and scales it further. ADE Pro, the industry conference side, runs October 21 through 24, while club nights and parties run across all five nights of the week. Organizers are billing it as the largest edition in the festival's history, with the eventual full program set to grow past 3,000 artists across more than 200 venues citywide, according to [ADE's own announcement](https://www.amsterdam-dance-event.nl/en/news/amsterdam-dance-event-reveals-250-artists-in-first-lineup-release-for-30-years-ade/2806298/).

Put plainly: there is no ADE main stage. A church hosts a live set one night, a former industrial site hosts a techno rave the next, and the only thing holding the week together is the ADE Pro badge and a shared program guide. That decentralized model has always been the point, treating the entire city as the venue rather than building a fenced-off festival ground, but at 30 years old and 200-plus venues deep, it's also a model straining against its own scale, worth examining later.

## Who's confirmed for ADE's 30th anniversary lineup?

ADE announced its first wave of more than 250 artists in early September 2026, as [Beatportal reported](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1496910-amsterdam-dance-event-announces-first-wave-of-artists-for-30th-anniversary-edition). A second wave followed shortly after, pushing the total confirmed count past 1,500 artists, per [Festival Insights](https://www.festivalinsights.com/2026/09/amsterdam-dance-event-reveals-second-wave-of-artists-for-30th-anniversary/), well before the full program (eventually expected to top 3,000) is even finalized.

The names confirmed so far cover most of the genre's current commercial and underground spectrum in one week. Techno and tech house bring Adam Beyer, [Amelie Lens](/artists/amelie-lens), Chloe Caillet, Ame, Maceo Plex, and Nico Moreno. The UK contingent includes Fumi, Sammy Virji, Skepta, and HAAi. From house and disco-adjacent lineages there's The Martinez Brothers and Todd Terry, still playing the genre he helped build in the 1980s. Melodic and progressive pull in Tinlicker and Eric Prydz, and big-room crossover names David Guetta (bringing his AMF/Monolith production to Amsterdam) and Armin van Buuren round out the commercial end. Kittin and The Hacker represent electroclash's continued relevance, Max Cooper and Paul Kalkbrenner cover the more contemplative and live-performance end of the spectrum, and [Jeff Mills](/artists/jeff-mills) is booked to play an Awakenings show at SugarFactory, a pairing of Detroit's founding techno generation with the Dutch promoter most responsible for exporting Amsterdam's techno scene worldwide.

That range, from a Detroit originalist playing a 400-capacity room to a stadium-scale name like Guetta headlining an arena show the same week, is itself the story of how wide ADE's tent has become. It's also exactly the kind of program depth that keeps label showcases from imprints like [Kompakt](/blog/kompakt-records-cologne-history) and [Rawax](/blog/rawax-records-robert-drewek) a fixture of ADE week every year, smaller rooms where a label's actual roster plays together rather than one headliner surrounded by filler.

## What's different about the 30th anniversary programming specifically?

Beyond the lineup scale, ADE built a handful of pieces specifically for the 30-year mark. The centerpiece is Jean-Michel Jarre, named the official 30th-anniversary guest of honour, performing the opening concert at AFAS Live in ArenAPoort. Jarre isn't a token booking here. He's one of electronic music's actual founding-generation artists, a composer whose 1976 album Oxygene predates ADE itself by two decades and predates most of the artists on this year's bill by longer than that. Opening a 30th anniversary week with him is a deliberate bookend: the genre's living history welcoming in whatever comes next, in a festival format itself now old enough to need that kind of framing.

The rest of the anniversary programming leans into spectacle and cross-discipline collaboration rather than just more club nights. Artist Boris Acket presents an installation called EINDER. Het Nationale Ballet and dance company ISH open ADE with a piece titled "Swan Lake Remixed for ADE," pairing a 19th-century ballet score with electronic reinterpretation in what's presumably the least techno-adjacent room booked all week. ARTIS Aquarium hosts a 4DSOUND spatial-audio experience, using the venue's tanks and acoustics for something closer to sound art than a DJ set. There's a special anniversary ADE Pro Pass bag for delegates, and "30 YEARS ADE" branding running through all official programming, the kind of institutional self-marking that only makes sense once an event has enough history to look back on.

## Why does Resident Advisor's own anniversary tour matter this same week?

Here's the connection that makes this week genuinely unusual rather than just another big ADE year. RTS.FM covered Resident Advisor's [25th anniversary](/blog/resident-advisor-25th-anniversary) earlier this month, including the RA25 world tour, ten dates running from a September 25 London show through a December 5 finale in Sydney, where RA was founded in 2001. One of those ten stops is Amsterdam, on October 22, 2026: a free event called Greenhouse Sessions at Bar San Francisco, and RA's own tour announcement places it deliberately during Amsterdam Dance Event.

That's not RA piggybacking on ADE for foot traffic, or at least not only that. It's two of electronic music's institutions, a 30-year-old festival format and a 25-year-old media outlet, both mid-anniversary-tour, choosing to occupy the same city in the same week because that's simply where the industry already gathers every October. RA's editorial team didn't need to build an Amsterdam stop into its own milestone year from scratch. ADE had already made the choice for them thirty years ago, by turning one European city into the genre's annual meeting point every autumn.

Worth sitting with for a second: a publication that spends its year reviewing other people's anniversaries is now old enough to be running its own tour through a festival that's celebrating an anniversary of its own, in the same week. That's what a genre with enough accumulated history looks like. It has institutions that outlive single scenes, and those institutions are now old enough to mark their own birthdays, sometimes in the same city, in the same October.

## Is ADE now too big to actually experience?

It's worth being honest about the tension "largest edition in ADE's history" creates rather than just repeating it as a selling point. More than 3,000 artists across 200-plus venues, with no single site tying the week together, is an extraordinary amount of programming density. It's also, for any one attendee, arguably unnavigable. Nobody sees ADE. They see a version assembled from whichever fraction of 200 venues they physically got to that week, shaped by which showcases they had wristbands for. The citywide format that makes ADE feel like Amsterdam itself becoming a festival is the same format that guarantees no two attendees' ADE looks remotely alike.

That's not a new problem exactly, ADE has been sprawling for years, but 30 years and 3,000-plus artists is a genuinely different order of scale than the event that started in 1996. The ADE Pro conference side, where labels, agents, and the business end of the industry actually do their work over four days, is arguably a better lens for understanding ADE's real function now than the club nights are. Conversations about [vinyl's continued sales strength](/blog/vinyl-sales-record-2025) or where independent labels sit in the market happen in ADE Pro conference rooms as much as any club, because that's genuinely where the deals and the discourse get made, even if the club nights are what gets photographed. Bigness and discovery are pulling in different directions here, and ADE's 30th is the edition where that tension is hardest to wave away.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is ADE's 30th anniversary edition and how big is it?

Amsterdam Dance Event's 30th anniversary edition runs October 21 through 25, 2026, with the ADE Pro conference running daytime hours October 21-24 and club nights and parties spread across all five nights. The full program is expected to grow past 3,000 artists across more than 200 venues citywide, making it, per organizers, the largest edition in ADE's history.

### Who is confirmed to play ADE 2026?

More than 1,500 artists were confirmed across two announcement waves as of late September 2026, including Adam Beyer, Amelie Lens, Chloe Caillet, Sammy Virji, Skepta, The Martinez Brothers, Eric Prydz, David Guetta, Armin van Buuren, Todd Terry, Jeff Mills, and Jean-Michel Jarre, who headlines the opening concert. The full lineup is still expected to grow, with organizers targeting over 3,000 artists in total.

### What is special about ADE's 30th anniversary programming?

Beyond the expanded lineup, ADE built anniversary-specific programming including Jean-Michel Jarre's opening concert at AFAS Live as official guest of honour, an installation called EINDER by artist Boris Acket, a collaborative opening piece called "Swan Lake Remixed for ADE" from Het Nationale Ballet and dance company ISH, and a 4DSOUND spatial-audio experience at ARTIS Aquarium, alongside dedicated "30 YEARS ADE" branding and a special anniversary ADE Pro Pass bag.

### Why is Resident Advisor's anniversary tour relevant to ADE's 30th?

Resident Advisor is marking its own 25th anniversary in 2026 with a ten-date world tour, and one of those stops, a free Greenhouse Sessions event at Bar San Francisco on October 22, lands in Amsterdam during ADE week by RA's own design. It means two of electronic music's institutions, a 30-year-old festival format and a 25-year-old media outlet, are both marking milestone anniversaries in the same city during the same week, a sign of how much accumulated history the genre's infrastructure now carries.

### Is ADE too big for one person to actually attend properly?

That's a fair tension to name rather than gloss over. With more than 3,000 artists spread across 200-plus venues and no central site, no single attendee can realistically experience more than a fraction of ADE in one week. The citywide, no-main-stage format that gives ADE its identity is the same format that makes total discovery effectively impossible, meaning most attendees end up with a personal, partial version of the festival shaped by whichever venues and showcases they physically reached.
