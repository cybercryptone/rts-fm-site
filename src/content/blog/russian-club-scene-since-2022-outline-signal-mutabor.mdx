Russia's club scene did not disappear after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, but it got smaller, more domestic and much more exposed to the state. Western headliners largely stopped coming, many Russian artists and promoters left, and the rooms and festivals that stayed have been pushed around by court cases, police raids and, in two recent summers, local authorities citing drones. The foreign DJs who did come back are documented mainly at festivals, above all Outline in 2024, and the evidence for club-level Western bookings in 2025 and 2026 is thin.

This piece follows that story from the first weeks of 2022 to this week's SHKEDUL date at RNDM in Moscow. We use the Russian term only once, here: Russian officials and media call the war the "special military operation", abbreviated in Russian as SVO. Everywhere else we describe it as the war or the invasion, because that is what it is in international law and in the sources we cite. RTS.FM is a radio and label project with roots in Moscow, so we have a stake in this story. This article takes no position on whether artists should or should not play in Russia. It reports what the sources say, attributes contested claims, and marks what we could not confirm.

**The short version:** Visa, Mastercard, the major labels and Ukraine's club scene all moved within the first two weeks of March 2022. Foreign acts "almost disappeared" from Russian festival lineups by 2023, according to Snob. Outline, run by the team behind the club Mutabor, returned in 2023 and drew Western DJs and criticism in 2024, then was cancelled on opening day in July 2026 on a district order citing drones. Signal was blocked at its home in the Kaluga region in 2025 and moved to Moscow with about 7,000 guests, down from more than 20,000. Mutabor, closed for 90 days in January 2024 after a scandal, was renamed Arma, raided in November 2024 and shut in April 2025; Gazgolder and Propaganda also closed. Western acts in Russia in 2025 and 2026 are documented mostly for pop and rap, plus a handful of dance names at a Moscow city festival, according to sources we flag as organiser-side.

## What changed in the first weeks of 2022?

Almost everything the international scene touches changed within about two weeks. The dates below are from the outlets named.

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 5 March 2022 | Visa and Mastercard announced they were suspending operations in Russia. | AP via PBS NewsHour |
| 5 March 2022 | Ukraine's electronic music scene, including the clubs Closer and Cxema, the Strichka festival and promoters and artists, published an open letter urging a boycott of Russian artists and organisations that do not actively oppose their government, and calling for refusing Russian money. | Resident Advisor; MusicTech reported 50 plus signatories, Monocle about 90 |
| 8 to 10 March 2022 | Universal, Sony and Warner each suspended their Russian operations. | NPR |
| 25 March 2022 | Spotify announced it would suspend its Russian service from April, citing new laws on "false information" about the army. | Meduza |
| 21 May 2022 | Nina Kraviz was dropped from Movement Detroit, The Crave in The Hague and PollerWiesen; her label's distributor, Clone, ended its relationship with the label. | Resident Advisor |
| 8 June 2022 | Ukrainian DJ Nastia pulled out of Awakenings rather than share a lineup with Russians she said tolerated the war. | Mixmag |
| 5 December 2022 | Russia expanded its "LGBT propaganda" ban from minors to the whole population. | The Moscow Times |

On Kraviz: none of the festivals gave a reason, according to RA, and the two European events said the decision was theirs. Clone's founder cited what he called her "pro-Putin" views. That is his stated reason as reported by RA, not an established fact. Kraviz said she had never been involved in supporting politicians or political parties, and her representatives said the critics' evidence was taken out of context. We cover her broader career in [our Nina Kraviz page](/artists/nina-kraviz).

On Nastia: Awakenings said it respected her decision but kept Kraviz and the Russian DJ Vladimir Dubyshkin, saying Dubyshkin had spoken out explicitly against the war and Kraviz had made her position clear privately. The festival's logic was to judge individuals, not nationality. Nastia has [her own page](/artists/nastia) on this site.

We found no policy statements about Russia from Resident Advisor itself, Beatport, Boiler Room or Bandcamp in the pages we could open, so we make no claim about them.

## The boycott debate: who argued what

The case for a boycott is spelled out in the March 2022 letter and repeated since. Monocle's reporting from Kyiv in June 2022 found signatories who felt let down by Russian peers' silence. Alisa Mullen, press officer for Kyiv nightlife organisations, called the boycott "an answer to ignorance" and said only about 1 per cent of Russian DJs had protested, which is her estimate, not a verified statistic. The Ukrainian activist Maya Baklanova has carried the argument to Western DJs who play in Russia, saying they normalise the war and help the Russian economy, in the Guardian and in Mixmag.

The counter-arguments in our sources are narrower than a defence of playing in Russia. Elena Natale, co-founder of Tallinn's Hall, told Monocle that shutting out Russians, especially those living abroad, goes too far, and that her club employs Estonians, Russians and Ukrainians. Awakenings' line about individuals is the same logic. We did not find a prominent DJ arguing on principle that artists should keep playing to Russian audiences. The artists who did play at Russian festivals, quoted below, defended it as apolitical or as a bridge between cultures, which is a different claim.

A related argument now plays out the other way round. When ADE 2026 removed the Russian DJ Alexander Komarov from a label showcase after an anti-Ukraine message, Ukraine's honorary consul for the Dutch dance scene said he did not want all Russian artists barred. See [our ADE 2026 wrap-up](/blog/ade-2026-wrap-up-30th-edition) for that case.

## What did the scene look like in 2023?

Reduced, then recovering. The Moscow Times reported in October 2023 that bars were packed, that techno festivals Sigma, Outline and Signal drew thousands, and that one anonymous organiser of a Moscow techno community estimated about 40 club events a weekend, roughly 1.5 to 2 times fewer than before the war. That is one organiser's estimate, not a statistic. The article's explanation is that many local artists left after February 2022 and many international acts boycotted, which opened room for Russian artists.

Snob's June 2023 look at the festival market gives the same picture from the business side. Foreign acts, it said, had almost disappeared from Russian lineups. The Stereoleto festival had two foreign headliners that year, about one eighth of five years earlier, and Chess & Jazz had previously drawn about 70 per cent of its artists from abroad. Many Russian artists had left, which shrank the pool and produced overlapping lineups. Festivals such as Picnic Afisha and Bol were named as gone from the map. Snob also noted that Signal still planned some headliners from Europe and South America.

Both articles are from outlets working under Russian wartime censorship rules or in exile, as noted in our method section below.

## Outline: the festival that came to stand for the argument

Outline is an electronic and art festival founded in 2014, cancelled by Moscow authorities hours before opening in 2016, and relaunched in 2023 near the Dubna river in the Taldom district, about 90 km north of Moscow. It is run by the team behind the club Mutabor, according to RA and Mixmag.

- **2023, 6 to 9 July.** RA reported international names including Atom(TM), Margaret Dygas and Le Syndicat Electronique, and described a tent with military recruitment posters near the entrance on day one and at least two cars marked with a "Z". The media partner was Lenta.ru. Mixmag reported that the Russian producer Pavel Milyakov, known as Buttechno, publicly criticised the festival and the artists who played. Organisers did not respond to RA.
- **2024, 18 to 22 July (the Guardian says to 24 July).** More than 100 artists, including Magda and Daniel Bell in the early announcements, alongside a four-day run of Russian bands on a separate stage. The Guardian reported that more than a dozen Western DJs played, and Mixmag and RTVI relayed the story. Only two are named in what we could read: Atom(TM) of Germany, who called the criticism a "smear campaign" and said he considers himself "trans-political", and the Brussels-based Alexandre Guisson, who said he had a good time and that people there were against the war. Official partners were T-Bank and Yandex; the Guardian characterised both as companies that have come under Kremlin influence, and two anonymous Russian music insiders told it that events of Outline's scale need government approval. Those are the Guardian's reporting and its sources' claims, not ours. No cancellations of any artists were reported.
- **2025, 8 to 14 July.** A new, larger site of about 40 hectares, nine stages and more than 150 artists from over 15 countries, according to Afisha Daily and Snob. Omar Souleyman headlined, with Romanian, Italian, Portuguese and Berlin-based electronic names billed. We found no post-event reporting, so we treat the 2025 bill as announced, not confirmed.
- **2026, planned 21 to 27 July.** Headliners billed included A Guy Called Gerald, DJ Pierre, Daniel Bell and Cezar, with single-day tickets from 7,500 to 49,000 roubles, per Afisha Daily. On the opening day the Taldom district administration told the organisers the festival could not proceed, citing a "threat of drone danger", according to RA and Meduza. The organisers said they had done everything they could and promised to return. No source says any foreign artist had arrived or played, so we do not say any did. RA noted the festival had mostly featured Russian artists. Start dates conflict across outlets (21 or 23 July), and Moscow's mayor had reported nearly 1,900 drones targeting Moscow in the previous week, as relayed by RA and Mixmag.

## Signal: from Nikola-Lenivets to Moscow and back

Signal is a festival at the art park Nikola-Lenivets in the Kaluga region, held since 2017 and founded by Sergei Fadeev, according to Snob. We found only one festival of that name in the Russian sources we opened.

- **2023.** Snob reported 60 per cent of a planned 20,000 tickets sold in early June.
- **2024, 15 to 19 August.** Eight stages, more than 250 artists and more than 20,000 guests, according to Snob.
- **2025.** Nine days before the planned start, the head of the Dzerzhinsky district said the festival could not be held because of a threat to participants' life and health, linking it to drones. The organisers said they received no formal ban, only the village administration calling it "inadvisable". Signal moved to Moscow as a five-venue programme, with DEX as the main hall, the Kristall factory, partner clubs including Gazgolder, and others. Fadeev told Snob attendance was about 7,000 against 20,000 expected, about 4,000 people were refunded, and the festival lost 95 per cent of its sponsors. He said 80 to 90 per cent of the international lineup was kept, with Atom(TM) and Kyoka as headliners and showcases from Nyege Nyege, PAN and others. One US act, Ducktails, was postponed.
- **2026.** The organisers announced a return to Nikola-Lenivets for 20 to 24 August 2026, after approvals held until the last moment, per Afisha Daily. We could not confirm whether it took place.

Between them, Outline and Signal show the new risk profile of a Russian festival. The threats cited are drone-related, the decisions are made by local administrations, and the pattern is that a large, advertised event can be stopped days or hours before it opens.

## Which clubs closed, and which are still open?

The closures below are documented with dates. Reasons are usually not given.

**Mutabor, then Arma, then Dex.** The Moscow techno club opened in April 2019 on the site of a former bearing plant, founded by the team behind Arma17, and the same team runs Outline. In the night of 20 to 21 December 2023 it hosted a closed "almost naked" party organised by the TV presenter Nastya Ivleeva, whose photos angered pro-government activists. On 10 January 2024 a Moscow court suspended the club for 90 days over sanitary violations after a visitor was reportedly poisoned by beer, in a closed hearing, according to Meduza. Ivleeva was fined 100,000 roubles, and 50,000 more in April 2024 over a March 2022 anti-war post, per Zona Media. The club reopened as Arma in March 2024. In the night of 30 November 2024 police raided it, along with Mono, Simach and Inferno Night, in what the Interior Ministry described as action against "LGBT propaganda"; dozens were detained, 12 Arma visitors got 15 days in jail and some men were served military enlistment summonses, per Novaya Gazeta Europe. On 21 January 2025 the venue announced it would close as a permanent location without giving a reason; the last event ran in late April 2025. The label Private Persons opened a new club, Dex, on the site in July 2025, with a darker, more industrial programme, according to The Blueprint.

**Gazgolder.** The club on Nizhny Susalny Lane opened in 2005 and was tied to Basta's Gaz label. It announced its closure on 26 November 2025, after 20 years, with farewell parties in December and January and no stated reason, per Moskvich Mag and The Blueprint.

**Propaganda.** One of Moscow's oldest clubs, open since 1997, announced it would close at the end of July 2024, with no reason given, per Snob. It had hosted artists such as Derrick May and Marcel Dettmann.

**Powerhouse** is reported by The Blueprint (July 2026) to have closed in spring 2026; we found only that source.

**Still open, per The Blueprint and Vedomosti.** Dex, Fabula HQ, RNDM, Nevrotik, NII Snegiri, M2 and Zheltyi Dom in Moscow, Studio 3, which opened in June 2026, and in St Petersburg Blank, Union, Kuznya and K-30. Vedomosti noted in May 2025 that Outline and Signal continue but once a year, and The Blueprint said new venues are appearing less and less often.

For the wider European picture, compare our [Europe club closures tracker](/blog/europe-club-closures-tracker) and the demand-side data in [why people go out less](/blog/why-people-go-out-less-nightlife-data-2026).

## How has state pressure shaped the culture?

The most documented change is on the queer and fetish end of the scene. On 30 November 2023 the Supreme Court declared an "international LGBT public movement" extremist, in a closed hearing without a defendant, though rights groups said the named organisation does not exist (Novaya Gazeta Europe). Within 48 hours, security forces searched a Moscow nightclub, a sauna and a bar hosting LGBTQ+ parties, reported AP, without naming venues. Novaya Gazeta Europe's February 2024 piece, headlined with a line about nobody wanting to be "the second Mutabor", quoted an organiser of the Kinky Party series saying its format had closed after a warning from state bodies and that authorities now go to venues rather than organisers. In July 2024 the organisers of Hunters Party said they would resume, with buyers required to confirm they are not part of the "international LGBT movement". The raids on Arma, Mono, Simach and Inferno Night on 30 November 2024 were followed on 6 and 7 December by raids on Sisters and Bizarre. In October 2025, according to Novaya Gazeta Europe, police raided a "kissing party" at the Graphite club in eastern Moscow.

Novaya Gazeta Europe's March 2025 piece described one surviving venue, called "Club X" in the text, that checks passports, displays a military recruitment poster, bans same-sex kissing and requires drag performers to present as male. That is one outlet's account of one venue, not a scene-wide rule.

Other pressures are more diffuse. Mobile internet shutdowns hit Moscow from 5 March 2026, officially for anti-drone security; AP's report does not mention nightlife, but Kommersant estimated Moscow businesses lost 3 to 5 billion roubles in five days. We found no sourced rules specific to alcohol licensing, foreign-agent bookings or drone-alert procedures for clubs, and we are not claiming any.

Our own [SHKEDUL listing](/blog/shkedul-moscow-rndm-24-october-2026) was built from the organisers' announcement and ticket page, with tickets from 1,500 roubles, and nothing in our sources reports how the night went.

Three things are not on that list on purpose. The Outline 2026 foreign names (A Guy Called Gerald, DJ Pierre, Derrick May and Daniel Bell among them) were billed for a festival that was cancelled on opening day, so nobody can say they performed. The Moscow Times reported in January 2026 that a small, growing group of second-tier US acts returned in 2025, including DaBaby, Tyga, Lil Pump, Akon, Xzibit and Gucci Mane, but it named no electronic acts. And we saw but could not open reports, including a paywalled trade piece about increased Russian touring and activist posts about named Western DJs, that we are not publishing because we could not read the underlying articles. Naming an artist as having played in Russia is a serious claim, and we hold it to a high standard.

On substitutes: we found no outlet reporting a systematic shift in Russian clubs to Turkish, Serbian, Armenian, Kazakh, Chinese or African DJs, so we do not state that as a trend. What the sources do show is a mix at festivals: Romanian, Italian and Berlin-based DJs, Omar Souleyman of Syria at Outline 2025, a Chinese media artist, and the Nyege Nyege and PAN showcases at Signal 2025.

## What did money and audiences do?

There is little club-specific data, so these figures are market-wide and should be read that way. MTS Live data reported by Kommersant FM put the average Russian concert ticket at almost 2,500 roubles in October 2024, up by nearly a third in two years, and Moscow above 2,800. An agency director quoted there said many musicians left, so those who stayed raised their fees. Kommersant reported in June 2026 that festival tickets were down 9 per cent in January to May compared with a year earlier, while Yandex Afisha's tickets sold rose 36 per cent. A May 2025 report on summer presales found Yandex Afisha counting 157 summer festivals, up 43 per cent, and Signal named among those growing audiences.

Outline's 2026 day tickets, from 7,500 to 49,000 roubles, are the only electronic-festival price we found, so there is no trend to state. We found nothing sourced on DJ fees, sound-system and equipment imports, Bandcamp or Beatport access, or the post-2022 generation of local DJs and labels. Spotify's announced exit is the only distribution fact we could confirm.

The Moscow Times piece said the absence of foreign acts opened room for Russian artists, and Outline's programme shows that in practice: its stage of Russian bands, including Khaski, Slava KPSS and TGK, and a returning cast of local DJs such as Denis Kaznacheev and Sofia Rodina. Signal in 2025 cut some domestic showcases for cost and logistics, so the trend is not one-way.

## What about the Ukrainian side, and Russians abroad?

Ukraine's clubs operated under very different constraints. Mixmag and RA reported in October 2022 that Closer and K41 in Kyiv reopened after more than six months, on daytime schedules because of an 11 pm to 5 am curfew, running on alternative power after Russian strikes. Foreign DJs travelled for the first time since February, a journey of about 28 hours one way, per RA. We could not find current 2025 and 2026 information on Cxema, Techno Kyiv or Brave! Factory in the sources we opened.

Russians who left built their own circuits, though what we could verify concerns indie and rock more than club music. In Belgrade, a promoter told Kosovo 2.0 in February 2023 that Russian-artist concerts happened most weeks, and Novaya Gazeta Europe reported ticket prices from 2 to 3 euros for underground shows up to 40 to 120 euros for stars. In Tbilisi, Radio Svoboda reported that a musician's pay per Russian show fell from about 200,000 roubles to about 3,000, in his own figure, and that Bassiani had turned away some Russians after passport checks, per Monocle. We found no usable sources on club promoters in Almaty, Istanbul, Dubai or Berlin.

Russian artists abroad have faced consequences too, with named cases being Kraviz in 2022 and Komarov at ADE in October 2026. The Bell reported in 2024 on exiled Russian anti-war performers hit by cancellations and entry bans, though none were DJs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Western DJs still playing in Russia?

Some are, but the confirmed cases are few. Atom(TM) and Alexandre Guisson are the two DJs named as playing Outline 2024 in reporting we could read, and the Guardian said more than a dozen Western DJs played. Organisers' partner posts list several dance-pop names at Portal 2030-2050 in September 2025. We found no confirmed Western club-night booking for 2025 or 2026, and SHKEDUL's 24 October 2026 date at RNDM in Moscow is billed by organisers as his first visit.

### What happened to Outline festival in 2026?

It was cancelled on its opening day, 21 July 2026, when the Taldom district administration told the organisers it could not go ahead, citing a "threat of drone danger", according to Resident Advisor citing Meduza. The festival had been planned for 21 to 27 July with more than 100 artists. The organisers said they had done everything they could and promised to return. No source reports that any foreign artist played.

### What happened to Mutabor in Moscow?

After the "almost naked" party of 20 to 21 December 2023, a court closed the club for 90 days over sanitary violations in January 2024. It reopened as Arma in March 2024, was raided by police on 30 November 2024, and announced on 21 January 2025 that it would close as a permanent venue, with its last party in late April 2025. A new club, Dex, opened on the site in July 2025.

### What is Signal festival and did it go ahead?

Signal is an electronic music and art festival at Nikola-Lenivets in the Kaluga region, founded by Sergei Fadeev. In 2025 the local authorities cited drones and it moved to Moscow with about 7,000 guests. The organisers announced a return to Nikola-Lenivets for 20 to 24 August 2026, but we could not confirm it took place.

### Which Russian clubs have closed since 2022?

Propaganda (end of July 2024), Mutabor and its successor Arma (April 2025), Gazgolder (announced 26 November 2025, with farewell parties through December and January) and, reportedly, Powerhouse (spring 2026). Reasons were generally not given. Dex, Fabula HQ, RNDM, Studio 3 and others remain open.

### Did Western artists cancel Russian shows in 2022?

Yes, broadly. The Guardian wrote in 2024 that most Western musicians and artists have cancelled concerts in Russia since 2022, without naming electronic acts. In electronic music the best-documented cases were festivals dropping Nina Kraviz in May 2022 and Nastia leaving Awakenings in June 2022. We did not find a named Western DJ's public refusal of a Russian booking.

### Why do some artists criticise Western DJs who play Outline?

The Ukrainian activist Maya Baklanova says playing in Russia normalises the war and helps the Russian economy and military budget. The Guardian also reported that Outline's partners included T-Bank and Yandex and that insiders said events of its scale need government approval. The artists named say they are apolitical or see music as a way of connecting people.

### What has changed for LGBTQ+ clubs in Russia?

After the Supreme Court's 30 November 2023 ruling declaring an "international LGBT public movement" extremist, security forces raided venues, including Arma, Mono, Simach and Inferno Night on 30 November 2024, and Sisters and Bizarre a week later, according to Novaya Gazeta Europe. Several parties changed format or stopped, and Inferno closed.
