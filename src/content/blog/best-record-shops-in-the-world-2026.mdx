As of 18 November 2026, this unranked guide covers 24 record shops across six regions, from Hard Wax in Berlin to Red Eye in Sydney. We drew them from The Vinyl Factory's 2016 list, the Financial Times picks of January 2025 and our own checks. Several have moved since 2016, so each entry says what we could confirm, and when.

## What are the best record shops in the world?

As of 18 November 2026, there is no official ranking of the world's best record shops, so this guide builds an unranked shortlist from repeated mentions and our own source checks. Hard Wax in Berlin (1989), Rush Hour in Amsterdam, Discos Paradiso in Barcelona and London's Rough Trade (1976), Phonica (2003) and Honest Jon's (1974) anchor the European picks. Piccadilly in Manchester, Rubadub in Glasgow and Idle Hands in Bristol add British dance and indie strength. In the United States the guide covers Amoeba Music (1990), Dusty Groove, Waterloo Records, Electric Fetus, Third Man Records and The Thing. Disk Union (1967) and Tower Records Shibuya represent Tokyo, Red Eye Records in Sydney (1981) and Real Groovy in Auckland (1981) represent Oceania, and Rockers International in Kingston, Mabu Vinyl in Cape Town and Casarão do Vinil in São Paulo complete the list. Each entry says what we could confirm and from which source.

This is not a ranking. The table is grouped by region and the order inside each region is ours, not a score. "VF 2016" means The Vinyl Factory's list of 50 shops, and "FT via Time Out" means the January 2025 Financial Times selection as summarised by Time Out.

## How did we choose these record shops?

We used four tests. A shop had to be named by at least one published list or reputable press source, to have a verifiable founding story or specialism, to add a region or scene the others do not, and to leave a trail we could check against an official site, Wikipedia or recent press.

We did not rank, because a basement thrift shop in Brooklyn and a nine-floor Tokyo flagship are not competing for the same prize. The word "best" appears only when attributed, as in "named by".

The harder test was currency. The Vinyl Factory list is a decade old, and several of its addresses have changed. Where we found a 2025 or 2026 source we say so, and where we did not we write "as of the source date". For the dance-music end of the spectrum, our [guide to the best techno and electronic record shops](/blog/best-techno-and-electronic-record-shops-2026) goes deeper.

## What do the Vinyl Factory and Financial Times lists say?

The Vinyl Factory's unranked list of 50 was published on 24 December 2016. It describes itself as the first fifty shops of a series, and it spans Berlin, Kingston, Beirut, New Delhi and Cape Town. It includes Hard Wax, Rough Trade West, Dusty Groove, Disk Union, Rockers International, Amoeba Music, Phonica, Rush Hour, Honest Jon's, Piccadilly Records, Idle Hands and Rubadub.

The Financial Times list appeared in January 2025. The paywalled original did not open for us, so we rely on Time Out's coverage, which says the FT asked its own writers and editors to name their favourites. The FT list, as summarised by Time Out, named Red Eye Records in Sydney, Dizonord in Paris, Andra Jazz in Stockholm, Ooh Aah in Copenhagen and Barcelona City Records. It also named Jazz Record Center and Amoeba Music (the Berkeley branch) in the US. Time Out UK counted 18 British shops, from Sounds of the Universe in London to Cob Records in Porthmadog.

Five names appear on both lists, sometimes through a different branch:

- Rough Trade: Rough Trade West in the VF list, Denmark Street in the FT list.
- Honest Jon's in London.
- Amoeba Music: Los Angeles in the VF list, Berkeley in the FT list.
- Red Eye Records in Sydney.
- The Thing in Brooklyn.

The differences are as telling. The VF list reaches far outside Europe and North America, while the FT selection leans British and favours small specialists. We treat both as starting points, not verdicts.

## Which record stores are the most famous?

Fame is not measurable here, so we describe it through repeated mentions. Rough Trade, Honest Jon's, Amoeba and Red Eye surface in both major lists we read.

Tower Records is the odd case. The US chain filed for bankruptcy in 2006 and its last US stores closed on 22 December 2006, according to Wikipedia. Tower Records Japan had become independent in October 2002 through a management buyout, which is why the Shibuya flagship below still trades.

## Which record shops are the best in each region?

No source we read can answer that with one name, so here are 24 that keep appearing in credible lists, grouped by region.

### Which UK record shops are famous?

**Rough Trade, London.** Geoff Travis opened the first shop on 23 February 1976 at 202 Kensington Park Road, Ladbroke Grove, according to Wikipedia. The Talbot Road shop, Rough Trade West, has traded at 130 Talbot Road since 1982. Wikipedia also lists Brick Lane (2007), Denmark Street (October 2024), Nottingham, Bristol and Liverpool. The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile says 1978, which conflicts with Wikipedia's exact date, so we use 1976.

**Phonica, London.** Phonica opened at 51 Poland Street, Soho, in 2003, a time when The Vinyl Factory says Soho record shops were closing. Manager Simon Rigg's "broad church" approach covers rare soul 7 inches through to techno 12 inches, according to VF in 2016 and 2017. We found no 2025 or 2026 source we could open, so treat its status as of the source date.

**Honest Jon's, London.** John Clare began trading jazz records from a former butcher's on Golbourne Road in 1974, according to The Vinyl Factory, and the shop moved to Portobello Road in the 1980s. Its own site now lists two shops, at 278 Portobello Road and Coal Drops Yard, with opening hours. It was also named in the FT list, as summarised by Time Out. For the city around it, see our [London clubs guide](/blog/best-london-clubs-2026-guide).

**Piccadilly Records, Manchester.** The Vinyl Factory describes Piccadilly as founded in the same year as Factory Records, 1978, and located at 53 Oldham Street in the Northern Quarter. The shop's own site still sells merchandise marked "Classic Since 1978". Its stock leans to new music and reissues, as of the source date.

**Rubadub, Glasgow.** The Vinyl Factory dates Rubadub to August 1992 and puts it at 35 Howard Street. It started as a dance music shop, then added DJ gear, studio equipment and a distribution arm. We could not open an official page for 2026, so this is as of the source date.

**Idle Hands, Bristol.** Chris Farrell opened Idle Hands in February 2011 on Stokes Croft, according to The Vinyl Factory. Mixmag reported on 18 November 2024 that it was reopening on 20 November in a central Bristol site after closing in 2022, with over £12,000 raised by supporters. We did not open a 2025 or 2026 confirmation of its trading hours.

### Which European record shops are the best known?

**Hard Wax, Berlin.** Hard Wax was founded in 1989, according to Wikipedia, which credits Mark Ernestus of Basic Channel. The Vinyl Factory adds Moritz von Oswald as co-founder. Its 2016 address is out of date: Wikipedia records a 2023 move to Kraftwerk Berlin, and the shop's own info page lists Köpenicker Straße 70 with Monday to Saturday hours of 15:00 to 20:00. See our [Berlin clubs guide](/blog/best-berlin-clubs-2026-guide) and the [Ostgut Ton history](/blog/ostgut-ton-berghain-label-history) for the wider scene.

**Rush Hour, Amsterdam.** The Vinyl Factory says Antal Heitlager and Christiaan Macdonald started in 1996 by buying the basement stock of Coco Records, then launched a mail-order business. I amsterdam, the city's tourism site, gives 1997 as the start of the label, so we say 1996 or 1997. I amsterdam lists the shop at Spuistraat 116, where The Vinyl Factory says it moved in 2016. See our [Amsterdam clubs guide](/blog/best-amsterdam-clubs-2026-guide) for the nightlife that grew around it.

**Discos Paradiso, Barcelona.** Discos Paradiso opened in April 2010 in the Raval, at Carrer de Ferlandina 39, according to The Vinyl Factory. The same profile quotes Sónar calling it the epicentre of electronica in Barcelona. Status is as of the source date.

### Which North American record stores are worth the trip?

**Amoeba Music, USA.** Amoeba was founded in 1990 in Berkeley by former Rasputin Records staff, according to Wikipedia. San Francisco opened on 15 November 1997 in a former 24,000 square foot bowling alley, and the Hollywood store reopened on 1 April 2021 at Hollywood Boulevard and Argyle Avenue after the old Sunset Boulevard site closed in 2020. Time Out, summarising the FT, put the Berkeley shop at 2455 Telegraph Avenue.

**Dusty Groove, Chicago.** Rick Wojcik and JP Schauer started Dusty Groove as an online shop in April 1996, according to Wikipedia. The storefront opened daily in 2001, and a reissue label followed in 2007. The shop sits at 1120 N Ashland Avenue, according to The Vinyl Factory, and it specialises in jazz, funk, soul, Latin and Brazilian records.

**Waterloo Records, Austin.** Louis Karp opened Waterloo in 1982, according to KUT. KUT reported on 2 January 2025 that it would move five blocks north under new owners Caren Kelleher and Trey Watson. The shop's own contact page now lists 1105 North Lamar, and a KWBU report of 11 August 2026 quotes Kelleher on strong sales and foot traffic since the move. This is one shop where The Vinyl Factory's 2016 address is no longer right.

**Electric Fetus, Minneapolis.** The Vinyl Factory says Electric Fetus was founded in 1968 and sits at 2000 4th Avenue South. It estimates 50,000 records at the Minneapolis shop and notes Prince shopped there days before his death. We found no current source we could open, so this is as of the source date.

**Third Man Records, Nashville, Detroit and London.** Jack White's label was founded in 2001. Wikipedia lists Nashville at 623 7th Avenue South, Detroit from November 2015 and London in Soho since September 2021. The Detroit shop's own page lists 441 W Canfield Street and Third Man Pressing in the Cass Corridor. For the city's wider history, read our [Detroit techno guide](/blog/detroit-techno-guide-2026-clubs-labels-history).

**The Thing, Brooklyn.** Named in both lists, The Thing is a basement thrift shop at 1001 Manhattan Avenue. The Vinyl Factory said in 2016 that it was marking its 20th anniversary, and Time Out says the FT's view is that visitors should expect crates rather than alphabetised genres.

**Jazz Record Center, New York.** Time Out says the FT named this shop at 236 W 26th Street, reached by elevator to the eighth floor, for new and second-hand jazz. We could not verify when it opened.

### Which record shops in Japan and Asia should be on a bucket list?

**Disk Union, Tokyo.** The Vinyl Factory says Disk Union was established in 1967 and calls it a sprawling network, including the Shibuya store in the Antena21 building, 30-7 Udagawacho. The "hundreds of thousands" of records it mentions is an estimate from 2016, not a verified count.

**Tower Records Shibuya, Tokyo.** A Tower Records Japan press release of 25 November 2025 gives the address as 1-22-14 Jinnan, the opening as March 1995 and the sales floor as about 5,100 square metres. The sixth-floor vinyl section already held more than 100,000 titles, with a grand reopening after renovation set for 28 February 2026. Time Out Tokyo reported in February 2024 that 30,000 records were being added. We found no source we could open that confirms the finished 2026 layout.

**ZudRangMa Records, Bangkok.** The Vinyl Factory calls it the Bangkok affiliate of the Paradise Bangkok label, run by DJ Maft Sai, and says it stocks Thai luk thung and molam records. Its 2026 status is unverified.

### Which record shops stand out in Oceania?

**Red Eye Records, Sydney.** Red Eye is at 143 York Street in the CBD, according to Wikipedia, which dates it to 1981. Time Out's FT summary says it has sold vinyl since 1981. Wikipedia lists ARIA Awards for outstanding independent retailer in 2012, 2013 and 2015.

**Real Groovy, Auckland.** Real Groovy's own site gives 1981 as the year it opened on Mt Eden Road, and lists its moves: 438 Queen Street in 1991, 369 Queen Street in 2016, 520 Queen Street in 2019 and 19 Victoria Street West in 2023. The Spinoff reported in January 2023 that owner Chris Hart expected lower rent and more foot traffic. The 2016 address in The Vinyl Factory's list is therefore out of date.

### Which record shops stand out in Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean?

**Rockers International, Kingston.** The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile traces Rockers to Augustus Pablo, who launched his label and shop in the 1970s at 135 Orange Street. It says Rockers and Randy's were then the last vinyl shops in downtown Kingston, and that manager Mitchie Williams was refurbishing the building. We could not open a 2025 or 2026 source, so status is as of the source date.

**Mabu Vinyl, Cape Town.** The Vinyl Factory says Jacques Vosloo established Mabu in 2001 and co-owns it with Stephen Segerman, who appears in the film *Searching for Sugar Man*. It lists 2 Rheede Street, but we saw signs the shop has moved since and could not confirm a current address.

**Casarão do Vinil, São Paulo.** The Vinyl Factory says it opened to the public in 2014 at Rua dos Trilhos 1212 in Mooca, in a house where each room is sorted by price. The same profile says the owner sold about 750,000 LPs to collector Zero Freitas, which we could not confirm elsewhere. Status is as of the source date.

## Why do record shops matter in 2026?

Demand is real. Music Week reported on 16 March 2026 that US vinyl sales passed $1 billion in 2025, up 9.3%, with 46.8 million units sold and a 19th consecutive year of growth. Our pieces on [vinyl's best year since 1983](/blog/vinyl-sales-record-2025) and the [pressing plant capacity crisis](/blog/vinyl-pressing-plant-capacity-crisis) show where that demand meets a shortage.

A shop is also where a collection changes hands, as in our report on the [JD Twitch collection sale](/blog/jd-twitch-optimo-record-collection-sale-world-of-echo). For labels that depend on shop orders, see our [independent label survival study](/blog/independent-label-survival-study) and the history of [Kompakt](/blog/kompakt-records-cologne-history).

## What we could not verify

Plenty did not make the cut, or came with caveats.

- **The FT list itself.** It is paywalled, so we only repeat what Time Out reported. We could not confirm the full list or its size, and Time Out does not give a total.
- **Shops named but not profiled.** Dizonord, Andra Jazz, Ooh Aah, Barcelona City Records and Superfly in Paris appeared in coverage we read or searched, but we found no source we could open for details.
- **New Gramophone House, New Delhi.** The Vinyl Factory describes a shop established in Lahore in 1930 and moved after the 1947 Partition. We found no 2025 or 2026 source, so we left it out.
- **Official sites that failed.** Amoeba, Dusty Groove, Disk Union, Phonica, Rubadub and Rough Trade's store page returned errors or redirects. Rush Hour's site loaded but showed no address.
- **Reddit.** A thread on shop visits was blocked, so we do not cite it.
- **Status of older entries.** Rubadub, Electric Fetus, Rockers, Mabu, Casarão and ZudRangMa rest on 2016 sources unless stated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most famous record store in the world?

No source we read ranks fame, so we cannot name one. Rough Trade, Honest Jon's, Amoeba and Red Eye appear in both The Vinyl Factory and Financial Times lists as we read them, which makes them the strongest candidates by repeated mention.

### What is the largest record store in the world?

We could not verify a global record. Tower Records Shibuya lists a sales floor of about 5,100 square metres, according to a Tower Records Japan release, and Wikipedia describes it as nine floors. The Vinyl Factory calls Amoeba Hollywood "quite possibly" the largest entertainment store in the US, which is a hedge, not a measurement.

### What is the oldest record shop in the world?

We could not verify an oldest. Among shops in this guide, Disk Union dates to 1967 and Electric Fetus to 1968, both per The Vinyl Factory. That same list describes New Gramophone House as established in Lahore in 1930, but we found no current source to confirm it is trading.

### What are the best record shops for techno?

Hard Wax in Berlin, Rubadub in Glasgow, Rush Hour in Amsterdam, Phonica in London and Discos Paradiso in Barcelona are the dance-focused names in the lists we read. See our [techno and electronic record shop guide](/blog/best-techno-and-electronic-record-shops-2026) for the full breakdown.

### How many record shops are left?

We have no global count. The Record Store Day UK site describes a celebration involving 314 independent record shops across the UK and Ireland, which is the number of participants, not the total number of shops.

### Are vinyl sales still growing?

In the US, yes in 2025. Music Week reported on 16 March 2026 that US vinyl sales passed $1 billion, up 9.3%, with 46.8 million units and a 19th consecutive year of growth. We did not open a figure for 2026 so far.

### Which record shops have moved or closed since the 2016 Vinyl Factory list?

Hard Wax moved to Kraftwerk Berlin in 2023, Real Groovy to 19 Victoria Street West in 2023, and Waterloo Records, announced in January 2025, to 1105 North Lamar. Idle Hands closed in 2022 and reopened in November 2024. Amoeba's Sunset Boulevard store closed in 2020 before the Hollywood Boulevard shop opened in 2021.

### Does Tower Records still exist?

In Japan, yes. Tower Records Japan became independent of the US company in October 2002, according to Wikipedia, and its Shibuya store opened at its current address in March 1995. The US chain's last stores closed on 22 December 2006.
