As of 23 November 2026, the second annual Deezer and Ipsos survey says 80% of respondents want AI music clearly labelled, 60% say fully AI-generated songs should stay out of the main charts, and 72% of streaming users want to be told when a service recommends them. The 80% headline is unchanged from 2025.

## What did the 2026 Deezer and Ipsos survey find?

The second annual Deezer and Ipsos survey, published by Deezer on 17 November 2026, asked 9,000 adults in eight countries about AI-generated music. Eighty percent said both fully AI-generated and AI-assisted music should be clearly labelled. Sixty percent said fully AI-generated songs should not appear in the main charts beside human-made ones, up eight points on 2025, and only 7% wanted them treated equally. Of streaming users, 72% want to know when a service recommends fully AI-generated music, and 74% believed their own playlists contained none. Deezer's own subscriber data says otherwise: about 57% of more than 53,000 people who joined in October already had at least one such track in their playlists. In the United States, only 37% now see AI as a valuable tool for musicians, down from 54% in 2025. Deezer commissioned the work.

### How was the survey run?

Ipsos surveyed 9,000 adults online in the United States, Canada, Brazil, the United Kingdom, France, the Netherlands, Germany and Japan, according to [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/80-of-people-want-ai-music-labeled-says-deezer-ipsos-survey-as-37-of-americans-now-see-ai-as-a-valuable-tool-for-musicians-down-from-54-in-2025/). Fieldwork ran from 9 to 14 October 2026, with 1,000 respondents per market and 2,000 in the US. Of the total, 6,672 were music streaming users, and the streaming questions apply only to them.

[Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/digital/read/deezer-ipsos-survey-reveals-1-in-2-users-now-have-ai-music-in-their-playlists/095505) and [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/?p=109137) both published their reports on 18 November. DJ Mag also covered the survey, but its site blocked our fetcher, so we know that coverage only through search summaries and do not rely on it for any figure.

## How does the 2026 survey differ from 2025?

The 2026 edition repeats most of the 2025 questions but changes the sample, adds a new labelling question and drops the headline-grabbing blind test from the coverage we read. Deezer's [November 2025 release](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2025/11/deezer-ipsos-survey-ai-music/) reported 9,000 adults aged 18 to 65, surveyed from 6 to 10 October 2025, with 6,791 streaming users. The 2026 sample covers all adults aged 18 and over.

The 2025 edition led with a different number: 97% of respondents failed to pick the human-made track in a blind test with two AI songs and one human one. The 80% labelling figure was in that release too, which is why the headline reads the same a year later.

### What changed in the United States?

The sharpest movement is in the US, where Music Business Worldwide reports falls on almost every attitude question between the 2025 and 2026 waves:

- **AI as a valuable tool for musicians, artists and composers:** 37%, down from 54%, the biggest drop of any general-attitude question in the US.
- **AI will play a significant role in music creation over the next 10 years:** 51%, down from 62%.
- **Fully AI-generated music will become popular:** 41%, down from 60%.
- **Have listened to fully AI-generated music:** 45%, down from 52%, and the only market of the eight to fall.
- **Fear of lost creativity:** 69%, up from 65%.

Other markets moved less sharply. Belief that fully AI-generated music will become popular fell from 46% to 33% in Germany and from 49% to 37% in the UK. Brazil is the outlier: 63% there say they have listened to fully AI-generated music, up from 46%, and 61% see AI as a valuable creative tool, against 35% globally.

### What stayed the same?

Demand for disclosure barely moved. The 80% labelling figure is identical, and the share of streaming users who want to know about AI recommendations went from 73% in 2025 to 72% in 2026. Treat those as stable, not as a trend. France sits above the global figure: 88% want fully AI-generated music always labelled and 90% want the same for AI-assisted music, according to Music Business Worldwide.

## How many people want AI music labelled?

Eighty percent of 2026 respondents agree that fully AI-generated music should be clearly labelled, and 80% say the same about AI-assisted music. The second question is new, so there is no 2025 comparison, and the survey does not say where respondents draw the line between "assisted" and "generated".

One caution on reading other coverage. A DJ Mag summary we saw in search results gave 88% and 90% for the labelling questions. Music Business Worldwide and Deezer's release attribute those two figures to France, so we treat 80% as the global number.

Labelling is also the one question where the survey and platform practice already overlap. Respondents want to be told, and several services have started to tell them, as the platform section below sets out.

## Should AI-generated songs be in the charts?

Sixty percent of 2026 respondents say fully AI-generated songs should not be in the main charts alongside human-made music, up eight points from 52% in 2025. Only 7% say they should be treated like any other chart entry, down from 11%, according to Music Business Worldwide and RouteNote.

That is opinion, not policy. We have not seen a major chart body in the coverage we read respond to the survey, and the survey does not ask what should happen to AI tracks that already chart. The cases that prompted the debate are in our [report on Spotify's AI Persona badge](/blog/spotify-ai-persona-badge-ifpi-fraud), which covers two AI acts that built real audiences through playlists and chart mechanics.

## What does Deezer's own data say about AI uploads?

Deezer's platform numbers sit beside the survey and are not part of it. The 2026 coverage repeats figures Deezer first gave in July: nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks received a day, more than half of daily deliveries on peak days, but only 1% to 3% of streams. Deezer also says up to 85% of streams on fully AI-generated tracks in 2025 were fraudulent and excludes manipulated streams from royalty payments.

The 2025 release had lower figures: roughly 50,000 fully AI-generated tracks a day, about 34% of daily deliveries, about 0.5% of streams and up to 70% of those streams fraudulent. Those are different dates and different readings, not a contradiction. Our [earlier Deezer report](/blog/deezer-ai-uploads-streaming-flood) traces the climb from 44% of uploads in April 2026 to more than half in July.

Deezer announced on 13 October 2026, according to [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/deezer-to-let-users-block-ai-generated-music-from-search-results/), a toggle that hides fully AI-generated tracks, albums and related artists from search, due in every market before the end of 2026. The survey's filter question, 45% in 2025 and 41% in 2026, is the demand side of that product. Deezer also says its detection tool can produce false positives, as Music Business Worldwide notes.

## Which platforms already label or ban AI music?

The platforms differ on whether they remove, label or demonetise AI music. This table covers the services we have sources for, with dates as reported.

| Platform | What it does | Date | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deezer | Detects fully AI-generated tracks, excludes them from recommendations and editorial playlists, demonetises fraudulent streams; search toggle announced | 2025 onward; toggle 13 October 2026 | Music Business Worldwide, our Deezer report |
| Spotify | AI Persona badge on artist profiles that are synthetic identities | Announced 12 August 2026 | Our Spotify report |
| Bandcamp | Bans music generated wholly or in substantial part by AI | January 2026 | Our Bandcamp report |
| Beatport | Blocks fully or majority AI-generated tracks at ingestion, tags AI-assisted tracks | 12 August 2026 | Beatportal |
| Apple Music | Transparency Tags for artwork, track, composition and music video, applied by labels and distributors | 4 March 2026 | Music Business Worldwide |
| YouTube Music | Labels on the player page for tracks made primarily with generative AI, using partner information and its own detection | 14 October 2026 | YouTube blog |
| Tidal | Removes and refuses fully AI-generated tracks; before that, labelled them and paid no royalties on them | Ban from 9 December 2026, announced 24 November; labels from 15 July 2026 | [Our TIDAL ban report](/blog/tidal-ai-music-ban-december-2026-deezer-survey) |

**Update, 26 November 2026:** on 24 November, after this report was first published, TIDAL announced that it will [remove fully AI-generated tracks from 9 December](/blog/tidal-ai-music-ban-december-2026-deezer-survey).

The approaches split three ways. Bandcamp, Beatport and, from 9 December, Tidal keep out what they judge to be fully or mostly AI. Deezer and YouTube Music detect and label or demote. Apple Music relies on declarations: its tags are currently optional, and Music Business Worldwide reports that labels and distributors will be required to use them when delivering new content in future. Apple leaves it to content providers to decide what counts as AI-generated.

Beatport's rule matters most to dance music. [Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1571333-beatport-expands-beatdapp-partnership-to-detect-ai-generated-music) reports that its updated guidelines say fully or majority AI-generated tracks "will not be permitted on the platform", with Beatdapp's technology used to separate them from AI-assisted tracks. It also cited its own user survey, in which 60% said they would not play AI-generated tracks in their sets. Beatport gave no sample size or method for that survey, so it cannot be compared with Deezer's.

## What does it mean for DJs and labels?

A DJ cannot play what they cannot identify, and the 2025 blind test suggests listeners often cannot identify it either. Labelling therefore moves the burden from the crowd to the supply chain: distributors, labels and platforms would have to declare how a record was made before it reaches a DJ's crate. For a DJ who buys on Beatport or Bandcamp, that is already partly true, since both screen at the door. For one who digs on a streaming service, the labels in the table above are patchy.

For labels the practical issue is what a tag will mean. Apple, YouTube and Beatport all separate "fully" or "primarily" AI-generated from "assisted", and the survey's 80% on AI-assisted music suggests many listeners want that line shown as well. Where the line falls is the whole argument. Todd Edwards says he samples and chops Suno output, and [our report on his comments](/blog/todd-edwards-ai-sampling-producers-split) shows why that sits on the wrong side of a simple label. [Charlotte de Witte's Azadi EP](/blog/charlotte-de-witte-azadi-ep-ai-vocalist-nava), with its AI singer NAVA, tests the same question on a release by [one of techno's biggest names](/artists/charlotte-de-witte): our report found no platform had said how its rules apply.

Independent labels have the most to gain from clear disclosure and the most to lose from a crude one. Our [independent label survival study](/blog/independent-label-survival-study) shows how thin their margins already are, and a false AI flag, as [Bandcamp's six-month record](/blog/bandcamp-ai-ban-six-months-on) shows, can pull a catalogue. The sensible response is the one we gave then: keep stems, session files and notes, and credit AI use openly. The topic also ran through the conference programme at ADE, as our [ADE 2026 wrap-up](/blog/ade-2026-wrap-up-30th-edition) records.

## What we could not verify

- **Deezer's own 2026 release.** We reached Deezer's November 2026 newsroom archive page, which summarises the release, but not a standalone press release page, and the full scorecards PDF was too large for our fetcher. Figures come from that summary and from Music Business Worldwide, Music Week and RouteNote.
- **DJ Mag.** Its article could not be opened, so we do not use it for any figure.
- **A 2026 blind test.** The 97% result belongs to the 2025 edition. The 2026 coverage we read does not mention a repeat.
- **Comparable wording.** Ipsos says the 2026 filter question is not comparable with 2025 because new answer options were added. The sample also widened from ages 18 to 65 to all adults.
- **Missing 2025 numbers.** Music Business Worldwide says fear of lost creativity is up three points to 67%, but we did not open a 2025 figure for it, so we omit one.
- **Commissioner and method.** Deezer commissioned the survey, sells AI detection technology and is launching an AI filter, so it has a commercial interest in the topic. The sample is online, so it excludes people without internet access, and results are self-reported opinion.
- **Beatport's survey.** The 60% figure comes from Beatport's own survey with no published sample size.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Deezer and Ipsos AI music survey?

It is an annual global survey commissioned by Deezer and run by Ipsos on listeners' attitudes to AI-generated music. The second edition, published on 17 November 2026, covers 9,000 adults in eight countries. The first was published on 12 November 2025.

### How many people want AI music labelled?

Eighty percent of 2026 respondents say fully AI-generated music should be clearly labelled, and 80% say the same for AI-assisted music. The 2025 edition also found 80% for fully AI-generated music, so the headline did not change. France is higher, at 88% and 90%.

### Do people want AI songs in the charts?

Most do not. Sixty percent of 2026 respondents say fully AI-generated songs should not be in the main charts alongside human-made ones, up from 52% in 2025. Only 7% say they should be treated equally, down from 11%.

### Is the 72% figure from 2025 or 2026?

Both editions asked it, with similar results. In 2026, 72% of streaming users said they want to know when a service recommends fully AI-generated music, according to Music Business Worldwide. In 2025 the figure was 73%, according to Deezer's release.

### Did US views of AI in music really fall?

Respondents' views did. Music Business Worldwide reports that 37% of US respondents see AI as a valuable tool for musicians, down from 54% in 2025, and belief that fully AI-generated music will become popular fell from 60% to 41%. It is a survey result, not a measurement of AI music itself.

### How much AI music does Deezer receive?

Deezer says it receives nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks a day, more than half of daily deliveries on peak days, but 1% to 3% of streams. In November 2025 it reported roughly 50,000 a day, about 34% of deliveries. These are Deezer's platform figures, not survey results.

### Does Beatport ban AI music?

Beatport's updated guidelines say fully or majority AI-generated tracks will not be permitted. AI-assisted tracks are accepted if the finished song is still mostly human-made, and they are tagged at ingestion. Beatportal reported the change on 12 August 2026.

### Which platforms label AI music?

Deezer and YouTube Music label or flag tracks they identify as AI-generated, Spotify badges AI artist personas, and Apple Music offers Transparency Tags that labels and distributors apply. Bandcamp and Beatport remove or block it instead, and Tidal removes fully AI-generated tracks from 9 December 2026. Practices differ on whether the label comes from the platform or the rightsholder.

### Can we trust a survey commissioned by Deezer?

Treat it as respondents' opinion, not as an independent measurement. Ipsos ran the fieldwork, but Deezer commissioned it and has products tied to the findings, including AI detection and a planned filter. The 2026 sample also differs from 2025, so some year-on-year changes may partly reflect method.

### What should DJs and labels do now?

Check each platform's rules before you release or buy, and disclose any AI element in credits. Keep stems and session files in case a flag needs answering. A label that states how a record was made is better placed than one a platform's detector has to guess about.
