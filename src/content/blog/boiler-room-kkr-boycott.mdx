Boiler Room spent over a decade building its reputation as a scrappy, artist-first broadcaster streaming sets out of warehouses and basements. That reputation is now colliding with who actually signs off on the company's decisions. In January 2025, the platform was acquired by Superstruct Entertainment, the pan-European festival conglomerate that is itself majority-owned by KKR, the American private equity giant. Eighteen months later, that ownership chain has produced a founder's exit, a canceled Dublin announcement, and street protests outside Boiler Room's own Brooklyn events. This is what actually happened, in order, with sources.

## Who bought Boiler Room, and why does it matter?

Boiler Room was sold by ticketing company DICE, which had owned it since 2021, to Superstruct Entertainment in January 2025, as first reported by [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/boiler-room-acquired-festival-giant-superstruct-entertainment). Superstruct is not a niche buyer. It's a roll-up operator that owns roughly 80 festivals across Europe, including Sónar in Barcelona, DGTL in Amsterdam, Wacken Open Air and Parookaville in Germany, Sziget in Budapest, Lost Village, Boardmasters, Kendal Calling, Tramlines, Field Day, and Mighty Hoopla, among many others, per reporting from [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/sonar-and-dgtl-festivals-issue-statements-concerning-kkr-and-superstruct-entertainment). KKR bought Superstruct in a deal reported at roughly $1.3 billion in 2024, folding one of dance music's largest festival portfolios, and now its most-watched broadcast platform, into a private equity firm's balance sheet.

That matters because KKR's investment book extends well beyond festivals. Critics, including the [BDS movement](https://bdsmovement.net/) and Palestinian solidarity organizers, have pointed to KKR's stakes in Israeli data infrastructure and technology firms and its ties to defense-linked companies such as German arms supplier HENSOLDT. None of that is about Boiler Room's programming directly. It's about what fees, ticket sales, and ad revenue from Boiler Room events ultimately flow toward once they clear the platform's own books.

## What did Boiler Room actually say about the sale?

On March 25, 2025, Boiler Room posted a statement on Instagram addressing the acquisition directly. The core of it, as documented by [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/boiler-room-issues-statement-concerning-acquisition-superstruct-entertainment-and-kkr), was an admission paired with a defense. The admission: "No Boiler Room staff at any level held any ownership or voting rights in the company and had no control over the sale." The defense: the platform reaffirmed itself as "unapologetically pro-Palestine," pointed to its history of BDS and PACBI-aligned programming decisions, and insisted "no investor, past or present, has ever influenced our output." The statement also conceded that KKR "has investments that categorically do not align with our values," a rare instance of a company naming its own owner's conflicts in writing rather than staying silent.

PACBI, the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel, responded the following day, unconvinced that editorial independence was enough of an answer when the money itself was the issue. That tension, between "we don't control who signs our sale documents" and "we still cashed the check", is the fault line the rest of this story runs along.

Boiler Room wasn't the only Superstruct property forced to respond. By May 19, 2025, both Sónar and DGTL had issued their own statements after more than 140 artists signed an open letter demanding action, according to [DJ Mag's reporting](https://djmag.com/news/sonar-and-dgtl-festivals-issue-statements-concerning-kkr-and-superstruct-entertainment). Sónar said it would "explicitly express our solidarity [with] the Palestinian civilian population in Gaza" and that it "distances itself from any action taken by KKR," though the BDS movement called the statement insufficient partly because Sónar didn't address its own sponsorship ties to Coca-Cola and McDonald's, both BDS-listed brands. Artist Dania withdrew from Sónar's 2025 lineup the following month over the same unresolved concerns. Sónar's founders would go on to step away from the festival entirely amid the ongoing controversy.

## Why did Blaise Bellville leave Boiler Room?

Founder and CEO Blaise Bellville, who built Boiler Room out of a single webcam in an East London flat back in 2010, resigned in December 2025 and officially departed the company the following month. A spokesperson confirmed the exit on January 19, 2026, with coverage from [Resident Advisor](https://ra.co/news/84337) noting the departure came on the heels of company-wide layoffs and left no named successor, only an interim leadership team. Boiler Room's own statement thanked Bellville for being "pivotal and highly influential in underground club culture" while offering no explanation tying the exit directly to the KKR controversy.

Whatever the internal reasoning, the optics were unavoidable: the one person most associated with Boiler Room's underground credibility left the building almost exactly a year after the Superstruct sale closed, and roughly six months before the boycott campaign against the company hit its most visible, physical phase.

## What happened at the July 2026 NYC protests?

By July 2025, a New York-based coalition calling itself Boycott Room had formed, initially as an Instagram page, to organize resistance to Boiler Room's ownership structure. A year later, on July 10 and 11, 2026, that campaign staged its biggest actions yet, timed directly against a Boiler Room shoot under the K Bridge in Brooklyn featuring JID and Kenny Beats, as documented in detail by [Unicorn Riot](https://unicornriot.ninja/2026/nyc-activists-maintain-boycott-against-blood-money-rave-boiler-room/).

On the Friday night, at least five protesters staged a die-in inside the K Bridge event, and a separate protester wore a shirt reading "Boiler Room is owned by Israeli arms investors [KKR]" before being removed. Organizers say footage from that night shows attendees stepping over and around demonstrators lying on the ground; Boycott Room's own statement put it starkly: "The rave went on as usual while our martyrs' faces were trampled with indifference." Boiler Room, for its part, told press it was "reviewing what happened and speaking with the venue" and said it had seen footage of "an attendee acting towards a protester in a way that has no place at our events," according to [NME's coverage](https://www.nme.com/news/music/kkr-protesters-at-boiler-room-event-in-new-york-reportedly-assaulted-and-trampled-3956916) of the incident. The exact sequence of what happened in the crowd that night remains disputed and, as NME noted, unproven in full.

The next night, Saturday July 11, Boycott Room ran a counter-rave rather than another confrontation, booking Archangel, DJ Haram, Sister Zo, Ella Hussle, and Janus Rose, with all proceeds donated to Palestinian aid organizations and to Wet'suwet'en First Nations solidarity efforts, per Unicorn Riot's reporting. It was framed less as a protest than as a demonstration that New York's underground scene could throw its own event, on its own terms, without a private-equity-owned platform's name on the flyer.

### Did DJs actually turn down paid bookings?

Yes, and this is the detail that gives the boycott real teeth rather than just symbolic weight. According to Boycott Room organizers cited by Unicorn Riot, offers collectively worth tens of thousands of dollars were turned down by essentially every DJ of relevance in New York's underground dance scene when Boiler Room came calling for its 2026 NYC return. Individual artists spoke on the record about it: Sister Zo said, "I just said no outright to my agent and left it at that." Ella Hussle framed the refusal as "solidarity through the simple act of saying no." Archangel put the broader argument plainly: "There's no magical bubble within the arts where politics don't apply." At least one booked artist, Noel Spiva, withdrew from a Boiler Room lineup just three days before a scheduled appearance.

This isn't unprecedented for Boiler Room specifically. Around the original March 2025 statement, artists including Ikonika, Beatrice M., Mia Koden, BasicDisarm, jtamul, 8ULENTINA, and DAYTIMERS pulled out of Boiler Room bookings. Ikonika's public statement at the time read, "In light of this, I will no longer be performing at the event and urge my peers on the lineup to reconsider appearing." What's changed by 2026 is that the refusals moved from scattered individual withdrawals to an organized, named campaign turning down money as a coordinated tactic rather than a one-off gesture.

## Why did Boiler Room pull its Dublin announcement?

The clearest sign that the boycott had shifted from an American phenomenon to a global one came on June 5, 2026. Boiler Room announced a free return to Dublin, a tech-house and garage-focused session on June 11 at an undisclosed venue headlined by UK artist Riordan. The post was deleted from Boiler Room's social channels within roughly five minutes, according to [Nialler9's reporting](https://nialler9.com/boiler-room-dublin-show-pulled-from-socials-after-backlash/), after an immediate wave of Palestinian flag emojis and comments objecting to the platform's ownership. Dublin DJ EMA's response, "Bye bye BR!!! Not in our city!!!", captured the tone. It marked Boiler Room's first attempted Irish return since the boycott calls intensified after the original January 2025 acquisition, and it collapsed before the RSVP window even opened properly, even though the event page itself briefly stayed live on boilerroom.tv.

That kind of speed, backlash arriving and forcing a retraction inside minutes rather than days, tells you the boycott isn't a slow-burn niche argument anymore. It's a reflex the audience now has ready to deploy the moment Boiler Room's branding shows up in a new city.

## What does this mean for independent platforms and labels?

There's a version of this story that's really about who owns the infrastructure the dance music world runs on, and it's a conversation worth having beyond Boiler Room specifically. We've written before about how consolidation quietly reshapes who controls the pipes independent scenes depend on, whether that's [the licensing pressure closing UK nightclubs](/blog/uk-nightclub-closures-licensing-reform) or distribution deals that route indie labels back through major-owned infrastructure. Boiler Room is the highest-profile case yet of a platform built entirely on underground trust getting absorbed into a private equity portfolio that has nothing to do with music at all, and then having to answer for it in public, repeatedly, for going on two years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who owns Boiler Room in 2026?

Boiler Room is owned by Superstruct Entertainment, a festival conglomerate that owns roughly 80 events across Europe including Sónar, DGTL, and Wacken Open Air. Superstruct itself is majority-owned by KKR, the American private equity firm, which acquired it in a deal reported at approximately $1.3 billion in 2024.

### Why are people boycotting Boiler Room?

The boycott targets KKR's broader investment portfolio, which critics say includes stakes in Israeli technology and data infrastructure firms and ties to defense-linked companies. Boiler Room itself has stated it holds no editorial control tied to that ownership, but organizers argue that revenue from Boiler Room events still ultimately benefits KKR as a shareholder.

### Did Boiler Room's founder leave because of the KKR controversy?

Boiler Room has not officially linked Blaise Bellville's departure to the ownership backlash. He resigned in December 2025 and left the company in January 2026 following a round of layoffs, with Boiler Room's statement thanking him for his role in underground club culture without addressing KKR directly.

### What happened at the Boiler Room event under the K Bridge in July 2026?

Activists from Boycott Room staged a die-in protest inside the July 10, 2026 event featuring JID and Kenny Beats. Organizers allege protesters were stepped on and ignored by attendees, and Boiler Room said it was reviewing footage of an attendee's conduct toward a protester, though the full details of the incident remain disputed.

### Has the boycott affected Boiler Room's bookings outside the US?

Yes. A planned Dublin show announced on June 5, 2026 was pulled from Boiler Room's social media within roughly five minutes after backlash from the city's electronic music community, marking the platform's first attempted Irish return since the boycott began in early 2025.

Two years on from the sale, Boiler Room still hasn't resolved the core contradiction organizers keep pointing back to: a platform that built its name on being close to the underground now answers to shareholders who have nothing to do with it, and every statement, departure, and pulled announcement since January 2025 has been an attempt to manage that distance rather than close it.
