**[Roni Size](/artists/roni-size) played a one-off set on Thursday, October 15, 2026 inside the abandoned Cwmorthin slate mine in Eryri (Snowdonia), North Wales, in a chamber the organisers put 1,375 feet (about 419 metres) below ground.** The event was run by Casio's G-SHOCK brand as the launch of its new MUDMAN GDG-B100 watch, and it was billed as the world's deepest underground rave. Entry was by ballot only, the ballot closed on October 9, 2026, and only "a handful" of places existed. That the night went ahead is now confirmed by a first-hand account from T3 (published October 19, 2026) and by a post-event report from Nation.Cymru, and G-SHOCK Radio has put the set on Mixcloud. The world record itself is still unverified, as set out in the section below.

That is the headline. The more interesting questions sit underneath it: who is paying for a drum and bass rave in a Welsh mountain, what "deepest" actually means when the Guinness concert record sits at roughly 2,700 metres, and what a ballot-only party for a few fit strangers says about where rave culture is in 2026. We read the organiser's own page, the press coverage before and after the event, and the sources on the mine, and we flag below everything we could not confirm.

## What happened at the Roni Size mine rave?

**The set went ahead, but the record has not been ruled on.** T3's Matt Kollat, who says he was among those who attended, [published a first-person account on October 19, 2026](https://www.t3.com/luxury/watches/g-shock-roni-size-welsh-mountain-worlds-deepest-underground-rave-1026). It describes a small group of ballot winners making a two-hour descent through dark, waterlogged passages with scrambles, abseils and zip lines, led by qualified guides from the operator Go Below. At the bottom, Size played inside a large chamber known as Deep Sleep, supported by G-SHOCK Radio's DJ Nav. [Nation.Cymru's post-event report](https://nation.cymru/news/welsh-mining-heritage-meets-rave-culture-in-world-record-attempt/), also dated October 19, 2026, gives the same outline. The organiser's own [G-SHOCK UK page](https://gshock.casio.com/uk/mudman), which we re-read on November 5, still reads as a pre-event announcement.

## What is the world's deepest rave with Roni Size?

It was a one-off "G-SHOCK Sessions with Roni Size" in a slate mine, with attendees reaching the dancefloor by descending dark tunnels and waterlogged passages, crossing underground ravines, riding zip lines and abseiling down rock faces. That is the organiser's own wording on [G-SHOCK's UK MUDMAN page](https://gshock.casio.com/uk/mudman), which gives the date as October 15, 2026, marked "AM + PM", and the venue as Cwmorthin Slate Mine, Eryri, North Wales.

[MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/artists/djs-producers/its-the-most-underground-of-underground-raves-ever-roni-size-to-play-the-worlds-deepest-rave-in-an-abandoned-welsh-mine) and [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/roni-size-host-world-deepest-rave-abandoned-welsh-mine-news) both ran the story in the first week of October, and Mixmag cites a press release as its source. The music is only half the pitch. The other half is a "specialist sound experiment": vibration transducers were to drive parts of Size's set into the slate, while geophones and hydrophones recorded how the frequencies travel through rock, mud and water. That recorded response was then to be fed back into the live mix.

## Where is Cwmorthin, and why does a slate mine matter?

Cwmorthin is a former slate quarry and mine west of Tanygrisiau, near Blaenau Ffestiniog in Gwynedd. According to [Wikipedia's entry on the quarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cwmorthin_quarry), the Casson family opened it in 1810, the Cwmorthin Slate Company formed in January 1861, and the neighbouring Oakeley quarry took it over around 1900. Oakeley closed in 1970, there was small-scale working in the 1980s and 1990s, and all work reportedly ceased in 1997. Sources disagree here: [Sub Brit](https://www.subbrit.org.uk/sites/cwmorthin-slate-mine/) says it stopped working as a separate quarry around 1939 and was afterwards reached through Oakeley. Both can be true, because one describes the independent quarry and the other the wider working life of the mine.

The bigger context is the slate itself. The [Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_Landscape_of_Northwest_Wales) became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021, and Wikipedia records that Wales produced 92 percent of UK slate in 1882, that output peaked in 1898 at over half a million tons with about 17,000 men employed, and that the Oakeley mine at Blaenau Ffestiniog was the largest slate mine in the world. Blaenau's slate was worked underground rather than in open pits, and the parish population rose from 732 in 1801 to 11,274 in 1881. So the drop into the dark is not a stunt location pulled from nowhere. It is a working landscape that built a town, and this is one more use for the chambers its miners left behind.

The same mine already sells a different kind of descent. Go Below runs Deep Sleep, an overnight stay in cabins at 1,375 feet that [Wikipedia describes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Sleep_(hotel)) as opening in April 2023, with guests given a helmet, light, harness and boots. That is the same depth figure the rave uses, and T3's first-hand account says Size played in the chamber known as Deep Sleep. So the number is the operator's established selling point rather than a fresh measurement made for the party.

## Who is Roni Size?

Roni Size, born Ryan Owen Granville Williams in Bristol on October 29, 1969, is one of the founding figures of drum and bass. According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roni_Size), he was expelled from school at 16, went to house parties thrown by the Wild Bunch sound system and learned production in a basement project, and with Krust, DJ Suv and DJ Die he founded the Full Cycle label.

His breakthrough was Roni Size and Reprazent. The group made its live debut in April 1997 at Tribal Gathering, and its debut album *New Forms* came out on June 23, 1997 on Talkin' Loud. That record won the 1997 Mercury Prize. "Brown Paper Bag", with vocalist Onallee, was one of its singles. Later albums included *In the Møde* in 2000 and a solo record, *Touching Down*, in 2002. Reprazent were revived in 2008 with *New Forms²*, and Wikipedia notes Coachella and Ultra appearances in 2009.

How he works matters to this story. In [a 2018 Mixmag piece](https://mixmag.net/read/roni-size-wed-go-to-raves-and-recreate-the-parts-we-liked-for-our-own-music-news), which originally ran in the Guardian, he describes going to raves and recreating the parts he liked when building his own music. In MusicRadar's report he talks about "happy accidents" such as playing a track at the wrong speed, and says Bristol raves happened "down in tunnels" and on front lawns. Playing to the room is the point of his whole approach, so a room made of slate is at least consistent.

He is also not retiring into novelty gigs. MusicRadar reports that he has announced an outdoor show at Bristol's Lloyds Amphitheatre on June 25, 2027, marking thirty years of *New Forms*, with Fabio and Grooverider supporting. For more drum and bass context on this site, see our report on [Noisia's reunion](/blog/noisia-reunion-2026).

## Is a rave underground a new idea?

No. Parties in caves, quarries and disused industrial buildings are as old as the rave itself, and what has changed is who foots the bill. Here is what we can source.

### What were the Coniston cave raves?

The best documented British precedent is also a slate quarry. According to [VICE's 2018 feature](https://vice.com/en/article/the-legendary-cave-raves-of-the-acid-house-era), Outhouse Promotions, later the "Cave Crew", threw parties in a cave at a remote slate quarry near Coniston in the Lake District from the late 1980s into the early 1990s. Organiser Dave "Steely D" Steel recalls them growing from about a dozen people to thousands, with house, acid, Belgian techno and Detroit records on the decks, and police roadblocks backing traffic up for miles. The [Vane gallery's note on Matt Stokes](https://www.vane.org.uk/exhibitions/space-between-us-matt-stokes) adds that word of mouth drew crowds from across the UK in summer 1991, that the landowner had the cave entrances drilled for blasting as a last resort, and that the Cave Crew had disbanded by 1993 after equipment was confiscated.

The parallel is useful and the contrast sharp. In Coniston the cave was the venue and the police were the problem. In Cwmorthin the mine is the venue and a watch brand is the permission slip.

### How deep have gigs actually gone?

Here "deepest" needs care, because it depends what is being measured. The Guinness record for the deepest underground concert has changed hands more than once, and the figures are not measured the same way.

| Event | Depth | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turda salt mine, Romanian Eurovision heat | about 86 m | February 2018 | Not a rave, tourist salt mine (Euronews) |
| Shaft Bottom Boys, Creighton Mine, Sudbury | 1,893.8 m below sea level | March 7, 2020 | Guinness record at the time |
| Miners and Sons, Kidd Mine, Timmins | roughly 2,700 m (9,000 ft level) | November 15, 2024 | Current Guinness record per CBC |
| Roni Size, Cwmorthin | about 419 m below ground (organiser figure) | October 15, 2026 | "Deepest rave" claim, evidence submitted, not yet verified |

The sources are [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/culture/2018/02/13/eurovision-goes-underground-in-romania), [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/commercial/2020/3/canadian-band-performs-record-breaking-concert-below-sea-level-612144) and [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7384952). Note the Guinness figure is below sea level, while the Cwmorthin figure is described as below ground level, so the numbers are not strictly comparable. Even so, the order of magnitude is clear: the concert record is several times deeper than the Welsh mine. So "deepest rave" is a narrow category claim, not a depth record in the usual sense. We have not found any prior "deepest rave" record to beat, and the organisers have not named one.

### What about other reused spaces?

Rave has always colonised buildings that were built for something else. The Pula fortress that first hosted [Dimensions Festival](/festivals/dimensions-festival) was a mid-19th-century military site, and London's [Printworks](/blog/printworks-london-reopening-2026) was a print works. Those venues were turned into clubs by promoters over years. What is different about Cwmorthin is the one-night, brand-built, broadcast-first format.

## How do you get in, and is it safe?

The ballot closed on October 9, 2026 at 15:00 GMT, and the event has now passed, so there is nothing left to apply for. If you were not picked, the practical route is the replay: G-SHOCK's [radio page](https://gshock.casio.com/uk/sounds/radio/) links to the [G-SHOCK Radio Mixcloud account](https://www.mixcloud.com/GShockRadio/), which lists [the full Roni Size set](https://www.mixcloud.com/GShockRadio/g-shock-radio-presents-roni-size-the-deepest-underground-rave-1375ft-1510/) as uploaded on October 17, 2026.

On safety, the facts are thin and we will not pad them. The route involves stairs, waterlogged passages, zip lines and abseils, T3 reports that qualified Go Below guides supervised the descent, and the organisers say winners must meet age, fitness, clothing and equipment requirements without publishing the detail. [Go Below's own site](https://www.go-below.co.uk) lists its Hero Xtreme trip at ages 14 and over and claims experienced guides, though it does not describe qualifications. [Secret GB](https://secretgb.co.uk/2026/10/07/cwmorthin-slate-mine-rave/) points out that a 2023 report on Deep Sleep, a different trip, describes about 45 minutes of uphill walking and then roughly an hour underground, so this is not a casual door-to-dancefloor evening.

On law, the rave provisions of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 were a response to the Castlemorton free festival of 1992, but [section 63 as written](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/33/section/63) covers gatherings on land in the open air of 20 or more people, with amplified music at night, so it is not the obvious framework for a managed underground event, and it exempts licensed activity under the Licensing Act 2003. What licence, if any, applies underground in Cwmorthin has not been published, and we are not offering a legal opinion. Our existing report on [why the UK keeps losing its nightclubs](/blog/uk-nightclub-closures-licensing-reform) explains how restrictive the UK's licensed-venue landscape has become, which is part of why a brand-run one-off in a mountain is attractive in the first place.

Accessibility is the clearest gap. We found no statement from the organisers on access for disabled people, and the route as described suggests it would be demanding for many. That should be said plainly, not buried.

## What we make of it

This section is RTS.FM's own opinion, not reporting. Each point comes with the strongest argument against it.

1. **It is a product launch first and a rave second.** The G-SHOCK page frames the whole thing as the launch of the MUDMAN, and press reports say attendees get the watch before its wider release. The rave is the advertisement. That does not make it fake, but it explains the shape: tiny capacity, maximum spectacle, broadcast as the real deliverable. *The counter: brand money is exactly what pays for the transducers, Starlink uplink and guides, and an artist like Size gets to try an acoustic experiment that no club would ever fund.*

2. **"Deepest" is the weakest part of the pitch and the least important.** As the table above shows, the concert record is far deeper, and 1,375 feet is also the operator's long-standing figure for its hotel. The honest framing is "deep for a rave, in a mine the public can reach", which is still extraordinary. Leaning on a superlative that is unverified invites exactly this scrutiny. *The counter: we found no earlier "deepest rave" claim to beat, so a category claim is a legitimate hook; organisers say they will submit it for independent verification, and until a body rules, scepticism is a courtesy, not an accusation.*

3. **The sound experiment is the one genuinely new idea.** Pumping a drum and bass set into slate and feeding the rock's response back into the mix treats the mountain as an instrument, which fits the happy-accidents instinct Size describes. Bass-heavy music in a mountain of waterlogged passages is at least a sound worth hearing, and it ties to the wider trend we covered in [why festivals now book hi-fi sound systems](/blog/hifi-sound-systems-festival-lineups-despacio-sunflower): sound as the headline act. *The counter: the replay is now on Mixcloud, so anyone can judge the result, and a live mix passed through transducers and a satellite link may well end up as a better story than listen. We have not heard it ourselves.*

4. **Access and safety are the real conversation.** Rave history is full of people turning up to unlikely places, from the Coniston cave to disused factories, and police stopping them. Here the state has been replaced by a ballot and a risk assessment, which is arguably safer, but also means a handful of fit winners are chosen by a brand. We would like to see capacity, accessibility and safety arrangements published, not implied. *The counter: an experienced operator running a controlled descent is exactly how an underground party should be run, and publishing every safety detail for a handful of attendees may simply not have been a priority.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was the Roni Size deepest rave?

It took place on Thursday, October 15, 2026. The organiser's page listed the date as AM and PM, and did not explain whether that meant separate sessions.

### What happened at the Roni Size mine rave?

Roni Size played a one-off set in the Deep Sleep chamber, supported by G-SHOCK Radio's DJ Nav, after a guided descent of about two hours, according to T3's first-hand account published on October 19, 2026. Nation.Cymru reported the same, and both say the Welsh national anthem was played before the set. The organiser itself has not published a full account, and the figure of 39 attendees comes only from an unbylined Brit Brief report.

### Where was the rave in a Welsh mine?

It was inside the abandoned Cwmorthin slate mine in Eryri (Snowdonia), North Wales, near Blaenau Ffestiniog in Gwynedd. The mine is west of the village of Tanygrisiau.

### How deep underground was the Snowdonia rave?

The organisers say 1,375 feet below ground, which is about 419 metres. One news site rounded this up to 1,400 feet in its headline, but the organiser figure is 1,375.

### What was the Casio G-SHOCK underground mine rave?

It was a promotional event run by G-SHOCK, the watch brand of Casio, to launch its MUDMAN GDG-B100 watch. The Cwmorthin rave was billed as "G-SHOCK Sessions with Roni Size." Press reports name the adventure operator Go Below as the guide for the descent.

### Can I still get Roni Size underground rave tickets?

No. Entry was by ballot on the G-SHOCK website, the ballot closed on October 9, 2026 at 15:00 GMT, and the event has now taken place. The organisers said only a handful of places were available. The next Roni Size date we found is Lloyds Amphitheatre, Bristol, on June 25, 2027.

### How much did the Cwmorthin rave cost?

Time Out reported that it was free, with entry by ballot only. The organiser page we read does not state a price, so the free claim is reported rather than confirmed.

### Can I still watch the set?

You can listen to it. G-SHOCK Radio's Mixcloud account lists the full Roni Size set, uploaded on October 17, 2026 and running about 64 minutes, plus a separate DJ Nav set from the same night. We confirmed the listing through Mixcloud's public API.

### Was the Roni Size mine rave a world record?

Not yet. T3 reports that G-SHOCK has submitted evidence for independent verification, and until that is complete the claim remains unofficial. No recognising body has been named and no ratification has been reported. The Guinness record for the deepest underground concert is a separate and much deeper category.

### Who else played at the Cwmorthin rave?

DJ Nav of G-SHOCK Radio supported Roni Size, per T3 and Nation.Cymru. Fabio and Grooverider are mentioned only as support for Roni Size's Bristol show on June 25, 2027.

### Was the rave safe?

The organiser has not published a safety plan. T3 reports that qualified Go Below guides supervised the descent, and Brit Brief adds that attendees wore helmets, which we could not confirm elsewhere. Go Below lists its own trips with age limits and guides, but we found no incident report either way.

### What was the sound experiment?

Vibration transducers sent parts of the set into the slate, while geophones and hydrophones recorded how the sound travels through rock, mud and water. T3 adds that engineers placed a speaker and two ambient microphones in a separate chamber, working like a studio echo chamber, so the mine's natural acoustics could be mixed into the set.
