As of 18 November 2026, the European record shops with the strongest evidence behind them are Hard Wax in Berlin, Rush Hour in Amsterdam, Superfly and Dizonord in Paris, Discos Paradiso in Barcelona, Snickars and Andra Jazz in Stockholm and Ooh Aah in Copenhagen. We checked 26 shops in 15 cities against official sites and press.

This is a city-break guide, not a ranking. Every shop below was read against its own website, a news report or a city directory, and each entry says what the source was and when it dates from. Where a source was old or two sources disagreed, we say so rather than smooth it over.

## What are the best record shops in Europe?

As of 18 November 2026, the European record shops we could verify cluster in a few cities. Berlin has Hard Wax (founded 1989) and OYE Records (2002). Amsterdam has Rush Hour (1997), Red Light Records and Concerto (1955). Paris has Superfly Records, Dizonord, Betino's and Techno Import. Barcelona's Discos Paradiso (2010), Stockholm's Snickars Records (1995) and Andra Jazz, Copenhagen's Ooh Aah Records and Can Records, Antwerp's Wally's Groove World, Ghent's Music Mania (1969), Munich's Public Possession, Oslo's Råkk & Rålls, Warsaw's Side One, Lisbon's Flur Discos, Vienna's Substance and Das Market, Rome's Radiation Records and Milan's Serendeepity and XTCR complete the list. The dance-focused picks are Hard Wax, Rush Hour, Discos Paradiso, Substance, Techno Import and Snickars. The UK has its own guide, and this list is not a ranking. Each entry notes the source behind its founding year.

## How did we choose?

We started from two public lists, The Vinyl Factory's 50-shop feature and the Financial Times list of the world's best record stores, then added shops from city directories and local press. A shop made the cut only if we could tie it to a dated source and find at least one concrete detail.

Two limits matter. Keep Them Spinning, a vinyl storage retailer, publishes city directories built from Google listings, so we used them only as a sign that a shop was still listed in July to October 2026, never for taste. And a shop absent from this page is not worse than one on it, because we only had room for what we could verify.

## What do the Vinyl Factory and Financial Times lists say about Europe?

The Vinyl Factory's feature, which carries a late 2016 date, collects the first 50 shops from its weekly series. It says plainly that the shops are not ranked. By our count 15 of the 50 are in continental Europe: Hard Wax, OYE, Superfly, Betino's, Wally's Groove World, Red Light Records, Rush Hour, Can Records, Public Possession, Side One, Snickars, Discos Paradiso, Flur Discos, Music Mania and Råkk & Rålls.

The Financial Times list is a January 2025 piece in which its writers and editors named their own go-to shops. The FT article is paywalled, so we relied on Time Out's reports. Time Out says the FT picked Dizonord in Paris, Andra Jazz in Stockholm and Ooh Aah Records in Copenhagen among its European inclusions, and Barcelona City Records as the place for Latin classics. Time Out France adds that four Paris shops appeared among 39 in total: Dizonord, Superfly, Hark Records and Bar Italia.

The two lists barely overlap. Among the FT names we could see, only Superfly also appears in The Vinyl Factory feature, which shows how personal this kind of list is. A 2016 roundup also needs checking before you book a train: several of its European shops have moved or changed since, as the entries below show.

## Which European city has the best record shops?

There is no clean winner, but the evidence points three ways. Paris has the deepest independent bench: the FT picked four of its shops, and Keep Them Spinning's July 2026 directory lists 27. Berlin has the heritage, with Hard Wax still the reference point for techno. Amsterdam has the density: iamsterdam's guide, updated on 14 October 2026, lists 15 shops in one city.

If you want one city for a weekend of digging, pick Paris for range, Berlin for techno and Amsterdam for walkability.

## Best record shops in Germany and Austria

### Berlin

**Hard Wax** was founded in 1989 by Mark Ernestus, according to Wikipedia and The Berliner. On 30 October 2023 it left its Paul-Lincke-Ufer home, where it had spent 27 years, for the Kraftwerk complex on Köpenicker Straße, which also houses Tresor and OHM. Resident Advisor and Mixmag reported that redevelopment of the old Kreuzberg building forced the move. The Berliner notes that Marcel Dettmann, Cassy and DJ Hell have all worked behind its counter, and hardwax.com was listing 2026 reissues when we read it.

**OYE Records** opened in 2002 under Lovis Willenberg, per a 2010 Slow Travel Berlin profile that says two long-time staff became partners in 2007. Its own site, copyrighted 2025, lists two shops: Oderbergerstraße 4 in Prenzlauer Berg and Friedelstraße 49 in Neukölln. The stock began with Latin, soul and funk and now covers house and techno too. Our [Berlin clubs guide](/blog/best-berlin-clubs-2026-guide) covers the nights.

### Munich

**Public Possession** is the cautionary tale. Mixmag reported on 9 March 2023 that the Klenzestraße shop, open since March 2013, would close after the owners said new landlords forced them out. Textilwirtschaft reported on 18 October 2024 that the team had opened a roughly 90 square metre store in Munich's Luitpoldblock, split about half fashion and design, half music and art. Go for the label's records and DJ nights, not a pure digging session.

### Vienna

**Substance** opened as a record store in 2001, says a 2019 Vienna Würstelstand guide, which calls it one of the city's largest shops for electronic dance music. It is on Westbahnstraße, and Keep Them Spinning's July 2026 Vienna guide still lists it at Westbahnstraße 16.

**Das Market** sits at the corner of Zieglergasse and Westbahnstraße and is run by Fritz Plöckinger, per the same 2019 guide, with disco, electro, house and techno on the racks. Keep Them Spinning also lists it in 2026, at Zieglergasse 40.

## Best record shops in the Benelux

### Amsterdam

**Rush Hour** is the city's best-known dance shop and also a label, distributor and events organiser, by its own description. iamsterdam says its label has been influential since 1997, and Mixmag reported its April 2016 move to a larger space on Spuistraat. Its 2026 contact page lists opening hours seven days a week, including Sunday from 13:00 to 18:00.

**Red Light Records** was on Oudekerksplein when The Vinyl Factory visited in 2016, and you had to ring a buzzer to get in. iamsterdam, updated in October 2026, lists it at Oudezijds Achterburgwal 133 with new and second-hand vinyl in oddball genres such as euro boogie, new age and Bollywood.

**Concerto** is not a dance specialist, but it is the oldest founding date in our list. Its own site says it has traded on Utrechtsestraat since 1955 and now fills five connected buildings with vinyl, CDs and coffee, plus a label, publisher and stage. iamsterdam calls it Amsterdam's oldest record shop.

### Antwerp

**Wally's Groove World** was founded in 1997 by Koenie van Immerseel, per The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile, which listed Lange Nieuwstraat 126 and a range from Belgian new wave to Detroit techno. The shop's own website, with a 2026 copyright, describes a physical store in Antwerp and a large online stock. We could not read its hours, so confirm before travelling.

### Ghent

**Music Mania** says on its own site that it has traded since 1969, and The Vinyl Factory called it Belgium's longest-running record shop in 2016. The site lists Sint-Pietersnieuwstraat 19, open Monday to Saturday and on Sunday afternoons, plus a second Ghent shop at Kraanlei 6. It was promoting "Highlights of 2026" when we read it.

## Best record shops in France, Spain and Portugal

### Paris

**Superfly Records** was founded in 2009, per Time Out France, and the owners are named there as Manu and Paulo. Its site at 53 rue Notre-Dame-de-Nazareth lists soul, funk, jazz, Afro, Latin and Brazilian, and shows an update schedule for November. The shop also runs a reissue label.

**Dizonord** opened on 8 February 2019 at 9 rue André Messager, with a two-day party featuring more than 40 DJs, as The Vinyl Factory reported at the time. It sells new and second-hand records across afro-funk, synth-pop, disco and field recordings, alongside a café and bar. Time Out France credited the FT with naming it in 2025.

**Betino's** is at 32 rue Saint-Sébastien near Oberkampf. The Vinyl Factory said in 2016 that it had been around since 1999, with original pressings and a Patrick Adams section. Its own site lists new releases and second-hand stock.

**Techno Import** has been Paris's techno headquarters since 1993, Time Out Paris wrote in April 2023, and owner Mazen said he keeps about 10,000 records in the shop. It is at 16 rue des Taillandiers, and Keep Them Spinning's July 2026 Paris guide ranks it fifth. Syncrophone Records, at 4 rue des Taillandiers, and Yoyaku are on that list too.

### Barcelona

**Discos Paradiso** opened in April 2010 in the Raval, says The Vinyl Factory, founded by Gerad Condemines and Arnau Farrés. The shop's website lists Carrer de Ferlandina 39 and offers free in-store pickup. Keep Them Spinning's October 2026 Barcelona guide includes it.

**Barcelona City Records** is at Carrer de Jerusalem 32 near La Rambla, per Time Out Spain on 25 January 2025. It reported roughly 17,000 records sorted by style and the FT's praise for its Latin stock, including Colombian cumbia from the 1960s. We found no 2026 confirmation, so check before you go.

### Lisbon

**Flur Discos** was founded in 2001 by Pedro Santos and José Moura, per The Vinyl Factory. That 2016 profile placed it on the river at Cais da Pedra, but its own site, copyrighted 2026, now lists Mercado de Arroios, Loja 28 and 29, open Monday to Saturday. Its highlights include the Príncipe label, which Moura helps run.

## Best record shops in Scandinavia

### Stockholm

**Snickars Records** says on its site that it has been buying, trading and selling records since 1995, and online since 2005. It is at Hökens gata 11, reached by walking through a gallery, and it lists Saturday afternoon gigs. Opening days are Wednesday to Saturday only.

**Andra Jazz** began in the late 1960s as the antiquarian bookshop Blå Tornet, its site says, and has been at Rödabergsgatan 9 since 2007. It claims about 100,000 jazz records in stock and opens Wednesday to Friday afternoons and Saturday midday. Time Out named it among the FT's European picks.

### Copenhagen

**Ooh Aah Records** buys and sells second-hand LPs and 45s in all genres at Ryesgade 77. Its site lists Monday to Friday afternoons and Saturday mornings. Time Out reported the FT naming it in January 2025.

**Can Records** is run by Martin Aalykke Kristiansen and his wife, per The Vinyl Factory, with a deliberately small stock of early electronic, ambient, new wave and disco. Keep Them Spinning's profile, dated 13 August 2026, still lists it at Tullinsgade 5.

### Oslo

**Råkk & Rålls** was a three-storey second-hand emporium at Akersgata 39 when The Vinyl Factory visited. Keep Them Spinning's July 2026 Oslo guide lists it at Stortingsgata 8, so it has moved since 2016. Treat the old address as obsolete.

## Best record shops in Poland and Italy

### Warsaw

**Side One** is run by Wojtek Żdanuk, known as DJ WWW, and The Vinyl Factory said in 2016 it had celebrated its tenth birthday with a compilation on its S1 Warsaw label. The shop's site gives ul. Chmielna 21, open Monday to Friday 13:00 to 19:00 and Saturday 12:00 to 16:00, and carries a "Best Records 2026" list.

### Rome

**Radiation Records** opened in 1996, per a 2019 Romeing guide, at Via Romanello da Forlì 14 in Pigneto, covering punk, metal, hip-hop, soul and jazz. Keep Them Spinning ranks it first in Rome in 2026. A reader comment from March 2025 says its Monti branch no longer exists.

### Milan

**Serendeepity** opened in 2009 on Corso di Porta Ticinese 100, its own site says, with records on the ground floor and vintage clothing in the basement. Lampoon's July 2024 profile says founder Nicola Mazzetti took over from a closing shop and was 23 at the time. The site carries a 2026 copyright.

**XTCR** is on Via Carlo Farini in Isola and is described by Keep Them Spinning in 2026 as sharply curated across electronic, leftfield and reissues. We found no founding year.

## Where to buy techno records in Europe?

Start with Hard Wax in Berlin, then Techno Import, Syncrophone and Yoyaku in Paris. Rush Hour in Amsterdam, Substance in Vienna, Discos Paradiso in Barcelona and XTCR in Milan are the other dance-first shops here, with Wally's in Antwerp if its status checks out.

## Where are the best record shops in the UK?

We kept the UK out of the main list. The Vinyl Factory's 50 includes Rough Trade West, Phonica, Honest Jon's, Piccadilly, Idle Hands, Rubadub and Transmission, and Time Out says the FT chose 18 British shops, among them Rough Trade, Third Man Records, Sounds of the Universe, Tribe Records and Vinyl Whistle. Start with our [UK record shops guide](/blog/best-record-shops-in-the-uk-2026).

## How do you plan a record shop city break?

Check hours first. Andra Jazz opens Wednesday to Saturday for a few hours, Snickars is closed Sunday to Tuesday, and Red Light Records asks you to ring a buzzer. Rush Hour and Music Mania open on Sundays, which suits a weekend trip.

Expect to dig rather than browse. Many shops here sell second-hand stock, and new vinyl is competitive: our report on [vinyl's best year since 1983](/blog/vinyl-sales-record-2025) shows why. Cash, carry-on weight and an online shop for the heavy stuff all help.

## What we could not verify

- **The FT list itself.** It is paywalled. We used Time Out's coverage, including the figure of 39 shops, and cannot say how the FT chose them.
- **Hard Wax opening hours.** We confirmed the 2023 move but did not read current hours.
- **Rush Hour's address.** Mixmag gave Spuistraat 116 in 2016, but the 2026 contact page lists Spuistraat 110.
- **Red Light Records.** The Vinyl Factory listed Oudekerksplein 26 in 2016 and iamsterdam lists Oudezijds Achterburgwal 133.
- **Wally's Groove World and Barcelona City Records.** We found no 2025 or 2026 report confirming their addresses or hours.
- **Founding years.** Betino's 1999 comes from The Vinyl Factory only. Side One's mid-2000s start is inferred from its tenth birthday.
- **Keep Them Spinning.** Its directories contain slips, such as Graz and Katowice addresses under Vienna and Warsaw, so we used them only as a presence signal.
- **Serendeepity on The Vinyl Factory.** We could not open that entry and used the shop's site instead.
- **Dropped.** Public Possession's Klenzestraße shop closed in 2023. Only the new Luitpoldblock store is listed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best record shop in Europe?

There is no single winner. The Vinyl Factory and the Financial Times both avoid a ranking, and their European picks barely overlap. For techno, Hard Wax in Berlin is the most cited reference.

### Which European city is best for vinyl shopping?

Paris has the deepest independent scene in our evidence, with four shops on the FT list of 39 and 27 in Keep Them Spinning's 2026 directory. Berlin suits techno and Amsterdam is the easiest to walk.

### Where can I buy techno records in Europe?

Hard Wax in Berlin, Techno Import and Syncrophone in Paris, Rush Hour in Amsterdam and Substance in Vienna are the clearest dance specialists. Discos Paradiso in Barcelona and XTCR in Milan also lean electronic.

### Which record shop in our list is the oldest?

Concerto in Amsterdam, which says it has traded on Utrechtsestraat since 1955. Music Mania in Ghent says 1969, and Andra Jazz traces its roots to a late 1960s bookshop.

### Which European record shops did the Financial Times name?

Time Out reports Dizonord, Andra Jazz, Ooh Aah and Barcelona City Records among the European picks. Time Out France adds Superfly, Hark Records and Bar Italia in Paris.

### Are the shops on The Vinyl Factory list still open?

Mostly, but not unchanged. Flur Discos and Råkk & Rålls have new addresses, Hard Wax moved in 2023 and Public Possession lost its original shop. We could not confirm Wally's.

### Which shops are best for jazz and soul?

Andra Jazz in Stockholm is jazz-only, with about 100,000 records by its own count. Superfly and Betino's in Paris, and Concerto in Amsterdam, cover soul, jazz and funk.

### Can I buy from these shops online?

Many sell online. Hard Wax, Rush Hour, OYE, Flur, Music Mania, Side One and Snickars all run webshops, and Andra Jazz sells through Discogs, according to their own sites.

### How should I plan a record shop city break?

Check each shop's own hours, since several are open only a few days a week. Allow time for second-hand digging.
