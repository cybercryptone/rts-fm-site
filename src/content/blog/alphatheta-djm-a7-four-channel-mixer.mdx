**AlphaTheta announced the DJM-A7 on 10 November 2026, a four-channel mixer putting the DJM-A9's layout in a DJM-750MK2-sized body. As of 13 November 2026, most outlets list US$1,599 before tax (RA says US$1,499). It adds Master Texture and two USB-C ports but fewer effects than the A9, and ships during November per DJ TechTools, 18 November in Japan per AlphaTheta.**

The DJM-A7 adds two Master Texture knobs that tune the output's high and low end to the room, two USB-C computer ports for fast changeovers and built-in SonicLink wireless headphone monitoring. It drops most of the A9's effects, the second headphone section and the dedicated mic strip. We have not tested it, so every opinion below is attributed to the people who have.

## What is the difference between the DJM-A7, DJM-A9 and DJM-750MK2?

The DJM-A7 sits between the two older mixers: the A9's layout and 45 mm faders in a body 11 mm wider than the 750MK2, with a feature set that is cut down from the A9 in places and new in others. Specifications come from AlphaTheta's comparison tables. As of 13 November 2026, prices come from [DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2026/11/10/alphatheta-djm-a7-a9-layout-four-channels-1599/), which gives the A9's current price as $2,899 and calls $1,399 the 750MK2's final price.

AlphaTheta's table counts 11 Beat FX on the 750MK2, while [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/?p=2799623) counts 14, so treat that cell as disputed.

## When does the DJM-A7 come out?

As of 13 November 2026, the DJM-A7 has a confirmed sale date only in Japan: AlphaTheta's [Japanese announcement](https://alphatheta.com/ja/information/djm-a7-4-channel-mixer-introduction/) sets it for 18 November 2026. Elsewhere, DJ TechTools and Magnetic Magazine say dealers ship it during November. Decoded Magazine says it is on sale from 10 November, which we could not corroborate.

## What is new on the DJM-A7?

The DJM-A7's headline feature is Master Texture, which AlphaTheta pitches at rooms without a sound engineer. According to [AlphaTheta's product page](https://alphatheta.com/en/product/dj-mixer/djm-a7/black/) and [launch announcement](https://alphatheta.com/en/information/djm-a7-4-channel-mixer-introduction/), the mixer has four channels with 32-bit ESS converters on the channel inputs and the master, booth and record outputs, running at 48 kHz, with a quoted signal-to-noise ratio of 110 dB over USB and 102 dB on line inputs.

One Master Texture knob moves the top end between Smoky and Bright, the other moves the low end between Smooth and Punchy. AlphaTheta calls it an industry first, based on its own research as of July 2026. [Decoded Magazine](https://www.decodedmagazine.com/alphatheta-djm-a7-four-channel-mixer/) notes that it is a manual tone control, not automatic room correction, and that the DJM-A9 does not have it.

Also new are a Split Filter among the four Sound Color FX, which works mainly on the midrange, and a Soft Mix Curve that rolls off the highs slightly as a channel fader comes down. The Beat FX selector knob becomes six illuminated buttons. A Link port supports Link Cue with compatible PRO DJ LINK players, and a third USB-C port, MULTI I/O, connects the RMX-IGNITE effects unit with one cable.

## What does the DJM-A7 lose compared with the DJM-A9?

Against the A9, the DJM-A7 keeps the channel strip layout, the 45 mm faders, the converters, Link Cue, dual USB-C and MULTI I/O. Beat FX fall from 14 to six and Sound Color FX from six to four. DJ TechTools reports no analogue send and return jacks, one shared headphone cue section instead of two, and a mic that shows up as a source on each channel's input selector rather than getting its own strip.

Digital DJ Tips adds that there is no X-Pad, that Beat FX loops stop at eight beats and that the Magvel crossfader of the 750MK2 is gone. Its reviewer, Phil Morse, rates the DJM-A7 3.5 out of 5 and concludes that it "gives away too much of what made the 750MK2 so versatile." He found Master Texture subtle, closer to crude bass and treble controls, and inert when centred.

DJ TechTools raises the booth-politics problem: a 9pm DJ who pushes Bright to the maximum leaves it for the 11pm DJ, and any venue with a real engineer will park both knobs at centre.

## Does the DJM-A7 work with Traktor and Serato?

As of 13 November 2026, AlphaTheta's page lists rekordbox for Mac and Windows (hardware unlock, no subscription), plus Serato DJ Pro, djay Pro and djay for iOS for the DJM-A7, each of the last three needing its own paid licence or subscription. Traktor is not named. AlphaTheta also says the rekordbox version compatible with the DJM-A7 is still to be released, so check that before promising a rekordbox-only venue anything.

AlphaTheta's 750MK2 comparison lists Traktor Pro 3, so the omission stands out now that inMusic owns Traktor, as we explained in [what happens next for Traktor](/blog/native-instruments-inmusic-traktor-what-happens-next). Omission from a list does not prove incompatibility, and we have not tested it. DJs who use djay Pro's real-time stems, covered in our [guide to AI stem separation in DJ tools](/blog/ai-stem-separation-dj-tools), will care that djay, including djay for iOS over USB-C, is on the list.

## Who is the DJM-A7 for?

The DJM-A7 targets the rooms where the 750MK2 became the default: bars, hotels, restaurants and small clubs with rotating line-ups. This is our reading, not a tested result. It matches the old mixer's depth and height but is 11 mm wider, so a booth cut-out or flight case built tightly around a 750MK2 needs measuring before anyone orders.

For bookers, the useful questions are practical. A DJ who builds sets around flangers, outboard effects through send and return, or a hosted mic with talkover should check the mixer on the advance sheet, because the DJM-A7 does not do those things like a 750MK2 or A9. A laptop DJ gets the opposite: two USB-C ports mean the next act can plug in before the last one leaves.

Money matters too. A 1,599-euro mixer is a real line item for a small venue, in a scene where, as [our look at DJ fees and promoters](/blog/dj-fees-underground-promoters-squeeze-2026) records, Mixmag estimated in 2015 that local residents were often paid nothing or about 100 pounds a night. The buyers are mostly venues and hobbyists: our [DJ economics data piece](/blog/underground-dj-economics-data) cites an analysis finding that 1.6 percent of Resident Advisor's 134,000 DJ profiles have five or more upcoming gigs.

## What it means for the gear market

AlphaTheta now has a clear step between small mixers and the A9. DJ TechTools figures two CDJ-1500X players plus a DJM-A7 come to $4,997 at street prices, against $8,897 for two CDJ-3000X players and an A9. Our [guide to standalone DJ systems](/blog/best-standalone-dj-systems-2026) covers the all-in-one alternatives and notes that the CDJ-1500X's US price is reported as both $1,599 and $1,699, the latter being what DJ TechTools' total implies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is the AlphaTheta DJM-A7?

As of 13 November 2026, most outlets and one US retailer list the AlphaTheta DJM-A7 at US$1,599 before tax. RA reports 1,599 euros and 1,369 pounds excluding tax, but gives the US price as $1,499, and Digital DJ Tips guessed 1,299 pounds for the UK. AlphaTheta's own product page lists no price.

### When is the DJM-A7 release date?

AlphaTheta's Japanese announcement sets a Japan sale date of 18 November 2026. Elsewhere, as of 13 November 2026, DJ TechTools and Magnetic Magazine say dealers ship the DJM-A7 during November, while Decoded Magazine says it is on sale from 10 November, which we could not corroborate. AlphaTheta's English pages list no sale date.

### DJM-A7 vs DJM-750MK2: what changed?

The DJM-A7 takes the DJM-A9's layout and 45 mm faders and adds Master Texture, two USB-C ports, built-in SonicLink and Link Cue, in a body 11 mm wider than the 750MK2. It drops that mixer's send and return, dedicated mic channel and talkover, and has six Beat FX against 11 per AlphaTheta (14 per Digital DJ Tips). Digital DJ Tips and Decoded Magazine both call it the replacement, and AlphaTheta says it is easy to swap into existing setups.

### Is the DJM-A7 compatible with Serato and Traktor?

As of 13 November 2026, AlphaTheta lists rekordbox for Mac and Windows, Serato DJ Pro, djay Pro and djay for iOS for the DJM-A7, and does not name Traktor. Omission from the list does not prove incompatibility, and we have not tested it. Only rekordbox is free with the hardware, and the compatible rekordbox version is still listed as coming soon.

### What is Master Texture on the DJM-A7?

Master Texture is a pair of knobs on the DJM-A7: one moves the top end between Smoky and Bright, the other moves the low end between Smooth and Punchy. AlphaTheta calls it an industry first, based on its own research as of July 2026, and pitches it at rooms without a sound engineer. Decoded Magazine notes it is a manual tone control, not automatic room correction, and Digital DJ Tips found it subtle.

### Does the DJM-A7 have a talkover or send/return?

According to AlphaTheta's comparison table, the DJM-A7 has one mic input and no talkover. DJ TechTools reports no analogue send and return jacks, and the mic appears as a source on each channel's input selector rather than on its own strip. A DJ who relies on either should check the DJM-A7 on the advance sheet.

### Is the DJM-A7 worth it?

We have not tested the DJM-A7, so we report only reviewers. Digital DJ Tips' Phil Morse rates it 3.5 out of 5 and concludes that it "gives away too much of what made the 750MK2 so versatile." DJ TechTools notes that a DJ who pushes Master Texture's Bright to the maximum leaves it for the next act, and that any venue with a real engineer will park both knobs at centre.
