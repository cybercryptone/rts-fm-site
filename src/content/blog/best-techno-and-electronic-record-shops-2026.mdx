As of 18 November 2026, we could confirm 18 dance music record shops in 15 cities from their own websites or 2024 to 2026 reports, including Hard Wax in Berlin, Rush Hour in Amsterdam, Phonica in London and Gramaphone in Chicago. Online stores and the items we could not verify are covered below.

Most "best record shops" lists are written once and never updated. Hard Wax moved building in 2023, Idle Hands closed for two years and came back, and Public Possession in Munich was forced out of its shop. So every entry here says what we read and how recent it is.

We started from The Vinyl Factory's 2016 feature of 50 shops and the Financial Times selection as summarised by Time Out in January 2025, then checked each dance-focused name against its own site, Wikipedia or reputable press. Our pillar guide and city guides are linked at the end.

## What are the best techno and electronic record shops in the world?

As of 18 November 2026, there is no official ranking of the world's best techno and electronic record shops, so this is a verified shortlist. Berlin has Hard Wax (1989), OYE Records and Spacehall. Rush Hour in Amsterdam, Clone in Rotterdam and Wally's Groove World in Antwerp cover the Low Countries. In the UK, Phonica in London, Rubadub in Glasgow and Idle Hands in Bristol lead the dance shops. Kompakt in Cologne, Syncrophone and Yoyaku in Paris, Snickars in Stockholm and Discos Paradiso in Barcelona complete the European picks. Beyond Europe, the list includes Gramaphone in Chicago, Submerge's Somewhere in Detroit outlet, A-1 Record Shop in New York and the club music shop at Disk Union Shibuya in Tokyo. Several of these also run labels or distribution, and we list opening years only where a source confirmed them.

There is no official ranking, and we do not pretend to offer one. What we can offer is a verified shortlist. The Vinyl Factory's 2016 feature is a useful yardstick: it pitched Discos Paradiso as Barcelona's answer to Hardwax or Phonica, and placed Wally's Groove World on a par with Phonica and Rush Hour. Those three names recur as its reference points.

## Which Berlin record shops matter for techno?

Berlin has the densest cluster in this guide, and our Berlin shop guide, linked at the end, goes deeper. Here are the three that fit a dance focus.

### Hard Wax

Hard Wax opened in 1989, founded by Mark Ernestus of Basic Channel, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Wax). In October 2023 it left Paul-Lincke-Ufer in Kreuzberg after 27 years because the building was to be redeveloped, [Mixmag reported](https://mixmag.net/read/hard-wax-relocation-kraftwerk-building-berlin-tresor-ohm-mark-enestus-news), and it reopened on 30 October in the Kraftwerk complex on Köpenicker Strasse, between Tresor and OHM.

The shop's own [contact page](https://hardwax.com/contact) lists Köpenicker Str. 70 and opening hours of Monday to Saturday, 15:00 to 20:00. [The Berliner's August 2026 guide](https://www.the-berliner.com/guides/best-vinyl-record-shops-berlin/) describes it as mostly stocking dub and techno.

### OYE Records

OYE's [own info page](https://oye-records.com/info) lists two Berlin stores: Oderbergerstr. 4 in Prenzlauer Berg (Monday to Saturday, 12 to 20h) and Friedelstr. 49 in Neukölln (Monday to Friday 13 to 20h, Saturday 12 to 20h).

The Berliner's 2026 guide says the shop focuses on house, disco, hip-hop and jazz. The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile described it as a first port for US and UK underground imports, so it counts for dance buyers even though its range is wider than techno.

### Spacehall

Spacehall, at Zossener Straße 35 in Kreuzberg, lists opening hours of Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday 12 to 20, Saturday 11 to 20, and closed on Tuesday and Sunday, according to its [own site](https://spacehall.de/). It buys vinyl and CDs in person or from an emailed list, and also sells through Discogs.

The Berliner describes two floors of electronic, rock and second-hand records. The site gives two postcodes, so check a map.

## Which Dutch and Belgian shops should a techno fan visit?

The Low Countries punch above their size, and our Amsterdam shop guide, linked at the end, covers the capital in detail.

### Rush Hour (Amsterdam)

Rush Hour's [about page](https://www.rushhour.nl/about-rush-hour-music) says the company formed in late 1997 with the opening of its record store and mailorder service, initially importing obscure house and techno records. It says distribution followed in 1999 and the label's first release came just before the millennium.

The [contact page](https://www.rushhour.nl/contact) lists the company address as Spuistraat 110 and store hours of 11:00 to 18:00 or 19:00 on most days, 12:00 to 18:00 on Monday and 13:00 to 18:00 on Sunday. The [distribution page](https://www.rushhour.nl/rush-hour-distribution) says it supplies independent stores and carries labels including L.I.E.S.

### Clone (Rotterdam)

Clone's [contact page](https://clone.nl/contact.html) lists the store at Raampoortstraat 12 in Rotterdam, open Monday to Thursday 10:30 to 18:00, Friday until 21:00, Saturday until 18:00 and Sunday 12:00 to 17:00. Its [homepage](https://clone.nl/) carries 2026 newsletters.

[VICE reported in 2017](https://www.vice.com/en/article/clone-serge-25-year-anniversary-interview) that the Rotterdam imprint behind the shop, run by Serge Verschuur, was marking its 25th birthday, which dates the label to about 1992. We found no source for the shop's own founding year, so the table says "label c. 1992".

### Wally's Groove World (Antwerp)

[The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile](https://www.thevinylfactory.com/features/50-worlds-best-record-shops) says Koenie van Immerseel founded Wally's in 1997, at Lange Nieuwstraat 126 at the time, with stock ranging from European dance music to Detroit and Chicago house, techno and acid. It lists Juan Atkins and Richie Hawtin among its customers.

The shop's [web store](https://wgwstore.com/) describes it as a music shop with a physical store in Antwerp. We found no 2025 or 2026 source for opening hours, so we give none.

## Which UK shops stock the best new dance music?

Our UK and London guides, linked at the end, cover the full picture. These three are the dance specialists we verified.

### Phonica (London)

Phonica opened in 2003 at 51 Poland Street in Soho, founded by Simon Rigg, Heidi Van Den Amstel and Tom Relleen, per [The Vinyl Factory's 2023 profile](https://thevinylfactory.com/features/phonica-records/). The same piece says it stocks everything from techno and house to Afro and Latin edits.

[Time Out's listing, published in November 2024](https://www.timeout.com/london/shopping/phonica), gives hours of 11am to 7pm Monday to Wednesday and Saturday, 11am to 7:30pm Thursday and Friday, and noon to 6pm Sunday. It describes a 1970s-style interior with worn leather sofas.

### Rubadub (Glasgow)

Rubadub's [about page](https://www.rubadub.co.uk/pages/about) puts the shop at 35 Howard Street, open 11am to 6pm Tuesday to Saturday, and calls the business independent with 30 years behind it. The page quotes Resident Advisor as saying the shop has inspired generations of Glasgow DJs, producers and promoters.

The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile dates the shop to August 1992 and notes its distribution arm.

### Idle Hands (Bristol)

Chris Farrell opened Idle Hands in February 2011, per the 2016 Vinyl Factory feature. It shut in August 2022 after 11 years on City Road, and [Mixmag reported](https://mixmag.net/read/bristol-record-store-label-idle-hands-reopen-two-years-closure-news) that it reopened on Wednesday 20 November 2024.

The shop's [own site](https://idlehandsbristol.com/) now lists 20-21 Lower Park Row, open Wednesday to Saturday 12 to 6pm and Sunday 1 to 5pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. Its site also says everything listed is in stock and that it takes no pre-orders, which matters if you are used to ordering ahead.

## Where are the best techno record shops in the rest of Europe?

Our Europe guide, linked at the end, takes the continent in full. Here are the dance specialists we could verify beyond Berlin, Benelux and the UK.

### Kompakt (Cologne)

The Vinyl Factory's 2017 profile says the shop opened on 1 March 1993 as the Cologne outpost of Frankfurt's Delirium, run by Wolfgang Voigt, Reinhard Voigt, Jörg Burger and Jürgen Paape, with the label following five years later. It lists Werderstrasse 15 to 19.

A [2024 Cologne guide](https://koeln.mitvergnuegen.com/2024/plattenlaeden/) gives hours of Tuesday to Friday 1 to 7pm and Saturday noon to 5pm. For the label, read our [Kompakt history](/blog/kompakt-records-cologne-history).

### Syncrophone (Paris)

Time Out Paris, in an April 2023 listing, says Syncrophone has run since 2005 at 6 rue des Taillandiers, is a label, distributor and shop at once, and that about 80 percent of its stock is electronic. A [July 2026 Keep Them Spinning guide](https://keepthemspinning.com/blogs/record-stores/best-record-stores-paris) lists it at number 4 rather than 6, so confirm the address before visiting.

### Yoyaku (Paris)

Yoyaku is a Paris label, distributor and shop. [Resident Advisor reported on 17 February 2025](https://ra.co/news/82140) that it was going "100 percent" vinyl, deleting its online store, and that it was founded in 2015, opened its first store in 2016 and a sister store, Chapelle XIV, in 2020. [Trommel's 2018 profile](https://trommelmusic.com/featured/yoyakus-patient-rise-to-the-vanguard-of-underground-music/) counted eleven labels and sublabels with in-house mastering and distribution, and [FACT covered the 2016 shop launch](https://factmag.com/2016/02/18/yoyaku-dance-record-shop-paris).

We could not confirm the current shop address or hours, so treat Yoyaku as a name to check, not a ready-made itinerary.

### Snickars Records (Stockholm)

Snickars' [own site](https://snickarsrecords.com/) says DJ Mika Snickars started it in 1995, that it has sold online since 2005, and that its main focus is "black and dance music": soul, funk, disco, hip-hop, techno and house. It lists Hökens gata 11, open Wednesday to Friday 12 to 7pm and Saturday 12 to 5pm.

It says its bargain bins exist only in the physical shop, and that live gigs usually run on Saturdays between 2 and 5pm. A July 2026 [Keep Them Spinning guide](https://keepthemspinning.com/blogs/record-stores/best-record-stores-stockholm) rates it 4.8 from 53 Google reviews.

### Discos Paradiso (Barcelona)

The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile says Gerad Condemines and Arnau Farrés opened Discos Paradiso in the Raval in April 2010, and quotes Sónar saying that if an electronica scene exists in Barcelona, this shop is its epicentre. The shop's [own site](https://www.discosparadiso.com/) lists Carrer de Ferlandina 39, free in-store pickup and free delivery within Spain from 100 euros.

The site gives no opening hours, and the only third-party listing we opened, [Time Out Barcelona](https://www.timeout.com/barcelona/shopping/discos-paradiso), is dated 2013.

## Which techno and house record shops are in the US and Japan?

### Gramaphone Records (Chicago)

[Wikipedia says](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramaphone_Records) Gramaphone opened in 1969, first sold folk, jazz and blues, was selling house music by the 1980s, and is now owned by DJ Michael Serafini. [Mixmag reported in January 2020](https://mixmag.net/feature/50-years-of-gramaphone-records-chicago) on its 50th birthday party at Metro and Smartbar with Ron Trent and Steve "Silk" Hurley.

A [July 2026 Keep Them Spinning guide](https://keepthemspinning.com/blogs/record-stores/best-record-stores-chicago) lists it at 2843 N Clark St, which matches the number on the awning in our hero photo, taken in November 2025.

### Somewhere in Detroit, the Submerge outlet (Detroit)

Submerge, the cooperative behind Underground Resistance, says in its [FAQ](https://submerge.com/faq) that it has a Somewhere in Detroit Outlet store, and that visits need an appointment: "No appointment, no entry." The FAQ gives no street address and no hours.

Our [Detroit techno guide](/blog/detroit-techno-guide-2026-clubs-labels-history) covers the museum, the appointment process and the closure of Spot Lite and its record shop in June 2026.

### A-1 Record Shop (New York)

[EV Grieve's April 2025 post](https://evgrieve.com/2025/04/a-record-store-day-appreciation-of-east.html) lists A-1 at 439 E. Sixth Street, between Avenue A and First Avenue, and says the shop turns 30 in 2026. The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile says it was opened in 1996 by Isaac Kosman and stocks second-hand dance floor records, with a growing emphasis on house and techno.

It names Ron Morelli of L.I.E.S. among former counter staff.

### Disk Union Shibuya Club Music Shop (Tokyo)

Disk Union's group history dates to 1967, per the Vinyl Factory. Its Shibuya club music branch is on the fourth floor of the Antenna 21 Building in Udagawa-cho, according to a [September 2025 Ella Records guide](https://ellarecords.jp/en/blogs/record-store-map/disk-union-shibuya-club-music-shop), which puts the stock at more than 25,000 records across hip-hop, R&B, techno and house, with DJ gear, open noon to 8pm.

## Which city has the best dance music record shops?

On our verified list, Berlin has three entries and Paris two, with 13 other cities having one each. Shop counts are not quality, so we do not crown a winner. The answer depends on what you want. For techno and dub, Berlin: The Berliner's 2026 guide calls Hard Wax mostly dub and techno. For house plus label and distribution culture, Amsterdam and Rotterdam, where Rush Hour and Clone both run distribution. For second-hand digging, Snickars and Spacehall both say they buy records, and Snickars also trades.

## Where to buy techno and house vinyl online?

Many shops above sell worldwide from their own webshops, and there are dedicated stores and marketplaces too. Here is what we could verify.

### Webshops run by the shops above

- **Hardwax.com** lists store sections for Electronic, Disco, Reggae-Dub, Wave and Outernational, plus tags such as Basic Channel, Detroit and Techno, and "This Week" and "Back In Stock" views.
- **Rush Hour's webshop** has a Used Records section and a "We Buy Records" page, according to its navigation.
- **Clone.nl** runs its own distribution and sells store-only releases.
- **Idle Hands, Snickars and Spacehall** each also sell through [Discogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discogs), per their own sites.

### Juno and Bleep

[Wikipedia's Juno Records entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Records) says it was founded in 1996 by Richard Atherton and Sharon Boyd as an information site and became an online store in 1997. It launched Juno Distribution in 2008, a wholesale division in 2023, and Juno Download shut on 1 June 2026, according to the same entry. Juno's own site blocked our automated check, so we could not confirm current stock.

[Bleep was launched on 14 January 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleep.com) as a download store for Warp Records, then merged with Warpmart in November 2008, becoming a mail order retailer of vinyl, cassettes and CDs as well as downloads.

### Discogs and Bandcamp

Discogs was founded in 2000 by Kevin Lewandowski and launched its marketplace in 2005, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discogs), which says its database had over 19 million user-submitted release listings in 2026. It is a marketplace of independent sellers, so condition and shipping vary.

[Bandcamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandcamp), founded in 2008, offers vinyl pressing to artists, where fans pre-order pressings instead of artists funding them upfront. Epic Games bought it in 2022 and Songtradr took it over in 2023. On 17 March 2026, [Mixmag reported](https://mixmag.net/read/physical-bandcamp-sales-to-count-towards-uk-charts-news) that physical sales for clients of The Pack Smart Group could now count towards UK chart positions.

### Established stores we could not open

Deejay.de, Decks.de, Boomkat, Phonica's webshop and Juno's own site refused our automated requests or sat behind a bot check, so we have no verified detail to add. Check them directly.

## How do DJs actually buy vinyl?

- **Distribution matters.** Rush Hour says its distribution arm exists to push its label releases to independent retailers, and Juno, Syncrophone and Yoyaku all run distribution too. A shop can hold records you cannot get elsewhere because its sister company distributes them.
- **Pre-orders vary.** Rush Hour's site has a "Forthcoming Titles" section, while Idle Hands says it sells only vinyl in stock. Bandcamp lets fans pre-order pressings.
- **Cutting houses sit close to shops.** Mixmag notes that the old Hard Wax building also housed the cutting house Dubplates & Mastering, which operated for 28 years.
- **Pressing queues are long.** Our own report on [pressing plant capacity](/blog/vinyl-pressing-plant-capacity-crisis) says independent labels still wait 6 to 9 months, which is why records sell out and why shop stock is not always on sale in your city.
- **Selling is part of it.** Rush Hour has a "We Buy Records!" page, and Snickars and Spacehall say they buy records.

## What we could not verify

We left several names off the list on purpose.

- **Can Records, Copenhagen.** The Vinyl Factory's 2016 profile describes early electronic, ambient, new wave and disco stock at Tullinsgade 5. That is a more eclectic offer than a dance-floor specialist, and we did not open a 2025 or 2026 source, so we did not include it.
- **Public Possession, Munich.** [Mixmag reported in March 2023](https://mixmag.net/read/munich-record-shop-public-possession-will-close-forced-out-new-landlords-label-news) that it would close its Klenzestraße 16 shop that month after the owners said they were forced out by a landlord change. A [October 2024 Textilwirtschaft article](https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/retail/public-possession-aktivert-den-muenchner-luitpoldblock-93532) describes a new Luitpoldblock store of about 90 square metres mixing music, merchandise, art and fashion, not clearly a record shop, so we dropped it.
- **Technique, Tokyo; Superfly, Paris; Piccadilly Records, Manchester.** Technique appeared as closed in search listings we did not open, Superfly is a vintage specialist, and Piccadilly is a broad shop. None fit with a source we could stand behind.
- **The Financial Times list.** It is paywalled, and the Time Out summary names no dance specialists among its European picks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best techno record shop in the world?

There is no official ranking. Hard Wax in Berlin, opened in 1989 by Mark Ernestus of Basic Channel, is the benchmark The Vinyl Factory used when it compared other cities' shops. It paired Hard Wax with Phonica in London and Rush Hour in Amsterdam as its yardsticks.

### Is Hard Wax still open in 2026?

Yes. Its own contact page lists Köpenicker Str. 70 in Berlin Mitte and hours of Monday to Saturday, 15:00 to 20:00, and The Berliner's August 2026 guide includes it. It moved there from Kreuzberg on 30 October 2023.

### Where can you buy techno vinyl online?

Many of the shops above sell online, including Hard Wax, Rush Hour, Clone, OYE and Idle Hands, and Juno, Bleep, Discogs and Bandcamp are also established options. Yoyaku deleted its online store in early 2025 per Resident Advisor. Deejay.de, Decks.de and Boomkat blocked our checks.

### Is Juno still a good place to buy vinyl?

Juno was founded in 1996 and became an online store in 1997, according to Wikipedia, and it launched a wholesale arm in 2023. Its download store, Juno Download, shut on 1 June 2026. We could not open Juno's own site, so check current stock there.

### Are record shops in Berlin better than elsewhere?

Berlin has three entries on our verified list, the most of any city, but that is not a quality ranking. Amsterdam, Rotterdam and Antwerp give the Low Countries three more, and Paris has two.

### What is the difference between Discogs and a record shop?

Discogs is a marketplace of independent sellers plus a database, so condition and shipping vary by seller. A shop curates its own stock, and several shops here also sell through Discogs.

### Can you visit the Detroit techno record store?

Only by appointment. Submerge's FAQ says its Somewhere in Detroit outlet needs one and gives no address, so email first. Our Detroit guide explains the process and the museum next door.

### Is vinyl still growing in 2026?

Yes. Our own reporting on 2025 figures, citing IFPI, puts global vinyl revenue growth at 13.7 percent, which is part of why plants and shops are busy.

### Why do some records sell out so fast?

Pressing queues are long. Our own report says independent labels still wait 6 to 9 months for pressing slots, so a limited run can be gone before a shop in your city ever sees it.
