Amsterdam Dance Event turned 30 this week, and with one night to go (it closes on Sunday 25 October) the shape of the edition is already clear. It was the biggest programme in ADE's history on paper, it opened with a Jean-Michel Jarre concert that got a mixed review, its conference argued about AI, consolidation and who owns the future of independent music, and it was shadowed by a run of booking rows that exposed how little control the festival says it has over what happens in its name.

This is a wrap-up written on Saturday 24 October 2026, before the closing night and before ADE has published any final figures. We read ADE's own press releases and programme pages, trade and local press, and the one full show review that exists so far, and we say plainly where the record is thin. Post-event coverage was very sparse on the day we looked, so the section on what the edition will be remembered for is our reading of the evidence, not a verdict handed down by the industry. We will update the piece when ADE publishes its numbers.

**The short version:** ADE 2026 was billed as 3,600-plus artists, 1,300-plus events and 300-plus venues across five days. The headline moments were Jarre's Oxygène 50th anniversary opening show on 21 October (reviewed as stylish at the start and weak at the end), Pasquale Rotella's ADE Pro keynote and the Map Point Summit he announced with ADE for May 2027 in Las Vegas, and a string of withdrawals: a Russian DJ removed from a label showcase after an anti-Ukraine message, a promoter pair cut from the official programme over a booking, and a Palestinian DJ pulling out of a panel. No 2026 attendance figure exists yet. The 2025 edition was reported at a record 600,000 visitors.

## How big was ADE 2026?

On paper, bigger than any edition before it, though ADE has not published attendance. The official programme figures, all from ADE's own releases, rose through the year:

| Release | Artists | Events | Venues |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 June 2026, first lineup | 250 plus announced, about 8 per cent of an expected 3,000 plus | not stated | 200 plus |
| 1 September 2026, second wave | 1,500 plus announced, with 3,300 plus expected | not stated | 200 plus |
| 6 October 2026, programme complete | 3,600 plus | 1,300 plus | 300 plus |

For comparison, ADE reported nearly 3,500 artists, more than 1,200 events and initiatives, more than 300 locations and a record 600,000 visitors in 2025. For 2024 it reported more than 1,000 events, more than 200 venues and more than 3,000 performing artists, with no visitor figure on the page we read.

Two cautions on those numbers. First, they count programme listings, not people, so a rise from nearly 3,500 to 3,600-plus artists is a small step, not a leap. Second, the totals drifted between releases (3,000 plus in June, 3,300 plus in September, 3,600 plus in October), which is normal for a programme that keeps filling until the week itself but means no single early figure should be quoted as final.

ADE's own anniversary copy frames the growth since 1996 as a move from 300 professional delegates and three nighttime venues to around 10,000 delegates and 300 venues. Another ADE page cites "11,000 plus" music professionals and businesses, so treat the delegate number as approximate. If you read our preview, one correction: the first wave of 250-plus artists was announced on 30 June, not in early September. The early September release was the second wave. The [preview](/blog/ade-2026-30th-anniversary) has been fixed.

ADE is run by a not-for-profit foundation, with BumaStemra as founding partner and Heineken as main partner. It has no main stage and no single gate; it is a citywide programme of club nights, concerts, a conference and art projects, which is why an attendance number is always an estimate.

## How did the opening night go? Jarre at AFAS Live

Reviewed with mixed feelings. Jean-Michel Jarre was ADE's guest of honour and played "Oxygène & Beyond: A Sonic Journey Celebrating 30 Years of ADE" at AFAS Live, presented by MOJO and Insomniac. ADE, MOJO and Amsterdam Now list the concert on Wednesday 21 October, and MOJO's page lists no other date. The show marked both ADE's 30 years and the 50th anniversary of Oxygène, which we covered in [Oxygène at 50](/blog/jean-michel-jarre-oxygene-50-years-ade).

The only full review we could find is by Atze de Vrieze for VPRO's 3voor12. A note on the date: the 3voor12 page carries a "Thursday 22 October" date line and describes the concert as happening that evening, which does not match the 21 October date everywhere else. We cannot resolve that from public pages, so we cite the review for what it says about the show, not for its date.

According to the review:

- **What worked.** The opening, Oxygène 2, was the most stylish moment, with camera glasses showing Jarre's hands at work. Révolution Industrielle 2, dedicated to Kavinsky, Équinoxe 4 and Brutalism were singled out as stronger moments. The laser harp appeared, in the encore, which the reviewer called a nice item for the Jarre bingo card.
- **The guests.** Armin van Buuren joined on Stardust, which the reviewer called an all-out trance banger and praised him as a great ambassador for ADE. Adiescar Chase appeared on The Time Machine in the encore.
- **What did not.** The reviewer found the music hard to dance to and closer to film-chase music than seduction, said Jarre used too many ideas and did not trust repetition, and said the set was largely the same as his summer show. The Oxygène 4 remix was called a disaster and the low point. Jarre's remarks about new technology, and the partly AI-made robot visuals, drew only a small round of applause.

That is one reviewer's view, and it is a Dutch-language review of a seated concert by an artist whose strongest case is the catalogue. It does not tell you how the room felt. It does tell you that the anniversary headline was not universally a triumph.

## Swan Lake Remixed, EINDER and the Gashouder return

The cultural opening of the week was built around three things, and we found no reviews of any of them.

**Swan Lake Remixed for ADE.** Het Nationale Ballet and the ISH Dance Collective staged a one-hour piece at Gashouder on 21 October, with 35 Dutch National Ballet dancers and 12 ISH performers, music by Rik Ronner and direction by Ernst Meisner and Marco Gerris, according to ADE's announcement. ADE's page showed it as sold out at EUR 54.

**EINDER.** Boris Acket and Making Waves built a textile installation of roughly 80 by 25 metres, moved by fans, in De Centrale Markthal. It runs from 21 October to 1 November, with the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra playing Beethoven's Fifth beneath it on the opening night and live sets through the week, including Djrum (Thursday) and choreographer Kiani del Valle (Friday and Saturday), per ADE. Seated concerts started from EUR 27.

**Gashouder.** The venue was closed for renovation from January 2026 and came back for ADE in a pre-opening season (Beatportal says its full reopening is in November). Mixmag NL's preview of the programme had Armin van Buuren's night on Wednesday, an Eric Prydz special performance and an I Hate Models and Nico Moreno night on Thursday, Job Jobse's biggest show to date on Friday, Franky Rizardo's FLOW with Cloonee on Saturday and Josh Baker closing on Sunday. That is a preview, not a report of how those nights went.

## What did ADE Pro argue about?

ADE Pro, the conference, ran from 21 to 24 October at Felix Meritis, the Andaz hotel and Rosewood Amsterdam. The Pro Pass sold out, and ADE's site now invites pre-registration for 2027. ADE set three themes in advance: market consolidation and the future of independence, rebuilding the relationship with Big Tech (streaming payouts, AI training without consent, alternatives such as fair-trade streaming models), and collective action in the face of conflict and pressure on cultural spaces. Focus regions were Brazil, the Middle East and North Africa, and Sub-Saharan Africa.

A caveat that matters: trade-press recaps of the conference sessions were not retrievable on the day we looked, so apart from one keynote we know what ADE scheduled, not what was said on stage. The programme gives a clear picture of what the industry chose to talk about:

- **AI.** Sessions included an AI platforms roundtable with Splice, SoundCloud, NVIDIA, Deezer, Believe and Image-Line, a Suno Insider Knowledge session with Paul Sinclair and Todd Terry, "Artists on AI: A No-BS Roundtable", and BumaStemra sessions on creators' rights and AI policy. If you want the background to those debates, see our reporting on [AI uploads flooding Deezer](/blog/deezer-ai-uploads-streaming-flood), [Spotify's AI persona badge and the IFPI fraud numbers](/blog/spotify-ai-persona-badge-ifpi-fraud), [Bandcamp's AI ban six months on](/blog/bandcamp-ai-ban-six-months-on) and [the Michael Smith streaming fraud sentencing](/blog/michael-smith-ai-streaming-fraud-sentencing).
- **Independence and money.** "Music & Machines" put Armin van Buuren, Jarre, Lady Starlight and JakoJako on one stage; Mau P spoke about building an independent business; Sam Feldt and Tinie Tempah discussed music as an investment. Our [independent label survival study](/blog/independent-label-survival-study) and [Ableton at 25](/blog/ableton-25-years-independence-gerhard-behles) cover the ground those talks were working on.
- **Clubs.** BumaStemra programmed a roundtable on the global decline of nightclubs, titled "The Lights Are Going Out". We track the numbers behind that title in our [Europe club closures tracker](/blog/europe-club-closures-tracker) and in [why people go out less](/blog/why-people-go-out-less-nightlife-data-2026).
- **Wellbeing and sustainability.** ADE Green ran on Friday 23 October at Felix Meritis, with sessions on low-impact touring, funding for scenes under pressure in places such as Kyiv, Ramallah and Beirut, and the ethics of drugs and hedonism. Afrojack appeared in a mental wellbeing session with MIND Us.
- **A new index.** ADE and the research group VibeLab scheduled the launch of a Nighttime Culture Index at ADE Pro: six cities (Amsterdam, Lagos, New York, Sao Paulo, Seoul, Sydney), six dimensions, a dashboard and a report, as the first year of a three-year partnership. It measures cultural vitality, not economic impact. We did not find a published launch write-up.

**The one confirmed keynote.** Pasquale Rotella, founder of Insomniac and the creator of EDC, was ADE Pro's final headline speaker on 22 October, in conversation with Billboard Dance's Katie Bain. He used the keynote to announce, with ADE, the Map Point Summit: a new three-day conference at Virgin Hotels Las Vegas in May 2027, between the two weekends of EDC Las Vegas, for independent promoters, artists, creators and industry. Speakers, programme and ticketing are still to come, per Beatportal and Electronic Groove. Rotella's stated themes were supporting independent promoters, sharing knowledge and giving the next generation opportunities.

## Gear and tech news from ADE week

Gear news was thin. The main launch was Korg's phase8 Companion App, previewed publicly on Friday 23 October in a session with the phase8 product lead and software engineer. Beatportal and MusicTech describe it as a browser-based tool for global parameter control, step sequencing, modulation and random sequence generation for the phase8 acoustic synthesizer. No release date or price was given on the pages we read. Korg also ran a free Gear Test Lab at Het Ketelhuis from 21 to 23 October. We found no new product from Native Instruments, Ableton, AlphaTheta or Roland tied to ADE, and no industry report published for ADE week.

## What were the rows and cancellations?

Four stories ran alongside the music. Each is summarised from what outlets reported, with their sources, and none should be read as more than that.

| Story | What was reported | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| Russian DJ dropped from a showcase | Alexander Komarov was removed from an Interplay Records showcase at Bacan on 23 October after a message to Ukrainian DJ Vadim Bonkrashkov, which called him a Nazi and wished death on him and on Ukraine, circulated on Reddit (RA, NL Times, DutchReview). Komarov confirmed to Algemeen Dagblad that it happened. | ADE said bookings are made by individual labels and collectives and can change at short notice, and that he is no longer on the list. None of the three outlets says who made the decision. Ukraine's honorary consul for the dance music scene urged further action, including a visa review. We found no visa decision. |
| Promoters cut from the programme | ADE withdrew the 21 October Komplott x Overgave party at Panama from its official programme on 13 September because the promoters' response to criticism over booking the French DJ Shlomo conflicted with ADE's values (Beatportal, Stereogum). Shlomo denies the allegations against him, which include sexual misconduct, and has called them a slander campaign. A criminal complaint has been reported in Paris. | Stereogum reported the Panama event was cancelled and replaced with a different lineup. Dutch DJs Xamuel and Within pulled out earlier. |
| Panel withdrawal | The Palestinian DJ Sama' Abdulhadi withdrew from an ADE panel on music and resistance, saying it would be disingenuous to speak about resistance while the festival books Israeli artists, some of whom she says served in the Israeli military (Mixmag, 21 October). She said she does not call for excluding artists for being Israeli. | Mixmag reported that ADE had not responded at the time of publication. We found no later statement. |
| Sona allegations | Het Parool reported on 17 October that 12 of 23 women it spoke to allege sexual misconduct by Omid Kahali, cofounder of the Dutch promoter Sona (RA, Mixmag). Kahali denies everything and says no police complaint exists. Sona announced his permanent exit on 14 October and says an independent integrity investigation is under way. | These are allegations only; no finding has been reported. RA and Mixmag do not link the story to ADE. Sona's No Art and GOLFOS events in Zaandam on 24 and 25 October are ADE-week events, according to local outlet De Orkaan. |

Two points of context. First, the Komarov and Komplott cases both turn on the same structural fact, which ADE states itself: it runs the platform and the conference, but organisers, labels and collectives decide who plays at their own events. ADE removed an event from its official programme in one case and said it has no insight into bookings in the other. That tension, a festival with values and a programme it does not fully control, is the most useful way to read the week.

Second, we found no verified reports of police incidents, drug emergencies, overcrowding or strikes for 21 to 24 October 2026. That is an absence in what we could find, not proof that nothing happened. For rules, the Dutch hospitality association KHN summarised the city's conditions for ADE dance events as including a wall-fixed drugs safe, free drinking water, certified security, trained first aid and communicated house rules, with the municipality inspecting during the week.

## What will ADE 2026 be remembered for?

This section is our reading, built from the facts above. Four threads stand out, and a fifth is in the background.

1. **The anniversary as a ritual.** Thirty years, a guest of honour whose album is 50, a cultural opening with a national ballet company and a 25-metre textile. ADE used its birthday to claim a place alongside institutions, and the Jarre review shows the risk of that: a heritage booking invites a heritage verdict.
2. **The week the independence argument went mainstream.** Rotella's keynote and the Map Point Summit, ADE's three Pro themes and the nightclub-decline roundtable all push the same case: that rave culture needs its own independent industry, and that Big Tech, consolidation and AI are the pressures. ADE and Insomniac creating a new conference in Las Vegas, where Beatportal notes few US electronic music conferences exist, is the concrete outcome so far.
3. **Booking responsibility.** Three of the week's most-discussed stories (Komarov, Komplott x Overgave, Abdulhadi) are about who gets a platform and who decides. Whatever you think of each case, they show that a festival of 1,300-plus events cannot outsource ethics to organisers and also claim moral authority over the lineup.
4. **Scale without a centre.** 3,600-plus artists across 300-plus venues is a programme no one can see more than a sliver of. The pieces that got press attention were the singular ones: one concert, one ballet, one installation, one keynote.
5. **Background: venue churn.** Gashouder returned in a pre-opening season while Tilla Tec, in the former De School building, announced in June that it will close, with a final event in November. Amsterdam's club map is shifting under the festival.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was ADE 2026 and how big was it?

Amsterdam Dance Event 2026 ran from 21 to 25 October, its 30th edition. ADE's final programme release on 6 October listed more than 3,600 artists, more than 1,300 events and more than 300 venues. ADE had not published attendance by 24 October. The 2025 edition was reported at a record 600,000 visitors.

### What was the biggest moment of ADE 2026?

The anniversary opening concert by Jean-Michel Jarre at AFAS Live, "Oxygène & Beyond", with Armin van Buuren as a guest on Stardust, is the most covered show. A review in 3voor12 praised the opening and the trance collaboration but called the music hard to dance to and the Oxygène 4 remix a disaster. The most significant industry moment was Pasquale Rotella's ADE Pro keynote and the announcement of the Map Point Summit.

### What controversies surrounded ADE 2026?

Four stories were reported. Russian DJ Alexander Komarov was removed from an Interplay Records showcase after an anti-Ukraine message. ADE withdrew the Komplott x Overgave party from its official programme in September over a booking. Palestinian DJ Sama' Abdulhadi pulled out of a panel citing the booking of Israeli artists. And Het Parool reported allegations against Sona cofounder Omid Kahali, which he denies, in stories that did not mention ADE.

### What is the Map Point Summit?

A new three-day electronic music conference from Insomniac and ADE, announced on 22 October 2026 and planned for May 2027 at Virgin Hotels Las Vegas, between the two EDC Las Vegas weekends. It is aimed at independent promoters, artists, creators and the industry. Speakers, programme and ticketing had not been announced.

### What did ADE Pro focus on in 2026?

Three themes: market consolidation and the future of independence, rebuilding the relationship with Big Tech (including AI training and streaming payouts), and collective action. Sessions covered AI platforms, independent labels, the decline of nightclubs, mental health and sustainability. ADE Pro ran from 21 to 24 October and the Pro Pass sold out.

### How many people attended ADE 2026?

Not yet known. ADE had not published 2026 attendance or delegate figures when we checked on 24 October. ADE says it has grown from 300 professional delegates in 1996 to around 10,000, and reported 600,000 visitors in 2025.

### When did ADE announce its 2026 lineup?

In three stages: a first wave of 250-plus artists on 30 June 2026, a second wave of 1,500-plus on 1 September, and the complete programme of 3,600-plus artists on 6 October.

### When is ADE 2027?

We did not find dates. ADE's site says Pro Pass pre-registration for 2027 is open, with the lowest rates in pre-sale. Check amsterdam-dance-event.nl for the announcement.

### What did Technics and Korg launch at ADE 2026?

Technics unveiled the SL-1210MKX turntable, available from January 2027 at US$1,699 (GBP1,299), according to Electronic Groove. Korg gave the first public preview of the phase8 Companion App, a browser-based control and sequencing tool for its phase8 acoustic synthesizer, with no price or release date announced.
