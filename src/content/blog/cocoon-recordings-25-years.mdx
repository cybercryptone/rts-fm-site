Twenty-five years is a long time to keep a dance music label standing on its own two feet, without a major distributor calling the shots and without chasing whatever algorithm happens to be paying that quarter. Cocoon Recordings has done it anyway. Sven Väth's Frankfurt imprint marked the milestone with a two-part anniversary release, "25 Years Cocoon Recordings," and the second volume landed on June 5, 2026, closing out a celebration that started with Volume One back in November 2025.

This is less a compilation review than an occasion to ask what actually kept Cocoon relevant for a quarter-century while dozens of labels that started alongside it disappeared, got absorbed, or turned into nostalgia acts. The short answer involves a parking garage in Frankfurt, a Monday night residency on Ibiza, and a stubborn refusal to let the label calcify into one sound.

## What Is Cocoon Recordings, and Why Does the 25th Anniversary Matter?

Cocoon Recordings is the record label arm of Sven Väth's wider Cocoon operation, founded in Frankfurt in 2000 as the recorded extension of the Cocoon party series Väth had already been running since 1997, according to [Resident Advisor's label profile](https://ra.co/labels/352) and the label's own [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoon_Recordings). Over 25 years it has grown into a catalog of more than 300 releases from over 350 artists, distributed to the point of roughly 1.2 million legal downloads worldwide, concentrated in Germany, Japan, the UK, the US, Italy and Spain, per figures published on [Cocoon's own site](https://www.cocoon.net/products/25-years-cocoon-recordings-volume-two).

The anniversary matters because Cocoon is one of the few labels from techno's post-rave, pre-streaming build-out that is still run by the person who founded it, still releasing new music on a regular schedule, and still able to pull in artists as far apart stylistically as Marcel Dettmann and Audion for the same compilation. That kind of continuity is rare in a genre that eats its own history for breakfast.

## Who Is Sven Väth, and How Did the Omen Club Start All of This?

Before there was a label, there was a club. In 1988, Väth partnered with Michael Münzing and Matthias Martinsohn to convert a former discotheque in a Frankfurt parking garage into Omen, a venue that became, by most accounts, one of the foundational rooms of German techno, hosting Carl Cox, Jeff Mills, Chris Liebing and DJ Dag across its ten-year run before closing in October 1998, as documented in a [retrospective on Väth's career from Musiknerd](https://musiknerd.org/en/artists/sven-vaeth/).

Väth had already been building a recording footprint through Eye Q Records, launched in 1992 alongside sister imprints Harthouse and Recycle or Die. When that operation ran into financial trouble, he regrouped around the Cocoon party brand, which he'd started in 1997, and used it as the foundation for both a label and a booking operation. That is the direct lineage from Omen's dancefloor to the catalog number on a Cocoon 12-inch: one continuous career, rebuilt more than once, never handed off to someone else's business plan.

## How Did Cocoon Become More Than Just a Label?

### A Club, a Festival Brand, and a Label Under One Roof

Cocoon Recordings never operated as a standalone catalog business. Väth opened the Cocoon Club in Frankfurt's Fechenheim district on July 18, 2004, and it was voted a top techno venue in reader polls during its run before financial pressure forced its closure at the end of November 2012, per the same Musiknerd account. The label, the club, and the festival arm all fed each other: artists who played the club got signed to the label, and the label's compilations became the soundtrack for the parties.

### Cocoon Ibiza: Two Decades on the White Isle

The Ibiza side of the story is arguably what carried Cocoon's name furthest. Väth brought the brand to Amnesia in 1999, where it held a Monday night residency for the better part of two decades, becoming, according to a [retrospective from 909originals](https://909originals.com/2020/06/29/from-freakshow-to-disco-invaders-cocoon-ibiza-was-the-white-isles-best-party-during-the-2000s/), the island's first underground night to routinely pull thousands of ravers and the first to properly cultivate an after-hours culture there. Cocoon stayed at Amnesia for 19 of its first 20 seasons before shifting to Pacha in 2018 and later returning to the island in newer configurations. It was, by any measure, one of the parties that taught Ibiza to take techno seriously rather than treating it as an import from Berlin and Frankfurt.

## What Made Cocoon's Sound Distinct Inside German Techno?

Cocoon never locked itself into one tempo or one aesthetic, and that flexibility is arguably the label's real signature. Its roster over the years has included minimal-adjacent names like Ricardo Villalobos, house-leaning talents like Guy Gerber, techno stalwarts like Loco Dice and Adam Beyer, and left-field electronic acts like Legowelt and Stefan Goldmann, spanning house, techno, minimal and tech house without ever feeling like a label chasing trends, per the artist roster referenced on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoon_Recordings) and [RA](https://ra.co/labels/352). That range is exactly why a 25th-anniversary compilation can credibly put Marcel Dettmann's industrial-leaning "Magnet" next to Audion's acid-flecked "Chides" and Roman Flügel's more melodic "Secret Service" without the tracklist feeling incoherent. It reflects a label built around a scene and a taste, not a genre formula.

## What's Actually on "25 Years Cocoon Recordings"?

### Volume One Set the Tone

Volume One arrived on November 11, 2025, a 15-track survey pulling in Butch, Robag Wruhme, Josh Wink, Guy J, Extrawelt, Harvey McKay, Damiano von Erckert, Raxon and Jonathan Kaspar, according to [Beatportal's coverage of the release](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1154230-cocoon-recordings-celebrates-25-years-with-monumental-anniversary-compilation). In that same piece, Väth described the label in his own words: "Cocoon Recordings was never just a label. It was a cultural space from day one, a home for visionaries. I wanted to create a platform where artists could unfold freely, beyond trends and boundaries."

### Volume Two Closes the Loop

Volume Two followed on June 5, 2026, again running 15 tracks and pressed as a 5x12" vinyl box set with a spectral-reflective foil finish, alongside a digital release, as listed on [Cocoon Recordings' Bandcamp page](https://cocoonrecordings.bandcamp.com/album/25-years-cocoon-recordings-volume-two) and the [Beatport release listing](https://www.beatport.com/release/25-years-cocoon-recordings-volume-two/6916709). The tracklist reads like a reunion of eras: Marcel Dettmann's "Magnet," Audion's "Chides," Roman Flügel's "Secret Service," André Galluzzi teaming with Gregor Tresher on "Strahlung," Pig&Dan joining Funk D'Void on "Overdose," plus Petar Dundov, Secret Cinema, Ricardo Tobar, Dana Ruh, Emanuel Satie, Krystal Klear, Fango, Daniel Stefanik, Matthias Schildger, and Awen with Denis Horvat and Floyd Lavine. It's a lineup that stretches from Cocoon's minimal-techno-heavy 2000s reputation through to producers who came up a decade or more later, which is precisely the point of putting "25 years" in the title rather than "greatest hits."

## Why Does an Artist-Run Label Like This Still Matter in 2026?

Because ownership is the whole story. A label that has stayed in the hands of the person who started it, for 25 years, is increasingly unusual in an industry where independence on paper often masks major-label-owned distribution underneath. As RTS.FM covered in [our look at the 44% market-share shift toward independent labels](/blog/independent-labels-market-share-2026), the headline number for indie ownership has climbed, but a lot of that growth sits on infrastructure quietly owned by the majors. Cocoon is a reminder of what the older, plainer version of independence looked like: a founder-DJ, a scene he built himself, and a catalog he still controls.

That's also the model RTS.FM has run on since 2006, as a non-commercial, artist-run radio station and label operating outside the major-label ecosystem entirely. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was Cocoon Recordings founded?

Cocoon Recordings was founded in 2000 by Sven Väth in Frankfurt, Germany, as the label extension of the Cocoon party brand he had launched in 1997. That makes 2025 and 2026 the label's 25th-anniversary period, which it marked with its two-volume compilation.

### What is the connection between Sven Väth's Omen club and Cocoon?

Omen was the Frankfurt club Väth co-ran from 1988 to 1998 with Michael Münzing and Matthias Martinsohn, and it's where he built his reputation and network as a DJ and promoter. After Omen closed and his earlier label Eye Q Records ran into difficulty, Väth channeled that experience into the Cocoon party series and, eventually, Cocoon Recordings.

### Where can I hear the "25 Years Cocoon Recordings" compilation?

Both volumes are available digitally and on vinyl through Cocoon's own Bandcamp page, with Volume Two also listed on Beatport as a 5x12" box set. Volume One came out November 11, 2025, and Volume Two followed on June 5, 2026.

### Which artists are on Volume Two of the anniversary compilation?

Volume Two features Marcel Dettmann, Audion, Roman Flügel, André Galluzzi, Pig&Dan, Gregor Tresher, Petar Dundov, Secret Cinema, Ricardo Tobar, Dana Ruh, Emanuel Satie, Krystal Klear, Fango, Daniel Stefanik, Matthias Schildger and others, across 15 tracks.

### Where was Cocoon Ibiza held?

Cocoon's Ibiza residency began at Amnesia in 1999 and stayed there for 19 of its first 20 seasons, making it one of the island's defining techno nights of the 2000s. It later moved to Pacha in 2018 and has since appeared at other Ibiza venues in more recent seasons.
