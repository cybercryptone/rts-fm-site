---
title: "Munich Loses BLITZ, One of Germany's Top Techno Rooms"
date: "2026-08-04"
excerpt: "Munich's BLITZ, an Ilian Tape home base since 2017, closes for good after an 80+ artist farewell run, amid Germany's wider club-closure wave."
---

BLITZ, the 600-capacity techno club on Munich's Museumsinsel, is closing for good. The venue's team confirmed the news in February 2026, citing "reasons beyond our control," and staged a four-day farewell run titled "THAT'S ALL FOLKS: CHAPTER 1 CLOSED" from July 31 to August 3, 2026, with more than 80 artists including Marcel Dettmann, Seth Troxler, Ben Klock, DVS1, Todd Terry and Steffi. Nine years after opening in 2017, one of Germany's most respected small-capacity techno rooms is gone, and its team says they are "working very hard to continue our story."

## What was BLITZ, and why did it matter to German techno?

BLITZ opened in April 2017 in a former industrial space attached to the Deutsches Museum on Munich's Museumsinsel, run by a crew with roots in earlier Munich clubs including Bob Beaman, Charlie, Registratur, King and Kong Club. The room held 600 people across two floors, each fitted with a Void Acoustics system, reportedly the largest installation the company had built at the time, including a custom horn design nicknamed the "Blitz horn." A strict no-photography policy on the floor was part of the pitch from day one: dancing first, documentation never.

What made BLITZ matter beyond Munich was its resident roster. The Zenker Brothers (Dario and Marco), Skee Mask and Stenny were all part of the club's resident lineup from early on, alongside Sascha Sibler, Julietta, Glaskin and La Staab, with David Muallem heading the musical program. All three of those headline names, the Zenkers, Skee Mask and Stenny, are core artists on Ilian Tape, the Munich label the Zenker Brothers founded in 2007 that grew from a regional imprint into one of the most respected outposts of rhythmic, rave-inflected techno anywhere in Europe. BLITZ hosted Ilian Tape's anniversary parties nearly every year of its run, effectively functioning as the label's home room, a rare case of a club and a label growing up together in the same city rather than through the usual festival and touring circuit.

That combination, a serious soundsystem, a strict door and floor policy, and a resident cast tied to an internationally respected label, is what pushed BLITZ into conversation with Berlin's bigger-name rooms despite operating at a fraction of the capacity. Guest bookings over the years included Jeff Mills, Richie Hawtin, Ben Klock, Marcel Dettmann, Nina Kraviz, Laurent Garnier, Surgeon, Chris Liebing and Modeselektor, according to the club's own programming history, a lineup roster that most rooms twice its size would envy.

## Why is BLITZ closing, and what happened at the farewell run?

The club's team announced the closure in a statement to [Resident Advisor](https://ra.co/news/84594) in February 2026, writing that "all good things come to an end" and that BLITZ would shut its doors in 2026 for "reasons beyond our control." The operators did not name a landlord dispute, a specific financial figure or a redevelopment plan, the phrasing stayed deliberately general, but they were explicit that the decision was not a choice to walk away from the project voluntarily. As [DJ Mag reported](https://djmag.com/news/munich-club-blitz-announces-permanent-closure-final-run-of-parties), the team framed the closure around the lease they signed "almost ten years ago," when they set out to build "a space rooted in openness, respect and dancing together."

Programming continued through the summer rather than stopping abruptly. July brought a run of special events, including a JAZZFEST x BLITZ crossover on July 17 and a night with Daphni and Shed on July 18, building toward the closing weekend itself. The farewell run, "THAT'S ALL FOLKS: CHAPTER 1 CLOSED," spanned four days from Friday, July 31 through Monday, August 3, 2026, and was billed by the club as the biggest event in its history. The final lineup, confirmed in mid-July according to Resident Advisor's follow-up reporting, ran to roughly 80 to 95 names across the four days, among them Marcel Dettmann, Seth Troxler, Ben Klock, DVS1, Todd Terry, Steffi, Ryan Elliott, tINI, Chez Damier, Freddy K and the Zenker Brothers themselves, plus live sets from HTRK and Stella and The Longos. It was less a closing party than a full survey of the artists who had defined the room's decade.

## What does "Chapter 1 Closed" actually mean for BLITZ's future?

The framing of the farewell run matters. "THAT'S ALL FOLKS" reads as a definitive goodbye, but "CHAPTER 1 CLOSED" is a much more specific piece of language, it treats the Museumsinsel-era BLITZ as one installment of a longer story rather than the whole of it. In their closure statement, the team said they were "working very hard to continue our story," language repeated across the RA and DJ Mag coverage, and DJ Mag noted the team's suggestion that they would "set sail to new shores."

None of the source reporting confirms a signed lease, a location or an opening date for whatever comes next, and nothing here should be read as a formal announcement of a new venue. What the language does confirm is intent: the crew is not dissolving, the brand and the community around it are meant to persist, and the current closure is being positioned as a relocation or reinvention problem rather than an ending for the operators themselves. For a scene that has watched plenty of clubs close with no stated plan to return, that distinction is worth taking at face value while reserving judgment on the details until they exist.

## Is BLITZ's closure part of a bigger pattern in German club culture?

BLITZ is not an isolated case. Germany's club scene, and Berlin's in particular, has been working through a wave of closures that local media has taken to calling Clubsterben, club death. Watergate, one of Berlin's most internationally recognized rooms, closed in January 2025 after 22 years, citing financial pressure from high rents, inflation and rising costs. Salon zur Wilden Renate shut down in January 2026 after a marathon 86-hour farewell party. SchwuZ, Berlin's oldest queer club with a 50-year history, filed for bankruptcy in 2025. The Berlin Clubcommission has warned that a large share of the city's clubs, reports have put the figure as high as 46 percent, were at risk of closing within a year, driven by rising real estate costs, gentrification pressure near former industrial spaces clubs typically occupy, and landlords opting to redevelop rather than renew.

Munich's club economics differ from Berlin's in scale, but the underlying pressures, expensive real estate, the difficulty of finding industrial or semi-industrial spaces zoned for late-night noise, and leases signed a decade ago coming up against a very different rental market today, are structurally similar. BLITZ occupied a distinctive spot in a museum-adjacent building, not the kind of raw industrial shell most techno rooms use, which made its physical setup harder to replicate even before any lease pressure entered the picture. Germany has also moved to formally recognize clubs as cultural and artistic venues under building-code reforms intended to make it harder for developers to displace them, a policy response to exactly this pattern, though the change came too late to help rooms like BLITZ, Watergate or Renate keep their original addresses.

The pattern that connects these closures is not that underground techno has lost its audience. BLITZ's farewell run pulling close to 90 artists and running four full days argues the opposite. It is that the real estate and lease conditions supporting small, artist-run, non-commercial rooms in major German cities have gotten harder to sustain, regardless of a club's reputation or booking pedigree.

Whatever comes next for the BLITZ crew, the closure removes one of the few rooms where a resident cast this tied to a working label could headline its own home turf night after night. For a scene built on exactly that kind of continuity, that is the harder loss to replace than any single address.
