Italian producer and vinyl DJ SHKEDUL plays a four-hour set at RNDM in Moscow on Saturday, 24 October 2026, with doors at 23:00, in what the organisers present as his first visit to Russia. The night is presented by ЭКО.СИСТЕМА × ACTION under the title Moscow Venezia, with local support from Boevik and Cobra and a Funktion-One sound system, according to the organisers' announcement and the [official ticket page](https://moscow-venice.ticketscloud.org), where tickets start from 1,500 rubles.

## What is happening on 24 October?

The event is a single night with one headliner. The organisers say SHKEDUL will play for four hours, and that the room will run on Funktion-One. ## Who are Boevik and Cobra?

Both are described in the announcement as local team bosses. Boevik is introduced as a long-standing vinyl enthusiast whose passion for records is "huge and uncompromising", in the organisers' words. Cobra is billed as the duo of Malko and Keepray, presented as explorers of hypnotic, powerful microhouse. We have not found independent profiles of either act, so those descriptions are the organisers' own.

## Who is SHKEDUL?

SHKEDUL is an Italian producer and DJ who plays vinyl and runs the vinyl-only label NORD LTD. His booking profile on the [Kappa FuturFestival website](https://www.kappafuturfestival.it/en/acts/shkedul-b2b-enrico-vivaldi) describes a sound that blends techno, acid, trance and progressive, with hypnotic sets, and says he has played Tresor in Berlin and clubs across Europe and Italy. The same bio claims he released more than ten vinyl records in 2025, all of which sold out within days. That figure comes from his own promotional text, not from an independent count.

A recent record gives a clearer picture of the music. *The Areca E.P.*, made with Enrico Vivaldi, came out on the label Zonate (catalogue number ZON004) as a 12-inch on 14 March 2025, according to a [store listing](https://chatnoir-store.common-ground.io/release/1741944598/shkedul-and-enrico-vivaldi-the-areca-e.p) that shows it sold out. The four tracks run from electro on *Channel Z* to acid-laced techno on *Discodancer* and a steady four-to-the-floor on *Nothing Changes*, which gets an Enrico Vivaldi remix. The ticket page also says Amelie Lens and Laurent Garnier play his tracks. We could not verify that claim independently, so treat it as the promoters' statement.

## Why a vinyl set in a Funktion-One room?

SHKEDUL's profile is built around records and small-run pressings, and the organisers frame the night the same way, with a vinyl-focused local support. Funktion-One is a familiar name in club sound: our look at [why festivals now book hi-fi sound systems](/blog/hifi-sound-systems-festival-lineups-despacio-sunflower) notes that Berghain's main room used a Funktion-One system for 18 years, per Mixmag. For listeners who follow the revival of physical formats, that pairing makes sense, and it sits against a year in which [US vinyl revenue passed $1 billion](/blog/vinyl-sales-record-2025) for the first time since 1983. For Tresor, where SHKEDUL says he has played, see our [guide to the best Berlin clubs in 2026](/blog/best-berlin-clubs-2026-guide).

## What we make of it

This section is RTS.FM's own opinion, not reporting.

**1. A single-headliner, four-hour set is the right format for this kind of artist.** The long set suits hypnotic, acid-leaning techno and gives a record-focused DJ room to build, which is the case the organisers are making. *The counter-argument:* a four-hour solo set puts the whole night on one person, and a first visit leaves the audience unsure what to expect from the room.

**2. The local supports are the other half of the story.** Giving the warm-up to a vinyl DJ and a microhouse duo signals what the night wants to be. *The counter-argument:* we found no independent coverage of either, so the billing rests on the organisers' word.
