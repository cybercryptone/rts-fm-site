Few techno labels get to mark thirty years under the person who started them, and fewer still do it with a festival, a conference session and a Faithless remix in the same calendar year. Drumcode, the Swedish label founded in 1996 by [Adam Beyer](/artists/adam-beyer), has done exactly that in 2026. It started as a small Stockholm outlet for a handful of local producers and grew into one of the most prolific brands in peak-time techno, with its own radio show, its own festival and a catalogue numbered past DC350. This is how that happened, release by release, with the sources we could check.

## From Planet Rhythm to Drumcode 01

Before Drumcode there was Planet Rhythm. According to Beyer's profile on Discogs, he co-founded that Swedish label, worked in its record shop and released his first album there in 1996, then decided that year to sell his share, because he had started experimenting with rawer, more distorted sounds. The same profile gives the founding of Drumcode as 1996, and our own [label survival study](/blog/independent-label-survival-study) records it as a Stockholm label from that year. [Wikipedia's label entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumcode_Records) agrees on the year and describes the imprint as a Swedish techno label managed by Beyer.

The first record, catalogued as DRUMCODE 01 (DC01), is a 1996 twelve-inch credited to Adam Beyer and Lenk, a Swedish duo according to Discogs, with four untitled tracks. The numbering that followed shows how quickly the label found its feet. Discogs lists DC03 as RND's "Codebase" in 1996, DC04 as Cari Lekebusch's "Råhångel Me" in 1997, and DC08 as Oliver Ho's "Chasm EP" the same year. By 1998 DC13 was a twelve-inch credited to [Marco Carola](/artists/marco-carola) and Beyer, an early sign that the roster was never going to stay entirely Swedish.

Discogs says as much in its label profile, which is written by contributors rather than the label itself: Drumcode was at first an almost entirely Swedish operation built around local colleagues such as Jesper Dahlbäck, Cari Lekebusch, Thomas Krome, Fredrick Almquist and Joel Mull, with international guests added from time to time. We treat that description as a useful summary, not a primary source, but the catalogue entries above back it up.

## Sub-labels and the Swedish sound

Beyer did not keep all of his ideas on the main label. Discogs lists Code Red as an early sub-label, with Code Red 05, 06 and 07 appearing in 1998 from Joel Mull and Beyer himself. Truesoul followed in 2002, the imprint on which Beyer put out his fourth album, *Ignition Key*, and Mad Eye in 2004, according to Discogs. Wikipedia's entry on Beyer lists the same set of labels: Drumcode, Truesoul, Code Red and Mad Eye.

Truesoul was still visible in 2018, when one of the four stages at the first Drumcode Festival was given over to it, which We Rave You described as Drumcode's sister label. For a comparison with another founder-run label that built a family of imprints, see our [Cocoon Recordings timeline](/blog/cocoon-recordings-history-timeline).

In 2006 the label marked its first decade with a compilation called *Swedish Silver: 10 Years of Drumcode*, released on double twelve-inch and digitally under the catalogue code DC10YEARS, per Discogs. A sequel, *Swedish Silver Vol. 2*, arrived in 2013 as DC107, with Ida Engberg, Joel Mull, Cari Lekebusch and Beyer among the names on the vinyl split. The pair of compilations bookend the period in which Drumcode was still positioned as a Swedish scene.

## Going global: the 2007 to 2013 roster

Around the end of 2006 the label's centre of gravity moved. Discogs dates the shift to a release by Pär Grindvik and describes a turn toward minimal and a wider international roster, with [Perc](/artists/perc) among the artists it names. The catalogue bears that out: Discogs lists Perc's "Sticklebrick" as DC32 in 2007.

From there the pace picks up. DC48 in 2008 was a Steve Lawler and Alex Tepper release. DC50, also 2008, was Joey Beltram's "The Scorpion EP". In 2009 and 2010 the roster that defined the next decade started to form. [Joseph Capriati](/artists/joseph-capriati) appears with DC53, "Sidechains / Kontrol Room", in 2009 and DC68, "Gashouder", in 2010. Alan Fitzpatrick arrives with DC56, "Static / Rubix", in 2009, and Slam is on DC65 in 2010.

Then comes the next wave: Sam Paganini's "Prisma EP" (DC89) in 2012, Nicole Moudaber's "Sonic Language EP" (DC103) the same year, and Bart Skils' "Brave New World EP" (DC104). DC100 in 2012 was Beyer's own, "The Color Out Of Space" with Ida Engberg, a sign of how many catalogue numbers the label had accumulated by then. In 2013 [Len Faki](/artists/len-faki) remixed Beyer and Fitzpatrick's "Human Reason" on DC105, and the label released two artist albums, Moudaber's *Believe* and Capriati's *Self Portrait*. Wikipedia's roster list for the label also names Amelie Lens, Green Velvet, Jay Lumen, Joey Beltram, Maceo Plex, Pan-Pot, Steve Lawler and Victor Calderone, among others.

Discogs' own label profile marks 2013 as the point where Drumcode loosened its stylistic borders, opening to progressive and tech house and moving to a monthly release schedule. That is a contributor's reading, and the label has not said it that way, but the shape of the catalogue is consistent with it.

## The A-Sides series

The label's annual compilation is *A-Sides*, curated by Beyer. Mixmag covered the fifth edition in July 2016, describing it as a 20-track release curated by Beyer to mark Drumcode's 20th anniversary, due out on 1 August of that year, with names including Enrico Sangiuliano, Hertz, Alan Fitzpatrick, Mark Reeve, Bart Skils and Layton Giordani. [EDMTunes reported the 14th volume](https://www.edmtunes.com/2025/12/iconic-drumcodes-series-returns-with-a-sides-vol-14/) on 16 December 2025: 20 tracks, led by Beyer's "We Don't Say Please", with HI-LO, Nicole Moudaber, Kaufmann, Mark Reeve, LUSU and Victor Ruiz among the contributors, and several first-time Drumcode artists. The same piece notes that Wehbba, Charles D and Raxon first appeared on the label through the series. Discogs lists it as DC340.

## Albums, anthems and milestone numbers

Several of the label's milestone numbers are well documented. DC200 in 2019 was Beyer's "Teach Me" with remixes by [Amelie Lens](/artists/amelie-lens). The same year DC216 paired [Maceo Plex](/artists/maceo-plex) with [Josh Wink](/artists/josh-wink) on "Destination Mars". The label's long-running pairing of Beyer and Bart Skils produced "Your Mind" (DC191, 2018), and Beyer, Layton Giordani and Green Velvet released "Space Date" as DC193 the same year. Layton Giordani's 2017 album *Where It Begins* (DC170) and his 2020 follow-up *New Generation* (DC231) are listed on Discogs as full-length releases, as is Enrico Sangiuliano's 2018 album *Biomorph* (DC190).

The 250th release in 2021 was a collaboration with a Detroit pioneer. Discogs lists DC250 as E-Dancer's *Re:Generate*, a 17-track remix package that also came as a numbered four-record vinyl set. E-Dancer is a project of [Kevin Saunderson](/artists/kevin-saunderson), according to Wikipedia. For a label that grew out of the Swedish techno of the 1990s, bringing in one of the Belleville Three for a milestone is a statement of lineage.

The 300th arrived in 2024: Discogs lists DC300 as Mha Iri's "Bombay" EP. In 2025 the label issued Beyer's own album, *Explorer Vol. 1* (DC329), as four twelve-inch records and a 16-track digital edition.

## Harvey McKay and the Drumcode core

No profile of the label's catalogue is complete without mentioning [Harvey McKay](/blog/remembering-harvey-mckay). The Glasgow producer, whose death was reported on 21 May 2026, released "Lost" (DC120) in 2013, "The Illusion Of Control" (DC124) in 2014, "Glasgow Safari" (DC138) in 2015 and "Black Dolphin" (DC182) in 2017, per Discogs. Drumcode's own tribute said his releases left a lasting mark on the label and the wider techno scene. His run of four EPs shows what the label was best at: giving an artist a consistent platform at a time when the catalogue numbers were already in triple digits.

## Drumcode Radio

The weekly radio show is, for many listeners, the label's front door. The Apple Podcasts listing describes *Adam Beyer presents Drumcode* as a weekly show from the label with live sets, studio mixes, artist profiles and previews of upcoming releases. Wikipedia credits Beyer as host. Episode numbers give a sense of the longevity: the Apple Podcasts feed dated DCR847 on 28 October 2026 and had reached DCR848, a Julian Jeweil studio mix, by early November. Beyer's recording from the Mallorca festival was released as DCR827 in June 2026, per EDMTunes. Wikipedia also quotes an audience figure for the show, but we could not trace it to a primary source, so we leave it out.

## The Drumcode Festival

Drumcode announced its own festival on 7 November 2017, Mixmag reported, with a first edition on 18 August 2018 at NDSM, a former shipyard in Amsterdam North, organised with Awakenings. Beyer had a long relationship with the Dutch promoter: Mixmag says he had played more than 75 of its shows since the late 1990s. Our [Awakenings festival guide](/festivals/awakenings-festival) has more on the organisation itself.

The event sold out, according to a review from We Rave You, with four stages (blue, purple, orange for Truesoul, and red) and more than 25 artists. Bart Skils, Pig&Dan, Enrico Sangiuliano, Amelie Lens, Nicole Moudaber, Ida Engberg, Pan-Pot and Joseph Capriati all played, and Beyer closed the red stage.

The format later travelled. In February 2022 EDM House Network reported a Drumcode Festival on Malta from 15 to 19 September, run with Pollen Presents, with a line-up that billed Beyer, [Ben Klock](/artists/ben-klock), [Chris Liebing](/artists/chris-liebing), Maceo Plex and [Richie Hawtin](/artists/richie-hawtin). We could not confirm every later edition from public sources, so we only cover the ones documented above and below.

## Where it stands in 2026

As of 5 November 2026, Drumcode is in its 30th year and the programme has been busy. Electronic Groove reported on 28 November 2025 that the label would hold a three-day festival on Mallorca from 8 to 10 May 2026, presented by Beyer, with Alan Fitzpatrick, Amelie Lens, Bart Skils, Eli Brown, Joel Mull, Joseph Capriati, Pan-Pot and others named in the first line-up. [EDMTunes confirmed](https://www.edmtunes.com/2026/06/adam-beyer-shares-full-mallorca-set-celebrating-30-years-of-drumcode/) the edition went ahead as the inaugural Drumcode Mallorca, adding Joris Voorn and Victor Ruiz to the names and noting that Beyer's longtime alias Cirez D made a special appearance.

In September 2026 the label released *Faithless Reworks*, a two-track package with Beyer remixing "We Come 1" and KASIA reworking "Tarantula", timed to the 30th anniversaries of both Drumcode and the British group, per [Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1651238-drumcode-celebrates-30-years-with-adam-beyer-and-kasia-remixes-of-faithless-classics) on 25 September. On 22 October, the Amsterdam Dance Event programme listed a talk titled "30 Years of Drumcode with Adam Beyer", with Beyer, label director Charlotte Spillerova and Milk&Honey's Jeremy Ford, covering how to run a label for three decades. Our own [ADE 2026 preview](/blog/ade-2026-30th-anniversary) has the wider context for the week.

On the catalogue side, Discogs shows the numbering in 2026 reaching at least DC351, Veerus and Mattia Saviolo's "Move Your Body", alongside DC342 (Mark Reeve, "Body Drops"), the DC4 compilation series and a continuing DCLTD strand of twelve-inch releases. The Drumcode website does not publish a history page or corporate details, so everything above comes from outside sources rather than the label itself, and we make no claim about the label's finances or business structure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who founded Drumcode and when?

Adam Beyer founded Drumcode in 1996, according to Discogs and Wikipedia. Discogs says he started it after selling his share in Planet Rhythm, the Swedish label and record shop he had co-founded. The first release was the DC01 twelve-inch by Adam Beyer and Lenk.

### What was the first Drumcode release?

Discogs catalogues DRUMCODE 01 as a 1996 twelve-inch by Adam Beyer and Lenk, with four untitled tracks. DC03 followed the same year with RND's "Codebase", and Cari Lekebusch's first release for the label was DC04 in 1997.

### What are the Drumcode sub-labels?

Discogs lists Code Red, Truesoul (2002) and Mad Eye (2004) as Beyer's early sub-labels, and DCLTD as the home of limited twelve-inch releases. Wikipedia's Adam Beyer entry names Truesoul, Code Red and Mad Eye as the other labels he runs alongside Drumcode.

### Who has released on Drumcode?

Wikipedia's label entry lists Amelie Lens, Green Velvet, Joey Beltram, Joseph Capriati, Maceo Plex, Marco Carola, Nicole Moudaber, Pan-Pot, Sam Paganini, Slam and Victor Calderone among others. Discogs shows Alan Fitzpatrick, Bart Skils, Layton Giordani, Enrico Sangiuliano and Harvey McKay as regulars with several catalogue numbers each.

### When was the first Drumcode Festival?

The first Drumcode Festival took place on 18 August 2018 at NDSM in Amsterdam, organised with Awakenings and announced by Mixmag on 7 November 2017. It sold out, according to We Rave You, and Beyer closed the red stage.

### How is Drumcode marking its 30th anniversary?

The label held Drumcode Mallorca from 8 to 10 May 2026, released the *Faithless Reworks* package in September 2026 and ran a "30 Years of Drumcode" session at ADE on 22 October. Beyer's set from Mallorca was released as Drumcode Radio 827.

### Is there a Drumcode radio show?

Yes. *Adam Beyer presents Drumcode*, also called Drumcode Radio Live, is a weekly show with live sets and studio mixes from artists on the label. Episodes are numbered DCR, and the numbering had reached DCR848 by early November 2026.
