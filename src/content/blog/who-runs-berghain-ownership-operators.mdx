Berghain is run by a company called Berghain Ostgut GmbH, according to the legal notice on the club's own website, which lists André Jürgens, Lisa Joy Miller and Patrick Thorsten Pfeil as the people who represent it. The club was founded by Norbert Thormann and Michael Teufele, whom Wikipedia describes as its owners, but none of the sources we could open states who holds the shares today. Under one roof the venue is organised as the Berghain main floor, the house-leaning Panorama Bar upstairs, and further rooms the club's site lists as Halle, Säule and Kantine, with the Ostgut Ton label attached to the brand. Nobody is publicly styled "CEO" of Berghain in anything we found, and we are not going to invent one.

That is the short answer to a question we keep seeing in search: who is actually behind the world's most mythologised techno club, and the other clubs at the top of the rankings? This guide sticks to documented public roles. Where the sources disagree or go quiet, we say so.

## Who runs Berghain, and how is the venue organised?

The most reliable source on who operates a German venue is usually its own legal notice, because German law requires one. Berghain's English-language imprint names the company as BERGHAIN OSTGUT GmbH, registered at the Berlin Charlottenburg court under HRB 121291 B, and gives three names under "Vertreten durch" (represented by): [André Jürgens, Lisa Joy Miller and Patrick Thorsten Pfeil](https://www.berghain.berlin/en/imprint/). The notice does not use job titles, does not list shareholders, and does not mention Thormann or Teufele. We read it as a statement of who can legally act for the company, nothing more.

German Wikipedia adds some history, citing the Northdata company database for the corporate name. It says the operating company was founded in 2009 as KulturKubus GmbH, was renamed and restructured in 2012, and that [Thormann and Teufele each held 50 percent of the shares for many years](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghain). It also says Teufele left the managing director role in 2017 and Jürgens took over. We cannot confirm from the sources we opened whether the shareholding has changed since, and the club does not publish it.

As for the venue itself, the [club's homepage](https://www.berghain.berlin/en/) lists separate programmes for Berghain, Panorama Bar, Halle, Säule and Kantine am Berghain, plus a shop and the label. English Wikipedia gives the capacity as 1,500 and says Panorama Bar opened in October 2004 with the main space following that December. We found no official text explaining how Halle and Säule differ in function from the two main dance floors, so we will not guess at it.

## How did Ostgut become Berghain?

The story, as [English Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghain) tells it, starts with Thormann and Teufele running Snax, a male-only fetish night, from 1992. Ostgut followed in a former railway repair depot in Friedrichshain; Wikipedia says it found its permanent home in 1998, while other accounts we read give slightly different years for the opening, so treat the exact date as loose. Ostgut closed in January 2003 when the site was cleared for development, and the same team opened Berghain in 2004 in a former heating plant, which Wikipedia describes as a combined heat and power plant built in 1953. The Commons description of our photo above dates it to the early to mid 1950s, which fits.

Two details complicate the tidy version. First, the building: English Wikipedia says it was rented from the energy company Vattenfall and has been owned outright by the club since 2011. German Wikipedia says the operators later bought it from Vattenfall but gives no year, so "2011" rests on one source. Second, the legal status of the party: German Wikipedia reports that a Berlin-Brandenburg tax court ruled in 2016 that Berghain events count as cultural events, taxed at the reduced 7 percent VAT rate rather than 19 percent, and that the Federal Fiscal Court took the same view in 2020. English-language coverage often compresses this into "a court classed it as a cultural institution". Both describe the same tax question.

## What is Ostgut Ton, and what happened to it?

Ostgut Ton is the label that grew out of the club. Wikipedia says it launched in 2005 with early releases from residents such as Cassy and Ben Klock, and describes it as owned by Berghain. It went quiet after the 2021 compilation Fünfzehn + 1, and its sister agency Ostgut Booking closed the following year. On 30 May 2025 Resident Advisor reported that [the label had relaunched after a four-year hiatus](https://ra.co/news/82895) with the compilation Klubnacht 01, marking its 20th anniversary. RA's piece does not say who owns or runs the label, and the label's own [catalogue site](https://www.ostgut.berlin/de/label) is a release listing without an About text. What the relaunch tells us is that the label is still part of the Berghain universe. Who sits behind it commercially is not something the sources say.

## What do we know about booking, the door and the photo policy?

Very little of it is official. English Wikipedia describes the door as "strict and opaque" and names Sven Marquardt as head bouncer, a role he shares with a career as a photographer. i-D's explainer calls entry seemingly random and notes that the club publishes no criteria; online advice about dressing in black is folklore, not policy. We previously covered the cultural afterlife of that door in our piece on [the Hollywood comedy about getting rejected at Berghain](/blog/berghain-hollywood-comedy-film).

The photo rule is better documented. Both Wikipedia and i-D report that security covers phone cameras with a sticker on entry, and that guests who peel it off to shoot are likely to be ejected. Booking is similarly opaque: residents and guests are published in the programme, but the process that produces it is not described anywhere we could open.

The one recent, well-sourced operational change is sound. Mixmag reported on 18 October 2023 that [Berghain had replaced its main-room Funktion-One Dance Stack](https://mixmag.net/read/berghain-updates-soundsystem-funktion-one-news), which had run since 2005 at about 40 hours a week on average, with a new system whose specification Funktion-One did not disclose. The same piece corrected earlier reports by saying Panorama Bar has never had a Funktion-One system, only DJ monitors. We did not find a sourced 2025 or 2026 structural or legal development at the company; the notable recent news is the Ostgut Ton relaunch and the club's place in DJ Mag's Top 100 Clubs, where Wikipedia lists it 13th in 2024 and 16th in 2025, and [The Berliner](https://www.the-berliner.com/english-news-berlin/berghain-down-to-21-place/) reported it at 21st in 2026, the only Berlin club on the list. Our fuller breakdown of that poll is in [our UNVRS and Berghain Top 100 post](/blog/berghain-unvrs-dj-mag-top-100).

## Who is publicly named behind UNVRS, Ushuaïa and Hï Ibiza?

Ibiza's top-ranked clubs follow a different model from a founder-owned Berlin company. According to Wikipedia's UNVRS entry, [UNVRS is operated by The Night League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNVRS), and the team behind Ushuaïa and Hï Ibiza announced in August 2024 that it would acquire the former Privilege site near San Rafael. The Night League's own site lists three venues in one portfolio: Ushuaïa Ibiza, Hï Ibiza and [UNVRS].

The named executive is Yann Pissenem. The company's [own leadership page](https://www.thenightleague.com/yann-pissenem) calls him "the owner, founder and CEO of The Night League, Ushuaïa Entertainment", and a later version of that bio adds The Ideation Bureau. [Wikipedia's entry on him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yann_Pissenem) gives the same titles, says he founded The Night League in 2001 and launched Ushuaïa in 2008, and says Hï Ibiza opened in 2017 after Space closed. Our own earlier post on the poll described him as CEO of The Night League, which is consistent. What none of these pages spell out is the legal chain: which entity holds which licence, who owns the land under each venue, and whether any outside investors are involved. Those details are not in the public sources we read, so we leave them blank.

One correction to a common assumption: Pacha is not part of that group. The Pacha Group was sold in 2023 to Dubai-based FIVE Holdings. Resident Advisor reported on 14 July 2023 that [FIVE had bought a stake reported at 320 million euros](https://ra.co/news/79296), citing Diario de Ibiza, with the group split so that FIVE took the hotels and nightclubs, including Pacha and Destino in Ibiza, while the previous owner Trilantic Capital Partners kept the Lío brand. A source close to Trilantic confirmed the deal took place but declined to discuss figures, and FIVE declined to comment at that stage. [Mixmag's report](https://mixmag.asia/read/dubai-holdings-company-buys-320-million-stake-in-pacha-group-ibiza-news) says the same, and Wikipedia's [Pacha Group entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pacha_Group) later records the acquisition as October 2023 at 302.5 million euros. Those two figures are a good example of public sources disagreeing, and we do not try to reconcile them. Wikipedia also names Kabir Mulchandani as chairman and Aloki Batra as CEO of the Pacha Group after the deal.

## What about fabric and Tresor?

Two clubs in the poll's orbit show how different "who runs it" can look. At London's fabric, Wikipedia names Keith Reilly and Cameron Leslie as founders when it opened on 29 October 1999, and lists the owner as Fabric Life Limited, after a consortium of that name took the club out of administration in 2010. Islington Council revoked its licence in September 2016 after two drug-related deaths, and an agreement reached on 21 November 2016, which [Resident Advisor covered](https://ra.co/news.aspx?id=37369) alongside London mayor Sadiq Khan's welcome for it, allowed it to reopen under tighter conditions, including photo ID checks and a ban on under 19s at certain hours. The point for our purposes: the operator is a named limited company, the founders are named, and the regulator's conditions are public record. We did not find a current statement of who holds fabric's shares.

Tresor in Berlin is more founder-led in the telling. A [Crack Magazine profile from March 2016](https://crackmagazine.net/article/music/tresors-founders-continue-to-fan-the-flames-of-berlins-nightlife) says Dimitri Hegemann runs Tresor, as well as the smaller Ohm club and the Kraftwerk venue, while Wikipedia calls him its founder. The club opened in March 1991, closed at its original Leipziger Strasse site in April 2005 and reopened in May 2007 in a former power plant, per Wikipedia. Tresor's own About page tells the history without naming anyone, and we could not find a company name in the pages we opened. That is a ten-year-old press description plus a founder entry, not a current corporate record. For where Tresor and the others sit in Berlin today, see our [guide to the best Berlin clubs](/blog/best-berlin-clubs-2026-guide).

## How ownership shapes the dancefloor (our analysis)

Everything above this heading is reporting. What follows is our analysis, and you should weigh it that way.

The clearest pattern is that disclosure tracks business model. A single-site, founder-led club like Berghain or Tresor tends to present itself as a place with a house sound rather than a company, and says little about its structure. A portfolio operator like The Night League presents itself as a brand with a chief executive and a roster of venues. A group that has changed hands, like Pacha, generates press coverage of the deals precisely because a buyer and a seller are involved. None of those choices tells you whether a club is good. They tell you who is accountable to whom.

For the person on the floor, we think the practical effects are about control over a few levers. A founder-run venue can hold the door, the photo rule, the booking policy and its in-house label in one set of hands, which is how a club ends up with a coherent identity and an opaque process. A multi-venue operator can program across rooms and move residencies between them, a rhythm you can see in the season-end run we mapped in [our guide to Ibiza's closing parties](/blog/ibiza-closing-parties-2026). Each model also has a different way of failing: a founder-led room is exposed to one lease and one set of decisions, as we saw in [Wilde Renate's close call](/blog/wilde-renate-berlin-club-history) and in the broader numbers in our [Europe club closures tracker](/blog/europe-club-closures-tracker), while a large operator is exposed to the reputation of its brand across several rooms at once. The recent argument over a buyout of another dance brand, covered in [our Boiler Room and KKR piece](/blog/boiler-room-kkr-boycott), shows what happens when ownership becomes visible to an audience that did not expect to care.

## What we make of it

This section is RTS.FM's own opinion, not reporting.

1. **Berghain's silence about its ownership is a choice, and it is part of the product.** A club that tells you little about its shareholders and less about its door is selling mystery as much as sound. That is legitimate, but it also means the usual accountability channels, such as the legal notice and court records, do the work that a press office would elsewhere. *The strongest counter-argument is that a private company owes the public nothing beyond what German law requires, and that clubbers go to dance, not to read a cap table.*

2. **Publicly named leadership is a better sign than a mythology.** Of the venues here, we trust the ones where a name, a company and a regulatory record can be checked more than the ones where the story is a legend. fabric's named operator and public licence conditions are easier to hold to account than a door policy nobody has written down. *The strongest counter-argument is that a named executive proves nothing about how a night actually feels, and that the most trusted rooms in techno have always been the least explained.*

3. **Ownership should be reported at the role level, and no further.** We would rather leave a blank in the table than fill it with a rumour or a number nobody has confirmed. Where the record is thin, as with Berghain's shareholders or Tresor's company details, the honest answer is to say so. *The strongest counter-argument is that a cautious approach lets opaque structures stay opaque, and that someone should be pushing harder on registers and filings.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who owns Berghain?

Berghain is operated by a company, Berghain Ostgut GmbH, according to the club's legal notice. Wikipedia describes founders Norbert Thormann and Michael Teufele as the owners, and German Wikipedia says each held 50 percent of the shares for many years. The sources we could open do not state who holds the shares today, so we cannot say.

### Who is the CEO of Berghain?

We found no public source that gives Berghain a CEO. The club's legal notice lists André Jürgens, Lisa Joy Miller and Patrick Thorsten Pfeil as the people who represent the company, without job titles. German Wikipedia says Jürgens took over as managing director in 2017.

### Who runs Berghain?

The company named in the club's legal notice is Berghain Ostgut GmbH, represented by André Jürgens, Lisa Joy Miller and Patrick Thorsten Pfeil. The venue includes the main floor, Panorama Bar, Halle, Säule and Kantine, and the Ostgut Ton label is part of the brand.

### Who is Norbert Thormann?

Norbert Thormann is named by Wikipedia as a co-founder of Berghain, together with Michael Teufele, and as one of its owners. They ran the Snax parties before Ostgut and Berghain. His current role in the company is not stated in the club's legal notice or in the sources we read.

### Who is Michael Teufele?

Michael Teufele is named as a co-founder and owner of Berghain alongside Norbert Thormann. German Wikipedia says he left the managing director role in 2017, when André Jürgens took over. Whether he still holds shares is not stated in the sources we read.

### Who is Yann Pissenem?

Yann Pissenem is described on The Night League's own site as the owner, founder and CEO of The Night League and Ushuaïa Entertainment, and a later version of the bio adds The Ideation Bureau. Wikipedia says he founded The Night League in 2001 and launched Ushuaïa in 2008. The companies run Ushuaïa Ibiza, Hï Ibiza and UNVRS.

### Who owns UNVRS?

UNVRS is operated by The Night League according to Wikipedia, and the company's site lists it among its venues. Yann Pissenem is described as the owner, founder and CEO of The Night League. Who holds the land and licences, and whether there are other investors, is not stated in the public sources we read.

### Who owns Pacha Ibiza?

Pacha Ibiza belongs to The Pacha Group, which Dubai-based FIVE Holdings bought in 2023, according to Resident Advisor, Mixmag and Wikipedia. The previous owner, Trilantic Capital Partners, kept the Lío brand. Reports differ on the price, with 320 million euros reported in July 2023 and 302.5 million euros given by Wikipedia.

### Does Berghain have an owner?

Yes in the legal sense: it is a company, Berghain Ostgut GmbH, and Wikipedia describes Norbert Thormann and Michael Teufele as its owners. The public record we could open does not show the present shareholders, so we can say who founded it and who represents it, but not who owns it today.

### Who owns Ostgut Ton?

Wikipedia describes Ostgut Ton as owned by Berghain, and Resident Advisor called it Berghain's in-house label when it relaunched on 30 May 2025. Neither source names a separate owner or manager, and the label's own site does not say.
