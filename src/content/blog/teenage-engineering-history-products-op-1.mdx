## What is Teenage Engineering?

Teenage Engineering is a Swedish hardware and design company based in Stockholm. It started as a maker of unusual portable instruments and became one of the most recognisable names in electronic music hardware, then widened into speakers, cameras, consoles and consumer electronics designed with other companies.

Highsnobiety places the company in Hammarby Sjöstad, a former industrial waterfront in Stockholm, and reports that it employs roughly 100 people and earns around $46 million a year, figures that come from that interview rather than from published accounts. Wikipedia recorded 55 employees in 2019. We could not find a source that says who owns the company, so we do not make a claim about ownership.

## Who founded Teenage Engineering and when?

According to Wikipedia, Teenage Engineering was founded in 2005 by Jesper Kouthoofd, David Eriksson and Jens Rudberg, who had earlier led a games company called Netbabyworld between 1999 and 2003. David Möllerstedt joined later. Kouthoofd is the founder and CEO and is the voice behind the current plan to leave synthesizers. Highsnobiety says he has the resources and experience to run it exactly as he wants.

The games background shows in the products. Many Teenage Engineering instruments feel closer to handheld consoles than studio gear: small screens, bright colour coding, pixel icons and a deliberate, playful limit on features.

## What is the OP-1 and why does it matter?

The OP-1 is the instrument that put the company on the map. Highsnobiety reports that it launched in 2011, remains among the most sought-after items on the Reverb used market, and that Kouthoofd sees it as a rejection of making music on a laptop. 
The OP-1's significance is as much about design as sound. It treated a musical instrument as a piece of industrial design and a collectible, which the company then repeated across other products. The OP-1 Field arrived in 2022 as its successor, with the company describing stereo throughout the signal chain, a 24-hour battery and an aluminium body.

The Pocket Operator range did a different job. Launched at NAMM in 2015 at $59, it brought hardware sequencing to beginners at a fraction of OP-1 prices. Teenage Engineering says the series has sold just shy of a million units, a company figure that has not been independently audited.

## Is Teenage Engineering really stopping making synthesizers?

**Founder and CEO Jesper Kouthoofd told Highsnobiety that Teenage Engineering plans to stop making synthesizers, and that the OP-1 is no exception. He said he told staff during a reorganisation in autumn 2025.** Resident Advisor, Engadget and Gearnews reported the interview on 8 October 2026. It is a stated intention, not a closing-down notice: no end date, list of affected products or support policy has been published, and the company has not issued a formal statement beyond sharing the interview. Gearnews names the OP-1, the OP-XY and the PO series as covered, but Highsnobiety names only the OP-1. Meanwhile the company shipped an OP-XY update on 15 October 2026 and launched the EP-35 amplifier in late October. As of 24 November 2026 we found no formal statement, clarification or walk-back from the company, and the OP-XY and OP-1 Field are still listed for sale on its store. The [full news report](/blog/teenage-engineering-stop-making-synthesizers) sets out what was and was not said.

## What has Teenage Engineering said since?

Not a great deal on the subject itself. Here is what we could verify, with dates:

- **10 October 2026:** Resident Advisor added to its report that the company had opened a 6,000 square foot department store on Lafayette Street in New York, with a soft launch event featuring Lykke Li.
- **15 October 2026:** Teenage Engineering's [news page](https://teenage.engineering/now) lists OP-XY 1.2.0, a major firmware update that adds a drum synthesizer called drumline, a dynamic filter and multichannel USB audio.
- **16 October 2026:** A sound and preset collection for the OP-XY by Douglas Carr, with more than 300 sounds, appeared on the same page.
- **23 October 2026:** MusicRadar reported the EP-35 amplifier, $349, with Bluetooth, a USB audio interface, XLR, guitar and line inputs and an FM radio accessory.
- **17 November 2026:** The most recent entry on the company's news page when we checked on 24 November was an artist feature, "Catching up with Ericsson & Reuter".

We found no formal statement, clarification or walk-back from Kouthoofd or the company after the interview, and nothing on the news page that mentions discontinuing a product. That is not proof that nothing has happened, and we will keep checking. A software update for an instrument is not a statement about production, but it does show existing instruments are still supported.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does Teenage Engineering make?

Teenage Engineering makes portable music hardware, including the OP-1, OP-1 Field, OP-XY, the Pocket Operator series, the EP-133 K.O. II sampler, the TX-6 mixer and the TP-7 field recorder. It also designs products with other companies, such as the Playdate console, a collection for IKEA and earbuds for Nothing.

### Where is Teenage Engineering based?

Teenage Engineering is based in Stockholm, Sweden. Highsnobiety places its headquarters in Hammarby Sjöstad, a former industrial waterfront area of the city.

### Is Teenage Engineering discontinuing the OP-1?

Founder and CEO Jesper Kouthoofd told Highsnobiety the company plans to stop making synthesizers and that the OP-1 is no exception. No date or formal discontinuation notice has been published, and the company has not said what happens to stock, repairs or firmware.

### Is Teenage Engineering really stopping making synths?

It has said so through its CEO, in an interview reported on 8 October 2026. As of 24 November 2026 we found no formal announcement, timeline or clarification from the company, so treat it as a stated plan rather than a completed change.

### Is the OP-XY still being made?

It is still on sale. On 24 November 2026 the OP-XY page on Teenage Engineering's store carried buy buttons, and the company released a major OP-XY firmware update on 15 October 2026. We found no notice of discontinuation.

### How much does an OP-1 cost?

We could not find a current official price for the OP-1 or the OP-1 Field, because the company's product pages do not display one. For reference, the EP-133 K.O. II was listed at $329 on the company's own page on 24 November 2026, and the OP-XY launched at 1899 pounds including VAT in the UK, per Sound On Sound. Check a retailer for current OP-1 pricing.

### What is the Teenage Engineering Pocket Operator?

The Pocket Operator is a series of pocket-sized synths and drum machines, sold without a case and with a small segment display, that debuted at NAMM in 2015 at $59. Teenage Engineering says the series has sold just shy of a million units. The first three models were the PO-12 rhythm, PO-14 sub and PO-16 factory.
