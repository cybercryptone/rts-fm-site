Detroit is still the city that techno is measured against, and 2026 has been a year of loss and consolidation there. [Juan Atkins](/artists/juan-atkins), the producer behind Cybotron and Model 500, died at 64, with his agency announcing it on 1 November (our [news report](/blog/juan-atkins-dies-detroit-techno-pioneer) sticks to what major outlets published). In July, the Underground Resistance orbit lost John Collins, the guide who ran tours of the world's first techno museum. In June, Spot Lite, the east side club, gallery and record shop that hosted Movement's official afterparties, shut for good. At the same time, the city made Detroit Techno Week official for the first time, Movement celebrated its 20th edition, and the rooms that carry the weekly programme, Marble Bar and TV Lounge, have events listed through December.

This guide covers the short history, the labels that still define the city's sound, and the practical question: where can you actually go in Detroit in 2026 and hear, buy or see techno? Each venue below carries a status and a source. Where a detail could not be confirmed from a current source, we say so rather than guess.

## Where Detroit techno began, in brief

Techno came out of Belleville, a town about 30 miles from Detroit, where three friends met at Belleville High School: Juan Atkins, [Derrick May](/artists/derrick-may) and [Kevin Saunderson](/artists/kevin-saunderson). According to [Wikipedia's account of the Belleville Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Belleville_Three), Saunderson moved to Belleville at age nine, Atkins and May followed from Detroit, and the three "gelled right away." Atkins moved first. Cybotron, his duo with Rick Davis, put out "Alleys of Your Mind" in 1981 on his own Deep Space imprint, and it sold around 15,000 copies. He then recorded as Model 500 (the landmark single was "No UFOs") on Metroplex, the label he founded. May's Transmat began life as a Metroplex sublabel before becoming his own, and Saunderson named KMS after his initials.

That is the origin story in three sentences, and our festival coverage ([Movement's 20th anniversary and the Belleville Three](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years)) goes deeper on how it fed the city's own festival. What matters for a visitor is the structure it created. Detroit techno was not built by a corporate scene. It was built by small labels run out of the city itself, and most of the living institutions below follow that pattern.

## The labels that still define the city

**Metroplex, Transmat and KMS** are the first-generation imprints, one per member of the Belleville Three. Atkins's death on 1 November leaves May and Saunderson as the surviving members of the trio. Both have artist pages here: [Derrick May](/artists/derrick-may) and [Kevin Saunderson](/artists/kevin-saunderson). Saunderson was on the Movement 2026 bill, and KMS was represented there, according to our [festival coverage](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years).

**Underground Resistance** is the second-wave answer. According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Resistance_(band)), UR was first formed in 1989 by "Mad" Mike Banks and [Jeff Mills](/artists/jeff-mills), and [Robert Hood](/artists/robert-hood) contributed to its early output before leaving in 1992. Submerge, the wider cooperative behind the label, is described there as a supportive economic community of labels, musicians, lawyers and financial experts that gave artists a safety net to experiment. The Hi-Tech Jazz release credited to Galaxy 2 Galaxy in the mid-1990s is the clearest example of how far the collective stretched beyond hard techno. We have a full profile of [Underground Resistance](/artists/underground-resistance) if you want the members and the discography.

**Planet E** is [Carl Craig](/artists/carl-craig)'s label, and Craig was artistic director of the original Detroit Electronic Music Festival in 2000, according to the [Detroit Historical Society](https://detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/detroit-electronic-music-festival). Detroit Love, a party series built around Craig, is one way to catch him in the city (more on that below). **Sound Signature** is [Theo Parrish](/artists/theo-parrish)'s label, founded in 1997 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Parrish)), and a reminder that Detroit's lineage runs through house, soul and jazz as much as through the machine funk of Cybotron. Hood's M-Plant and his 1994 album Minimal Nation, and the Floorplan project, link the city to the minimal techno that labels like ours still work in. Hood's page is at [Robert Hood](/artists/robert-hood).

## Clubs and rooms open now, with status

Detroit's techno infrastructure is thinner than its reputation suggests. A 2024 [Model D analysis by Alex B. Hill](https://modeldmedia.com/mappingdetroittechno/) of venues hosting Movement-related events ranked Marble Bar first (62 events), then Spot Lite and Tangent Gallery (54 each), TV Lounge (50), City Club (49), Lincoln Factory (47) and Motor City Wine (46). Two years later, one of those seven has closed. Here is where things stand for the rooms we could verify.

### Marble Bar: open, programme listed to New Year's Eve

Marble Bar is a Detroit room built around a weekly techno programme, and one 2026 ranking calls its Techno Tuesday the city's definitive weeknight session. Listings put it at 1501 Holden Street, and describe an indoor ballroom plus a large open air patio, booking techno, house, electro, disco, acid, trance and ghetto tech, with resident DJs, touring acts and live hardware sets. The event listing site [Ma.to](https://ma.to/venue/marbledetroit) shows a full run of upcoming nights as of this writing: Pariah and Verraco on 6 November, SPECTRO 006 with Detroit Techno Militia on 7 November, four years of the XOXR party on 14 November, the queer techno and acid house series Divalocity on 20 November, and a New Year's Eve party with Reptant (live) and Pearson Sound. Event listings for Marble Bar indicate most nights are 21+, so bring ID.

### TV Lounge: open, also booked into December

TV Lounge sits at 2548 Grand River Avenue and bills itself as the longest running nightclub in the city. According to its [Ma.to listing](https://ma.to/venue/tv_lounge), it is a multi-room venue for techno, house, disco and hip-hop, with indoor rooms and an outdoor patio. The November programme includes a "This Is TV!" night on 8 November with DJ Bone, Waajeed and Sillygirlcarmen, and Eli Escobar in the Red Room on 22 November. Jacques Greene's jg tour stop with Matthew Dear is listed for 12 December. The mix of local residents and touring names is the point: this is where Detroit DJs play for Detroit crowds, rather than a tourist stop.

### Lincoln Factory: open, with a Detroit Love date on 28 November

Lincoln Factory, at 1331 Holden Street, ranked sixth in Model D's count of venues hosting Movement-related events. A [Discotech listing](https://app.discotech.me/events/38288867-detroit-love-carl-craig-b2b-seth-troxler-at-lincoln-factory) shows Detroit Love on Saturday 28 November with Carl Craig b2b [Seth Troxler](/artists/seth-troxler) and Silverdome Boyz in support, doors at 9pm, 21+. If you are in Detroit that weekend, that is the date to circle.

### Spot Lite and UFO Bar: closed

Spot Lite, at 2905 Beaufait Street in Islandview, closed at the end of June 2026 after about five years. [CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/spot-lite-ufo-bar-detroit-closing/) described the 7,900 square foot space as an art gallery, bar, record store and music venue, and reported that owner Roula David wrote of "the most meaningful project of my life." [Daily Detroit](https://www.dailydetroit.com/spot-lite-detroit-and-ufo-bar-are-closing/) reports the final days as 28 June for Spot Lite and 30 June for UFO Bar in Corktown, with David saying she was moving away from nightlife. Outlets differ slightly on which weekday those dates fell, so we give the dates only. Cairo Coffee, which operated inside the Beaufait space, was set to stay open there temporarily while it looked for a new home.

The loss mattered beyond one room. Spot Lite hosted official Movement afterparties, and Crack Magazine's July 2026 [specialist mix from Stacey "Hotwaxx" Hale and Ceri](https://crackmagazine.net/?p=233274), recorded there in 2025, was released to mark the club's closure, and describes it as Hale's best residency. UFO Bar's space is slated to reopen under new owners as Detroit Vinyl Bar, a cocktail bar and record shop, according to [ClickOnDetroit](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2026/06/16/nightlife-mainstays-spot-lite-and-ufo-bar-closing-at-the-end-of-june/). We could not find an opening date, so treat it as announced, not open.

### What we could not verify

Plenty of "best techno clubs in Detroit" listicles also name Spot Lite as open, or list venues such as Northern Lights Lounge, Leland City Club, Elektricity and The Alley Deck without addresses or recent events. Several of those pages read as machine written or are undated, and one of them lists Spot Lite long after it closed. We found no 2026 programme or news for those rooms from a source we trust, so we have left them out. Check Resident Advisor or the venue's own channels before planning a night around any of them.

## The record shops and the techno museum

### Exhibit 3000 and Somewhere in Detroit (Submerge)

The most important stop for anyone interested in the music's history is not a club. Exhibit 3000 is the world's first techno museum, at 3000 E. Grand Boulevard, inside the building that houses Underground Resistance and Submerge Distribution. According to the Submerge [museum donation page](https://submerge.com/exhibit3000donation), the building has no sign, and it also contains a record store. A [Travel Addict visit report from October 2025](https://thetraveladdict.com/continents/northamerica/exhibit-3000-exploring-the-worlds-first-techno-museum-in-detroit/) describes exhibits tracing Detroit techno from its beginnings to the present, including an original Roland TR-909 drum machine donated by Juan Atkins.

Visits are by appointment. Submerge's [FAQ](https://submerge.com/faq) says the Somewhere in Detroit outlet store requires one ("no appointment, no entry," in its words, paraphrased here), and that you request a guided tour of the museum by emailing info@submerge.com with the subject line "Exhibit 3000 appointment." The same October 2025 report gave Saturday afternoons, 3pm to 7pm, as the record store's hours, but that is a visitor's account rather than an official schedule, so confirm with Submerge before you travel.

The status question is now harder. John Collins, who helped found Exhibit 3000 with Submerge and worked there as a tour guide and educator, died in July 2026. [Mixmag reported](https://mixmag.net/read/detroit-techno-pioneer-john-jammin-collins-has-died-news) that the Detroit Techno Militia announced his death on 26 July, and that Underground Resistance called him "a true, relentless soldier of the underground." He had been part of the Detroit scene since the early 1980s, including a residency at the club Cheeks alongside Jeff Mills, and had worked with Mike Banks at Submerge for around two decades. Mixmag's report does not say whether the museum's tours will continue or how visits are being handled now. The official email route above is the only way we can point you to, and the sensible approach is to ask politely and be prepared to be told no.

The museum also takes donations: Submerge suggests $20, with a minimum of $5, and says the donor gets a free digital download of Submerge Sessions Volume 1.

### Other shops

- **Peoples Records**, 1464 Gratiot Avenue, appears in Keep Them Spinning's [July 2026 guide to Detroit record shops](https://keepthemspinning.com/blogs/record-stores/best-record-stores-detroit), which rates it 4.7 on Google across 368 reviews. [105.1 The Bounce](https://1051thebounce.com/2026/04/06/vinyls-billion-dollar-comeback-best-detroit-record-stores-you-need-to-visit/) describes it as known for soul, funk, jazz and rare grooves. It is a general shop rather than a techno specialist, so treat it as a dig, not a destination.
- **Paramita Sound**, 1517 Broadway, is a downtown record shop and bar listed in the same guide. Axios Detroit named it alongside Marble Bar in the title of its [2023 guide to dancing in Detroit](https://www.axios.com/local/detroit/2023/12/01/where-to-dance-in-detroit-marble-bar-paramita-sound), which is a fair signal of where its crowd comes from.
- **Hello Records**, 4165 Fort Street, is in Lincoln Park, outside the city proper, per the same listing. It is worth the drive only if you are already heading that way.

We did not find a dependable 2026 source for current opening hours at any of these shops, so call ahead or check their social channels.

## Movement, Detroit Techno Week and Detroit Love

The festival is still the biggest single reason to visit. Movement 2026 ran 23 to 25 May at Hart Plaza, and its 20th anniversary edition fell inside Detroit Techno Week, which Mayor Mary Sheffield proclaimed for 18 to 25 May, according to [Playground Detroit](https://playgrounddetroit.com/city-of-detroit-announces-detroit-techno-week-may-18-25/). Festival producer Paxahau, which has run the event since 2006, ran six stages (Movement, Waterfront, Stargate, Underground, Pyramid and Detroit) at the 2026 edition. The same page describes "Respect the Architects," an exhibit in the Underground Amphitheatre, curated by Underground Music Academy with the Detroit Techno Foundation, honoring local figures including Mad Mike Banks, Delano Smith, Mike Huckaby, K-Hand, Ron Murphy and Ken Collier. It is the right idea: remember the people who built the scene, not only the headliners.

Movement 2027 is set for 29 to 31 May at Hart Plaza, according to the festival's own travel page. Our [Movement festival page](/festivals/movement-detroit) tracks the lineup status, the history and the practical details, and the broader context of this year's anniversary is in [Detroit Turns 20 (Again)](/blog/detroit-movement-festival-20-years). If you are building a 2027 festival calendar, our [guide to the best techno and house festivals of 2027](/blog/best-techno-house-festivals-2027) puts Movement in context with the rest of the year.

**Detroit Love** is the party series built around Carl Craig. Event listings have placed it at Movement afterparties in earlier years, and the Lincoln Factory date on 28 November shows it also runs outside the festival window.

One note on a name that sometimes comes up: **Dlectricity** is a Midtown art and light festival, not a techno event. It began in 2012 as a free nighttime festival along Woodward Avenue, and its second edition in 2014 featured 35 artists (per [CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/illuminated-art-returns-to-midtown-detroit)). We could not confirm a 2026 edition, and it is not part of the techno calendar, so we have not built any advice around it.

## How to get around Detroit

Detroit is a car city, and the venues above are spread out. A practical outline:

- **From the airport.** The festival's [travel page](https://movementfestival.com/travel) lists the SMART FAST Michigan regional bus as the connection between Detroit Metropolitan Airport (DTW) and the city, at $2 for a four hour pass or $5 for 24 hours. It does not give stops or timetables, so check SMART's own site.
- **Downtown.** The Detroit People Mover, the elevated loop around downtown, is free according to the festival's travel page, running 6:30am to midnight on weekdays. The QLINE streetcar on Woodward Avenue is also free, according to [QLINE's own site](https://qlinedetroit.com): 3.3 miles and 13 stops, Monday to Saturday from 8am to midnight and Sunday from 8am to 9pm. Both are useful for Hart Plaza and Midtown.
- **To the clubs.** Marble Bar and Lincoln Factory are on Holden Street, and TV Lounge is on Grand River Avenue. Those streets are not on the People Mover loop, and Holden Street is not on the Woodward line, so look at a map. For late nights, plan on rideshare or a car, and do not plan on public transport for a 3am exit.
- **Submerge.** The museum at 3000 E. Grand Boulevard is a short drive from downtown, and since it is appointment only, you will want a confirmed time and a driver who knows the address, as the building has no sign.
- **At Movement.** For Hart Plaza, the festival's travel page names Washington Boulevard and Larned Street as the rideshare drop-off and pick-up point, and points to Huntington Place for parking.

A sensible one weekend plan from these pieces: book a museum appointment for the daytime well in advance, pick up records on Gratiot or Broadway, then split the evenings between Marble Bar and TV Lounge, checking their listings the week you travel. Most nights start late, many rooms are 21+, and the best Detroit parties are often announced by the promoters themselves, not by travel sites.

## Why the city still matters for labels like ours

RTS.FM works in the territory Detroit opened: minimal house, minimal techno and electronica, with a bias toward independence and artist control. The Detroit model of small labels, cooperatives and records pressed by the people who made them is not nostalgia for us. It is a working template, and the 2026 losses make the surviving institutions more important to support. Buy the records, go to the museum if it will have you, and book the tickets for a Tuesday as well as a festival weekend.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the best techno clubs in Detroit in 2026?

Marble Bar (1501 Holden Street) and TV Lounge (2548 Grand River Avenue) are the two rooms with confirmed programmes into December 2026, and Lincoln Factory hosts Detroit Love on 28 November. Spot Lite, long a Movement afterparty host, closed in June 2026.

### Is Spot Lite still open?

No. Spot Lite on Beaufait Street closed permanently at the end of June 2026, according to CBS Detroit, Daily Detroit and ClickOnDetroit. Owner Roula David said she was moving away from nightlife, and UFO Bar closed two days later.

### Can you visit the Detroit techno museum?

Exhibit 3000 at 3000 E. Grand Boulevard is open by appointment only, per Submerge's FAQ, which asks you to email info@submerge.com with the subject "Exhibit 3000 appointment." Co-founder and guide John Collins died in July 2026, and reports do not say how tours are handled now, so ask before you travel.

### Where can you buy techno records in Detroit?

Submerge's Somewhere in Detroit outlet store, in the same building as the museum, is appointment only. Peoples Records on Gratiot Avenue and Paramita Sound on Broadway are general shops listed in 2026 guides, and Hello Records is in Lincoln Park. Check hours before visiting.

### When is Movement 2027?

Movement 2027 is set for 29 to 31 May at Hart Plaza, according to the festival's travel page. The 2026 edition, the 20th anniversary, ran 23 to 25 May, and our [festival page](/festivals/movement-detroit) tracks the 2027 lineup status.

### Do you need a car in Detroit?

For a festival trip centred on Hart Plaza, no: the People Mover and QLINE are free and the festival points to a rideshare drop-off. For the clubs on Holden Street and Grand River Avenue and for the museum, rideshare or a car is the practical choice, since they are away from the downtown loop and the Woodward line.

### Who invented Detroit techno?

Juan Atkins, Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson, the Belleville Three, are credited with inventing techno after meeting at Belleville High School. Atkins died on 1 November 2026; see our [news report](/blog/juan-atkins-dies-detroit-techno-pioneer) and his [artist page](/artists/juan-atkins).
