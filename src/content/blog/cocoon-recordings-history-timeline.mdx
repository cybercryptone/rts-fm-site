Most techno labels that launched in 2000 are either dead, absorbed into a distributor's back catalog, or running on nostalgia bookings alone. Cocoon Recordings is none of those things. It has a Discogs page with hundreds of entries, an alphabetical compilation series that has quietly worked its way most of the way through the alphabet, and a founder who still signs off on what gets pressed. [RTS.FM already covered the label's 25th anniversary compilation](/blog/cocoon-recordings-25-years) in detail, tracklists and all. This is a different piece: a year by year walk through how Cocoon actually got from a limited vinyl box set in Frankfurt to a 300-plus release catalog, without repeating the ground that anniversary piece already covered.

## 2000: The Label's First Release and Its Frankfurt Roots

Sven Väth registered Cocoon Recordings in 2000 as a division of Cocoon Music Event GmbH, folding the label into an operation that already ran parties and, from the following year, would run a booking agency. The label's actual debut on wax was Cocoon Compilation A, catalog number COR LP 001, a six times twelve inch box set capped at a reported 1,000 copies and sold through a short list of pre-selected record shops rather than a wide release. A CD edition, COR CD 001, followed to reach listeners who couldn't track down the vinyl, according to release data collated on [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/71280-Various-Cocoon-Compilation-A). The tracklist mixed Frankfurt regulars with names who would go on to define entire scenes of their own: Väth's own "Unmasked" sat next to Ricardo Villalobos's "Schoorille (While Dandy Was Dreaming Of Sara Mix)," alongside Pascal F.E.O.S., Chris Liebing and Der Dritte Raum.

That alphabetical compilation format, one letter, one year, became Cocoon's spine for the next two decades. It's a simple structure, but it means the label's history can be read almost literally off its own back catalog: Compilation B followed in 2001, Compilation C in 2002, and so on, each one a snapshot of who Väth and his A&R team were paying attention to that particular year.

2000 was also the first full season of Cocoon's Ibiza residency, though the groundwork for that had actually been laid the year before. Väth ran four test parties at Amnesia in 1999, taking advantage of a free Monday slot the club had going begging next to Manumission's dominance of that night at neighboring Privilege. Those four nights went well enough that Cocoon claimed the Monday slot outright for the 2000 season, kicking off what would become one of Ibiza's defining residencies, as detailed in [909originals' retrospective on Cocoon Ibiza](https://909originals.com/2020/06/29/from-freakshow-to-disco-invaders-cocoon-ibiza-was-the-white-isles-best-party-during-the-2000s/).

## 2001-2006: Building the Catalog Through the Minimal Wave Years

The early 2000s were the period when Cocoon's identity got tangled up, fairly or not, with the minimal techno wave coming out of Germany and Chile. Ricardo Villalobos was the connective tissue. His actual solo full-length debut, "Alcachofa," came out on Frank Wiedemann and Ricardo Villalobos's ecosystem label Playhouse in September 2003, not on Cocoon, which is worth being precise about since the two labels get conflated. What Villalobos did give Cocoon was "In The Mix: Taka Taka," a mix CD released June 4, 2003, that put his own selections and edits directly on Väth's imprint and cemented the association between Cocoon and the minimal sound even though its most famous solo record technically lived elsewhere, per release data on [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/158932-Ricardo-Villalobos-In-The-Mix-Taka-Taka).

The compilation series kept running in the background through these years, B through F, pulling in a rotating cast that included Guy Gerber, Alex Smoke, Gregor Tresher, Johannes Heil and Guido Schneider on Compilation F alone. It's easy to miss how much churn there was in the roster during this stretch. Cocoon wasn't building a fixed house style so much as auditioning one artist after another against Väth's own taste, which shifted from stripped down minimal tracks toward warmer, more track-oriented house and techno as the decade went on.

## 2004-2012: The Cocoon Club Years in Frankfurt

The single biggest capital investment in the label's history landed on July 18, 2004, when the Cocoon Club opened inside a purpose-built concrete structure nicknamed U.F.O. in Frankfurt's Fechenheim district. The building was designed by Dietz Joppien Architekten, with an interior fit-out by the design studio 3deluxe that alone reportedly cost around ten million euros, and a sound system built by Steve Dash priced at roughly 700,000 euros, according to figures cited on the [Cocoon club's Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoon_(club)). It held around 1,500 people across a main room, restaurants, lounges and the small upholstered "cocoons" built into the walls that gave the venue its name, and it positioned itself explicitly as the successor to Omen, the club Väth had helped run through the 1990s.

For eight years the club functioned as the Frankfurt engine behind everything Cocoon touched. Väth, Villalobos, Paul Kalkbrenner and Monika Kruse all played formative sets there, and the venue's booking policy fed directly back into who ended up on the label's compilations and the Ibiza lineups. That relationship, club informing label informing party series, is what made Cocoon function less like a normal record label and more like a single cultural operation with three separate revenue lines. It's also what made the club's finances a bigger liability than a typical label would carry.

The Cocoon Club closed on November 30, 2012, after what German press at the time described plainly as financial difficulties, and the space was taken over by new management and reopened as a different venue called moon13. Cocoon Recordings itself kept releasing records without missing a beat, which says something about how the label's actual operations, distribution deals, A&R, compilation cycles, were structurally separate from the club's balance sheet even when the two shared a name and a founder.

## 2005-2011: New Artists Widen the Label's Range

While the club was running, the roster kept expanding well past the minimal contingent Cocoon had been associated with in its first few years. Loco Dice arrived with the mix album "Green & Blue" in 2005, followed by the "Carthago / Sorted" twelve inch in 2006, tracks that leaned harder and more percussive than most of what had come before on the label. Guy Gerber's debut full length, "Late Bloomers," followed on May 15, 2007, built around the singles "Belly Dancing" and "Sea of Sand," and gave Cocoon one of its more commercially visible crossover moments of the decade, per [Discogs' release data](https://www.discogs.com/master/18346-Guy-Gerber-Late-Bloomers).

That same year, Compilation G quietly marked Marcel Dettmann's first appearance on the label, tucked in alongside Tiefschwarz, Audion, Jamie Jones, Deetron, Cobblestone Jazz and Stephan Bodzin. Dettmann's presence is worth flagging because his industrial-leaning Berlin sound is about as far from Cocoon's minimal-house origins as German techno gets, and his inclusion on a 2007 compilation shows the label reaching outward well before the eclectic reputation it's known for today had fully set in. Compilation H followed in 2008 with Cassy, Joris Voorn, Dubfire, Len Faki, Oliver Huntemann and Gui Boratto, another lineup that would have looked improbable on a single tracklist a few years earlier.

2009 brought Nick Curly's "Pujante" and Guy Gerber's "Timing," alongside a compilation that made the widening roster explicit: "Ten Years Cocoon Ibiza," mixed by Dubfire and Loco Dice, marking a full decade of the Amnesia residency and putting two artists from very different corners of the label's catalog on the same double disc. Two years after that, in June 2011, Adam Beyer and Dorian Paic mixed "Cocoon Heroes" (catalog CORMIX034) for that year's Ibiza pre-season, according to [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/3041998-Adam-Beyer-And-Dorian-Paic-Cocoon-Heroes). Beyer ran his own label empire through Drumcode by that point, so his willingness to mix a Cocoon release said more about the strength of Väth's Ibiza brand than about any label rivalry.

## 1999-2018: Two Decades at Amnesia, Then a Move to Pacha

Cocoon's Monday night slot at Amnesia is arguably the single asset that did more to build the label's audience than any individual release. What started as four test parties in 1999 grew into a fixture that, by most tallies, ran for around eighteen straight years on that same night, through a period when the island's clubbing culture still treated techno as an import rather than a headline genre. The residency is credited with helping normalize the idea that Ibiza's after-hours culture could run on minimal and techno rather than trance or big room house, a shift documented in detail by [909originals](https://909originals.com/2020/06/29/from-freakshow-to-disco-invaders-cocoon-ibiza-was-the-white-isles-best-party-during-the-2000s/).

That run ended in 2018, when Väth announced Cocoon would leave Amnesia for Pacha, moving from Mondays to Wednesdays with an inaugural party on May 30 and a closing date of October 3 that year, according to [DJ Mag's coverage of the move](https://djmag.com/news/cocoon-ibiza-will-move-pacha-summer-sven-v%C3%A4th-announces). Väth framed it at the time as "metamorphosis multiplied by two," language that fit a label whose whole history had been about restructuring itself rather than freezing in place. Amnesia's Monday slot, once synonymous with Cocoon, is now home to Nina Kraviz's Pyramid residency, a different generation's version of the same experiment in putting underground electronic music on a marquee weeknight, which [RTS.FM examined in a separate piece on that residency](/blog/nina-kraviz-amnesia-ibiza-pyramid-residency).

## 2018-2022: A New Ibiza Era and a Pandemic-Era Pause for Reflection

The Pacha years never quite replicated the mythology of the Amnesia run, but they kept the party series alive through a genuinely difficult stretch for the entire touring economy. When live events shut down in 2020, Cocoon Recordings turned inward, toward the catalog itself. Väth used the enforced downtime to put together "Retrospective Collection" and later, in December 2022, "What I Used To Play," a twelve times twelve inch vinyl box set and companion CD set that dug back into his own record bag from 1981 to 1990, long before Cocoon or even Omen existed. The idea reportedly took shape while he was locked down at an apartment in London, according to [909originals](https://909originals.com/2022/12/07/sven-vath-delves-into-his-musical-past-with-what-i-used-to-play-compilation/), turning a pandemic pause into an archival project rather than a dead period.

The label's actual 20th anniversary landed in that same window. "20 Years Cocoon Recordings" arrived February 19, 2021, a fifteen track compilation released digitally and as a deluxe six times twelve inch box set with a silver foil cover, featuring Rampa, Solomun, Josh Wink, Planetary Assault Systems, Tiga and Roman Flügel together, and André Galluzzi and Daniel Stefanik together, among others, per [Dancing Astronaut's coverage](https://dancingastronaut.com/2021/02/for-techno-devotees-sven-vaths-cocoon-recordings-20-years-compilation/). Cocoon also printed a limited run of heavyweight T shirts for the occasion, screen printed front and back with the name of every artist who had released on the label across its first two decades, a small but telling gesture from a label that clearly keeps count of its own catalog.

The following February, in 2022, Väth released his first solo album in close to twenty years, which also happened to be the fiftieth album issued under the Cocoon name, a catalog milestone that tied the founder's own return to the front of the roster directly to the label's numbering system, as reported by [909originals](https://909originals.com/2021/12/21/sven-va%CC%88th-set-to-launch-first-solo-album-in-close-to-20-years/).

## 2022-2025: The Alphabet Keeps Going

Cocoon's compilation series, the same format that started with Compilation A in 2000, was still running more than two decades later. Compilation T was announced in July 2022, featuring Daniel Avery, Claude VonStroke and YOKTO on a tracklist that would have been unrecognizable to anyone judging the label purely by its early minimal reputation, per [909originals](https://909originals.com/2022/07/06/cocoon-recordings-announces-cocoon-compilation-t-featuring-daniel-avery-claude-vonstroke-yokto-and-more/). By late 2023, Cocoon was framing 2024 as a 25th anniversary year for the party series specifically, tracing its own count back to those first 1999 test nights at Amnesia rather than the label's 2000 founding, and built a touring lineup around that milestone, according to [909originals](https://909originals.com/2023/12/13/sven-vaths-cocoon-to-celebrate-25-years-of-cocoon-events/).

Ownership is the quieter but arguably more important part of this later stretch. Cocoon Recordings has stayed under Cocoon Music Event GmbH for its entire run, with Väth listed as its owner even as the club side of the business (a separate legal entity, CocoonClub GmbH & Co. KG) operated with its own set of partners and eventually failed. That structural separation is part of why the label survived the club's 2012 collapse largely unscathed. It also puts Cocoon in a category that's rarer than it sounds: a label whose independence isn't a marketing label sitting on top of major-owned distribution, but an actual single owner who has held the company for a quarter century. RTS.FM has looked at how thin that distinction can get across the wider industry in [our coverage of the shift toward independent label market share](/blog/independent-labels-market-share-2026), where a lot of nominal independence turns out to run on major-owned infrastructure underneath. Cocoon is closer to the older, plainer definition.

## 2025-2026: The 25th Anniversary and What Comes Next

By January 2025, Väth had confirmed a one-off revival night at the old Cocoon Club building in Frankfurt, a brief return to the room that closed in 2012, treated as a nostalgia event rather than a relaunch of the venue itself. The bigger anniversary push came later that year, when Cocoon released "25 Years Cocoon Recordings, Volume One" on November 11, 2025, followed by Volume Two on June 5, 2026. Volume One's tracklist included Harvey McKay, whose work RTS.FM has [written about separately](/blog/remembering-harvey-mckay), alongside Butch, Robag Wruhme, Josh Wink and Guy J. Both volumes, along with the full lineup details for Volume Two, are covered in depth in [RTS.FM's dedicated piece on the anniversary compilation](/blog/cocoon-recordings-25-years), which is the place to go for tracklists rather than repeating them here.

What the 25th anniversary period actually demonstrates, looked at against the full timeline rather than just the newest release, is continuity of a specific and unusual kind. The label went from a 1,000 copy vinyl box set sold in a handful of Frankfurt shops to a catalog north of 300 releases, survived the collapse of its own flagship club, moved its signature party from one Ibiza institution to another, and kept the same person signing off on every one of those decisions the entire way through.

## FAQ

### When did Cocoon Recordings release its first record?

Cocoon Recordings' debut release was Cocoon Compilation A in 2000, catalog number COR LP 001, issued as a limited six times twelve inch vinyl box set of around 1,000 copies before a CD edition followed in 2001.

### Was Ricardo Villalobos's "Alcachofa" a Cocoon Recordings release?

No. "Alcachofa" came out on Playhouse in September 2003. Villalobos's actual Cocoon release that year was the mix CD "In The Mix: Taka Taka," which came out in June 2003.

### When did the Cocoon Club in Frankfurt open and close?

The Cocoon Club opened on July 18, 2004, in a purpose-built venue in Frankfurt's Fechenheim district, and it closed on November 30, 2012, after running into financial difficulties. The space later reopened under different management as a club called moon13.

### How long did Cocoon's Ibiza residency run at Amnesia?

Cocoon tested the concept with four parties at Amnesia in 1999, then held a Monday night residency there for roughly eighteen years before moving to Pacha for the 2018 season, where it switched to a Wednesday night slot.

### Who owns Cocoon Recordings?

Cocoon Recordings has operated under Cocoon Music Event GmbH since its founding, with Sven Väth as owner. This has kept the label functioning as a single-owner independent operation even though the separately structured Cocoon Club eventually went out of business in 2012.

### What is the significance of Cocoon's alphabetical compilation series?

Starting with Compilation A in 2000, Cocoon has released a roughly annual compilation moving one letter through the alphabet, reaching Compilation T by 2022. The series works as a rough chronological index of the label's roster and sound at any given point in its history.
