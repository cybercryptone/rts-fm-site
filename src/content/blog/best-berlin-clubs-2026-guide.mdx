## How Berlin's club scene stands in 2026

Berlin's club scene is under financial pressure but, by its own authors' reading, is not collapsing. The Clubcommission's study "Clubkultur Berlin 2026", presented on 7 August 2026 aboard the Hoppetosse, found that 61 percent of surveyed venues at least covered their costs in 2025, down from 79 percent in 2017. Of the 102 completed questionnaires (fielded by Goldmedia from 19 March to 24 April 2026), 23 percent of club owners said they had considered giving up within 12 months, and 95 percent said many clubs face long-term risk without structural improvements ([Pollstar, 10 August 2026](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/08/10/business-model-flipped-on-its-head-findings-from-berlin-club-study/)).

The study counts at least 24 club and cultural venues closed since 2020 and about 25 opened in the same period, and its authors say this is not a "Clubsterben". Admission now makes up 59 percent of revenue, against 21 percent in 2017, while food and drink fell to about 20 percent. Only 8 percent of respondents own their venue. For wider European context, see [our European closures tracker](/blog/europe-club-closures-tracker).

On rankings, the DJ Mag Top 100 Clubs 2026 places Berghain at No. 21, down five places, and it is the only Berlin club in the list ([The Berliner, 16 April 2026](https://www.the-berliner.com/english-news-berlin/berghain-down-to-21-place/)). The poll is an online fan vote that favours large international venues, so it is a popularity measure, not a quality ranking.

On legal status, be precise. Berlin techno culture entered Germany's national register of Intangible Cultural Heritage in February 2024, which is not UNESCO's international list. Under federal building law, clubs are not yet classed as cultural facilities: a bill approved by the federal cabinet on 27 May 2026 would create a separate category for music clubs instead, and we found no evidence it has become law.

## The clubs

### Berghain and Panorama Bar

Berghain is Berlin's best-known techno club and the only Berlin entry in DJ Mag's 2026 Top 100. It opened in 2004 in a former Friedrichshain heating power plant built in 1953 and 1954, with Panorama Bar opening on 15 October and the main floor on 18 December 2004. It succeeded the Ostgut club, which ran from 1998 to 2003. According to German Wikipedia, the operators bought the building after Vattenfall put it up for sale in 2010.

The official programme for 17 October to 28 November 2026 lists a Klubnacht every Saturday starting at 23:59, with Berghain and Panorama Bar floors and the Säule on some nights, plus Friday Panorama Bar nights from 22:00. The timetable for the 10 October Klubnacht ran continuously from Saturday night into Monday morning. Kantine am Berghain hosts concerts and Halle hosts exhibitions.

Berghain publishes no price, door policy or photo policy on the pages we could read. Its own statements are that entry is 18+ and that "entry is not guaranteed for anyone". The phone-camera stickers and photo ban are described by Mixmag and Wikipedia, not by an official club text. The nearest station is S-Ostbahnhof.

### Tresor

Tresor is one of Berlin's longest-running techno clubs and is open in October 2026. It opened in March 1991 in the vault of the former Wertheim department store on Leipziger Strasse, closed there on 16 April 2005, and reopened in May 2007 in the former Heizkraftwerk Mitte at Köpenicker Strasse 70. Founder Dimitri Hegemann received the Verdienstorden des Landes Berlin in October 2025 (Mixmag, 22 October 2025). Tresor Records is still releasing in 2026, and the club marked its 35th anniversary with a 30-plus-hour marathon starting 13 March 2026.

The October 2026 calendar shows a Wednesday series called New Faces, Friday nights hosted by labels and artists, and a Saturday Klubnacht. Official listings use Tresor, Globus and Aurora Bar as separate rooms. The club's guest information is explicit: "Strictly no filming or photos inside the club", and offenders are removed. Its ticket page states that "a ticket does not guarantee entry" and lists a door-only Community Ticket from 1 July 2026 ([Tresor ticket information](https://tresorberlin.com/club/tickets/)). An Awareness Team is available. The nearest station is U Heinrich-Heine-Strasse (U8).

Tresor.West, the separate Dortmund club, reportedly announced its own closure on 8 October 2026 (RA, single report). We found no closure threat for the Berlin club.

Kraftwerk Berlin is the building around Tresor, not a club. The former Heizkraftwerk Mitte was built in the early 1960s, shut in 1997, and now offers about 8,000 square metres of event space for up to 2,600 visitors, according to visitBerlin. Its halls host [Berlin Atonal](/festivals/berlin-atonal), which relaunched there in 2013, and exhibitions. The official site lists the exhibition "Tanzflächen der Wiedervereinigung" from 3 October to 9 November 2026, and Berlin Atonal 2027 is set for 25 to 29 August 2027. Tresor Berlin GmbH also operates OHM, the small club in the same complex.

### Sisyphos

Sisyphos is a Rummelsburg club known for marathon weekend parties, and it is open in autumn 2026. It sits in a former dog biscuit factory in Rummelsburg. A Sommerleuchten association took over the site in 2008, first parties followed in 2009, and the operator is Sisyphos Event GmbH. German Wikipedia lists five floors: Strand (outdoor), Wintergarten (house), Dampfer, Hammahalle and Tunnel, with a capacity of about 1,500 including the outdoor area.

Parties run most weekends from spring until New Year, mostly Friday 22:00 to Monday 10:00, with a winter break from after New Year's Eve until about early March. Time Out ranked Sisyphos first on its list of Europe's best nightclubs on 8 September 2026, noting a strict no-photos rule and relaxed dress code ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/news/europes-best-night-club-ranking-090826)). Its official door text was not readable, so secondary guides report 21+ and a door team with the final say. Regular weekend prices are not published. The nearest rail stop is S3 Betriebsbahnhof Rummelsburg, with tram 21 and bus N40 at Gustav-Holzmann-Strasse, per berlin.de.

### ://about blank

://about blank is a collectively run club, which visitBerlin calls rare even within Berlin's scene, and it is operating in October 2026. It opened in 2010 at Markgrafendamm 24c beside Ostkreuz. Its legal form is a registered cooperative (about blank eG). The club's own FAQ states that every role is paid one hourly wage, no shareholder takes profit, and it accepts no sponsorship. It describes its politics as anti-capitalist, feminist, anti-racist and LGBTQ+-supportive, with a queer-feminist booking policy.

According to Top10 Berlin (review stamped 25 September 2026), the club opens Thursday to Saturday from midnight, with a night ticket usually 15 euros before midnight and 20 after, sold mainly at the box office. The same source reports a strict no-photo rule with phone cameras covered at entry, and a free garden, the Sektgarten, from May to September. The official programme pages did not load for us, so treat hours and prices as reported. The Ostkreuz S-Bahn exit is next to the venue.

### Salon zur wilden Renate

Renate is a multi-floor Friedrichshain club that reopened in 2026 after nearly closing. It opened in 2007 in a former apartment block on Alt-Stralau. In August 2024 it announced permanent closure for the end of 2025, then secured a new ten-year lease on 15 December 2025 (Mixmag reports 16 December). A soft reopening followed on 30 and 31 January 2026, and a renovation celebration over Easter 2026 introduced an L-shaped dance floor and a new Kirsch Audio system. Our [Wilde Renate history](/blog/wilde-renate-berlin-club-history) tells the full story.

The club's own site lists regular hours of Thursday to Saturday, 23:00 to 09:00, with entry "typically around EUR 20, including coat check", 21+ with valid ID, card payments only, and a strict no-photography policy. It also states that a ticket does not guarantee entry. The nearest S-Bahn stations are Ostkreuz and Treptower Park.

### KitKatClub

KitKatClub is Berlin's sex-positive, fetish-oriented club and is open in 2026. Simon Thaur and Kirsten Krüger founded it in March 1994, and it has been in the Sage Club building at Köpenicker Strasse 76 since July 2007. Reports in 2019 said KitKat and Sage Club would have to leave, but German Wikipedia says the operators agreed a direct lease with the landlord.

The official 2026 calendar lists a weekly programme, including Disco Bizarre on Saturdays and Nachspiel on Sundays from 8 a.m. Berlin's tourism board says outfits should be creative, fetish-inspired or expressive, and that entry is not guaranteed even with online tickets. It also says filming is prohibited and camera stickers may be used. Berlin.de calls the door policy strict. The club's site publishes no prices and refers guests to each event's organiser. The nearest station is U Heinrich-Heine-Strasse (U8), directly below.

### Club der Visionaere and Hoppetosse

Club der Visionaere and Hoppetosse are the Treptow waterside pair, and both are seasonal, so check before you go. Club der Visionaere is a small wooden club at Am Flutgraben 1 with a minimal techno focus and an outdoor area by the water. German Wikipedia and listing sites describe it as closed in winter and open daily in season, usually from the afternoon. We could not verify its opening year. Its official site on 11 October 2026 showed a "closed" status badge alongside two upcoming events.

Hoppetosse is a three-deck club ship in the Osthafen at Eichenstrasse 4, run by the Club der Visionaere team. Top10 Berlin says it was built in 1928 as an excursion steamer. Berlin.de describes techno and house with an electro focus. Both venues' sites list the same two events, a Monday night on 12 October and "Healing Channel" on 16 October, without naming which venue hosts which. Buses 165, 265, N60, N65 and M43 serve the ship, per berlin.de. A Time Out review from 2008 gives U1 Schlesisches Tor or Treptower Park S-Bahn for the club, so confirm current lines.

### Ritter Butzke

Ritter Butzke is a Kreuzberg house and techno club that is still listed as operating in 2026. According to Top10 Berlin (review stamped 25 September 2026), it started as an unlicensed venue in 2007 and reopened legally at its current site on Ritterstrasse 26 in October 2009. It is listed as open Friday and Saturday from 22:00 to 09:00, 18+ with photo ID. The same source and event listings show a June open air and a community rave in August 2026. It sits in the Moritzplatz area. We found no official policy text for door or photos.

### More venues worth knowing

- **Cassiopeia and Lokschuppen (formerly Suicide Circus), RAW site, Revaler Strasse 99.** Both are open but only provisionally secure. On 15 June 2026 owner Kurth-Gruppe issued eviction requests, and Mixmag reported on 17 June that Cassiopeia had two weeks to leave. On 11 August 2026 the Senate, district and owner reportedly agreed a deal (dpa): the district would act as interim tenant from September 2026 for two years, with a 30-year lease for a general tenant. The interim lease still needed votes in early September, and we could not confirm they passed. Suicide Circus opened in 1994 and has been at the RAW site since 2009.
- **Heideglühen, Seestrasse 1, Wedding.** A house-focused venue listed as operating, with Saturday sessions about every two weeks that pause in winter. We found no confirmed 2026 event, so check before visiting.
- **Else, An den Treptowers 10.** A summer venue at Treptower Park with Sunday parties. Its 2026 season reportedly ended with closing parties on 18 and 19 September.
- **OHM.** The small techno and house club in the Kraftwerk complex is open, operated by Tresor Berlin GmbH, and has a full October 2026 programme. Its opening year is unverified.

## Clubs Berlin has lost

Berlin has lost several well-known clubs in recent years, though no permanent closure of a major club was announced in 2026 in the sources we reviewed.

- **Watergate** opened in October 2002 by the Oberbaumbrücke. Its operators said the lease was not renewed and ended club operations at the end of 2024, with a 35-hour final New Year's Eve party into 2025. Co-founder Ulrich Wombacher cited inflation, energy costs and rent (Mixmag). The name now lives on as pop-up open airs, including a 2026 series at SAGE Berlin and a 27 June 2026 event at the Malzfabrik. In November 2025 the culture senator suggested Watergate could take over the former SchwuZ premises, but that was not based on talks with the team.
- **SchwuZ**, founded in 1977, held its last party at its Rollbergstrasse venue on 1 November 2025 after filing for insolvency. A relaunch party took place on 16 May 2026 at Metropol, and as of 15 May 2026 no new site had been chosen (taz).
- **Busche**, a Friedrichshain queer club of more than 30 years, closed at the end of July 2025.
- **Loophole** in Neukölln closed in early July 2024 after a police and Ordnungsamt raid and noise complaints (taz, 8 July 2024).
- **Mensch Meier** held its closing party from 15 to 18 December 2023.
- **Griessmühle** left its Sonnenallee site when the lease expired on 31 January 2020, with a finale running to 1 February; the team reopened as Revier Südost.

## Threats to watch

The two clearest threats are development pressure around ://about blank and the planned A100 motorway extension. In July 2025 the Berlin Senate approved a 240-room hotel reported to stand barely 100 metres from ://about blank. The club's spokesperson called it a threat to the club and the other projects on the Markgrafendamm 24c site, and the Clubcommission called the decision short-sighted ([Mixmag, 2 July 2025](https://mixmag.net/read/about-blank-fears-for-future-after-plans-to-build-adjacent-hotel-given-green-light-news)).

On the A100, operators said in August 2023 that about blank, Club Ost, Fips and Renate would be directly affected by the extension. As of 20 August 2026, six route variants were still under study, so the impact cannot yet be assessed. The RAW site dispute, covered above, is the third live risk. The Clubcommission study adds structural context: only 8 percent of clubs own their venue and 31 percent have leases shorter than five years. After the study, Berlin's economy state secretary praised club culture but announced no concrete funding, according to Börsen-Zeitung on 14 August 2026.

## Practical tips for visiting Berlin clubs

- **Nights out.** According to visitBerlin, club nights last from Friday night until Monday morning. Berghain's Saturday Klubnacht and Sisyphos's weekend parties follow that pattern.
- **Weekend transport.** Per berlin.de (last edited 19 May 2025), the U-Bahn runs 24 hours on Friday and Saturday nights, and nights before public holidays, every 15 minutes. The S-Bahn runs every 30 minutes on those nights, and Metrotram lines run every 30 minutes from 00:30 every night, alongside night buses ([berlin.de](https://www.berlin.de/en/public-transportation/1859225-2913840-night-buses-public-transport-at-night.en.html)). On weeknights U-Bahn and S-Bahn stop around 01:00 to 01:30 and night buses take over. BVG leaflets say the U2 has been diverted between Senefelderplatz and Alexanderplatz since 13 July 2026, and U6 renovation work began on 13 January 2026 and is expected to last about two and a half years. Check bvg.de for your line.
- **Photos.** Tresor and Renate officially ban photos and filming. Berlin's tourism board says KitKatClub prohibits filming and may seal phone cameras. For Berghain, about blank and Sisyphos, the ban and camera stickers are reported by Mixmag, Top10 Berlin and Time Out rather than stated in club text we could read.
- **Door.** A ticket does not guarantee entry at Tresor, Renate or KitKatClub, according to each club or the tourism board. Berlin.de notes that many clubs run a strict door policy and often have no dress code.
- **Awareness.** Tresor has an Awareness Team and a quiet retreat space, and Berghain's awareness page describes a quiet room on the ground floor and a consent-based code of conduct.
- **Drug checking.** Berlin's health department has run a drug-checking counselling project at three fixed sites (Fixpunkt, vista and Schwulenberatung) since July 2023. It is not offered inside clubs.
- **Prices.** Prices change, and most clubs do not publish regular prices. Renate states about 20 euros including coat check, while about blank's 15 or 20 euros is Top10 Berlin's report and a Sisyphos night on 10 October 2026 listed 25 euros in advance (Technoairlines).
- **Neighbours.** Residents want quiet at night, so keep noise down on the way in and out, as visitBerlin asks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best club in Berlin?

It depends on the sound you want, but as of October 2026 Berghain is the highest-ranked Berlin club, at No. 21 in DJ Mag's Top 100 Clubs 2026 and the only Berlin entry. Time Out ranked Sisyphos first among Europe's best nightclubs on 8 September 2026. Tresor, about blank and Renate are the other main techno and house names.

### Is Berghain still open and when?

Yes. Berghain's official programme lists a Klubnacht every Saturday from 23:59 through 28 November 2026, with Berghain and Panorama Bar floors, and Friday Panorama Bar nights from 22:00. The Klubnacht of 10 October 2026 ran from Saturday night into Monday morning.

### How do you get into Berghain?

The club does not publish its entry criteria. Its own statements are that entry is 18+ and that entry is not guaranteed for anyone.

### Is Tresor still open?

Yes. Tresor's official calendar lists nights on Wednesday, Friday and Saturday through 31 October 2026. It reopened in May 2007 at Köpenicker Strasse 70 and has no announced closure in Berlin. The Dortmund club Tresor.West is a separate venue.

### What happened to Watergate?

Watergate's operators ended club operations at the end of 2024, with a final New Year's Eve night, after the lease was not extended. In 2026 the name appears only on one-off open-air events, and no new permanent club has been confirmed.

### Is Sisyphos open in winter?

Not continuously. Sisyphos runs weekend parties from spring until New Year and takes a winter break from after New Year's Eve until about early March, according to German Wikipedia and a guide checked on 30 September 2026. The 2026 and 2027 break dates are not confirmed.

### Which Berlin clubs allow photos?

Clubs that state a policy ban them. Tresor and Renate state a strict ban on photos and filming, and Berlin's tourism board says KitKatClub prohibits filming. Berlin.de says photos and videos are usually forbidden in clubs for privacy reasons.

### How late does the Berlin U-Bahn run on weekends?

On Friday and Saturday nights and nights before public holidays, the U-Bahn runs 24 hours with trains every 15 minutes, according to berlin.de. The S-Bahn runs every 30 minutes on those nights. Works on the U2 and U6 in 2026 can change routes, so check bvg.de.

### How many clubs have closed in Berlin?

The Clubcommission's study "Clubkultur Berlin 2026" counts at least 24 club and cultural venues closed since 2020 and about 25 opened in the same period. Its authors say this is not a "Clubsterben", although 61 percent of surveyed venues at least covered costs in 2025, against 79 percent in 2017.

### Are Berlin clubs legally cultural venues?

Not yet under federal building law. Berlin techno culture entered Germany's national Intangible Cultural Heritage register in February 2024, but that is not a planning classification. A federal bill approved by the cabinet on 27 May 2026 would give music clubs their own category, and we found no evidence it has become law.
