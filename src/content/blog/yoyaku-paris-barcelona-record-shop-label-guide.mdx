As of 24 November 2026, Yoyaku runs a record shop at 14 Boulevard La Chapelle in Paris and, since 15 June 2026, a second shop at Carrer de Llull 48 in Barcelona. It is also a label, a vinyl distributor and, since an early 2026 deal, the owner of the assets of a Berlin pressing plant.

## What is Yoyaku?

Yoyaku is an independent label, record shop and vinyl distributor founded in Paris in 2015. Its own site describes it as a record label, record store and vinyl distribution business based in Paris, and Resident Advisor reported in February 2025 that the label and store's range spans house, minimal, electro, dub techno and ambient. The first shop opened in 2016, a second Paris space, Chapelle XIV, followed in 2020, and a Barcelona shop opened in June 2026. Benjamin Belaga is the founder and CEO. A distribution arm sends independent labels' records to shops worldwide, and in 2026 Yoyaku added a Berlin pressing plant and a distribution platform called Kurtezy. It also sells vinyl online through its own website, which lists thousands of records. Few record shops are also manufacturers, which is why Yoyaku matters beyond its two storefronts.

## How did Yoyaku start?

Yoyaku started as a label and booking operation that grew a shop around it. Trommel's September 2018 profile dates Yoyaku's first release to October 2015, and says the shop had never had a primary label until Cabanne's Stereophobique EP, catalogue number YOYAKU001, launched one in 2018, with a release party at Concrete in Paris on 8 September. XLR8R covered the launch on 19 September 2018. FACT reported on 18 February 2016 that Yoyaku, then described as a French booking agency and distributor, was opening a physical record store in Paris, with a launch party on 27 February.

Belaga told Exhibition magazine that he began with a label that did fairly well, and that its artists and a network of independent shops led to a small shop of about 30 square metres in Belleville. He says the business later took over a former art gallery between La Chapelle and Stalingrad. The magazine also says the name is a Japanese word that means both booking and reservation, and that early on customers booked an appointment to buy records.

Resident Advisor's 2016 report called the first shop a low-key Paris location and said it was initially accessible to online customers only. No article we opened gives that first shop's address, but the description of Yoyaku's September 2018 instore session video with Cabanne lists the address as 74 rue des Cascades, 75020 Paris, which is in Belleville and fits Exhibition's account of a small shop there. We cannot confirm it was the 2016 location.

## What is Chapelle XIV?

Chapelle XIV is the larger Paris space Yoyaku opened in October 2020 at 14 boulevard de la Chapelle, in the 18th arrondissement. Resident Advisor and The Vinyl Factory both reported it on 9 October 2020 as a "cultural venue" with a record shop curated by the Yoyaku team, an art gallery, a cafe and an Atelier 14 workshop. The workshop has equipment for merchandise, record sleeves and 3D prints, and the fit-out used furniture and acoustic panels by CNTRL Acoustics and a custom Martion soundsystem.

RA said the space opened the previous week with a MUTEK Montreal showcase featuring Bambounou, Molly and NSDOS. The Vinyl Factory called it the second record shop from Yoyaku.

## Where is the Yoyaku shop in Paris and when is it open?

The Paris shop is at 14 Boulevard La Chapelle, in the third courtyard at the back, 75018 Paris, according to the official site. Exhibition describes entering through a heavy carriage gate and a long enclosed courtyard into a former workshop with large glass roofs, and says anyone can visit seven days a week.

The official site lists these hours as of 24 November 2026:

| Day | Hours printed on the official site |

| Monday to Friday | 10:00 to 20:00 |
| Saturday | Printed as 12:00 AM to 8:00 PM |

The Saturday opening is printed as 12:00 AM, which we read as noon, not midnight. Confirm before you travel. The contact page also links an in-store session RSVP.

## When did Yoyaku open in Barcelona and where is the shop?

Yoyaku Barcelona Store opened on 15 June 2026 at Annex Llull, Carrer de Llull 48, 08005 Barcelona, inside the Bridge 48 music hub in the Sant Martí district. The company's press release calls it its first record store outside Paris. Resident Advisor reported the plan on 8 June 2026, placing the shop near Ciutadella Park, and Electronic Groove and Trommel confirmed the opening after the date.

The press release says the shop sells new releases and second-hand vinyl and brings Paris's catalogue memory and independent-label network together with dedicated shelf space for local labels and distributions and Spanish records. It lists house, tech house, techno, electro, minimal and ambient, and says the local selection is meant to be central, not a corner. Bridge 48 itself is described as a hub of studios, education and events.

## What is Yoyaku's history with Barcelona?

Yoyaku had been returning to Barcelona for more than ten years before it had a shop, according to its press release, mostly around Off Week, the programme of parties that surrounds [Sónar](/festivals/sonar) in mid-June. The release traces three records:

- **2017:** a Barcelona white label with [Varhat](/artists/varhat), [Zendid](/artists/zendid), [Janeret](/artists/janeret) and [Oshana](/artists/oshana).
- **2018:** YOYAKU001, the [Cabanne](/artists/cabanne) EP Stereophobique, as an exclusive Barcelona Off version of 150 hand-stamped copies before the official release.
- **2025:** Yoyaku Barcelona 2025, a record with Hostom and Aline Umber.

Miniguide listed a three-day Yoyaku record store pop-up at Bridge 48, Carrer de Llull 48, on 12 to 14 June 2025 with more than 30 artists. The label's own release page says half of the 2025 pressing was sold at the Bridge 48 pop-up during Off Week, and a Yoyaku x [a:rpia:r] event took place at Poble Espanyol on 14 June 2025.

For 2026, Yoyaku Off Barcelona took place on Friday 19 June from 13:00 to 23:00 at Hola Club, Cala Vallcarca, in Sitges, with [Sonja Moonear](/artists/sonja-moonear), Saoirse, [Tomas Station](/artists/tomas-station), Mari.te, Lis Sarroca, Hugo Martinez and Shakèd. The press release says in-store sessions during Off Week were reserved for ticket holders.

## What does Yoyaku release and distribute?

Yoyaku is a label group as much as a shop. Trommel's 2018 profile counted eleven labels and sublabels, listing Aku, Hostom, Joule Imprint, Tartouffe, Trimurti, YoY, Yoyakuza, YYK no label, the YYK showcase series, the YYY series and Yoyaku itself, and said mastering and distribution of every Yoyaku record were done in-house. XLR8R's 2017 report on four releases named artists including Roger Gerressen, Hostom, Vincen and YYY.

The main Yoyaku label page on the official site lists 27 results as of 24 November 2026. It includes YOYAKU001 by Cabanne, YOYAKU004LP, the In The Shell album by Zendid, YOYAKU011 by Mandar, a Varhat and Akufen record titled Lieu des Herons, and a YYK edition by [Laurent Garnier](/artists/laurent-garnier).

The distribution side is larger than the label. Trommel reported in December 2021 that the arm, yydistribution, sent out about 150,000 records a year and that more than 2,000 labels fell under the umbrella, a figure RA matched by saying Yoyaku was in contact with more than 2,000 labels. Yoyaku's June 2026 French press dossier says more than half of the labels it distributes are French and that nearly 70 percent of distribution turnover is made abroad. Distribution matters for shoppers because a shop with a distribution arm can stock records that other shops struggle to get, a point we also make in our [techno record shops guide](/blog/best-techno-and-electronic-record-shops-2026).

Belaga told Doors Open in April 2026 that Yoyaku is a record store, a physical distribution service for independent labels and a label. Trommel also lists a booking agency arm that it says evolved into Interwave.

## Did Yoyaku really go 100 percent vinyl?

Yes for its releases and digital catalogue, but the shop's webstore still sells records. Resident Advisor reported on 17 February 2025 that Yoyaku was going "100 percent" vinyl, had deleted its online digital store and had removed its catalogue from streaming services. That store was AZE, a digital-only shop that Yoyaku launched on 9 December 2021 with more than 4,000 tracks, per RA at the time.

RA said Yoyaku reported a 30 percent rise in vinyl sales in 2024 and called it the strongest year yet, and that some music remained on its own site and Bandcamp until it was taken down. Named catalogue artists included Christian AB, Laurent Garnier, rRoxymore and Bambounou.

The official site, however, still operates an online vinyl store with add-to-cart, wishlists, pre-orders and a reserve option, and it also sells merchandise and DJ equipment. In other words, the move was away from digital sales and streaming, not away from selling vinyl online. For the wider market, see [our report on record vinyl sales](/blog/vinyl-sales-record-2025).

## What is Yoyaku's Berlin pressing plant and Kurtezy?

In 2026 Yoyaku added manufacturing. Resident Advisor reported on 18 May 2026 that Yoyaku had acquired the assets of Berlin pressing plant OBJECTS Manufacturing in a deal completed in early 2026. The plant operates several Alpha Phoenix presses and an in-house galvanic line, and the deal keeps much of the former team in place. RA said OBJECTS had filed for insolvency the previous October, citing higher production costs and European competition. Price and stake were not disclosed.

According to RA, Belaga will lead OBJECTS Manufacturing as CEO and relocate to Berlin, and Yoyaku said the deal is meant to create a long-term manufacturing base for independent labels across Europe rather than move its Paris operations. Jamie Fry, founder of KMA60, left Yoyaku to run Kurtezy full-time, with Sammy Dee as co-lead. Kurtezy is a Berlin-based physical distribution and logistics platform operating inside the factory.

Kurtezy's website, undated when we read it, describes B2B vinyl distribution and curation for independent record shops, pressing in Berlin and Paris, 48-hour EU delivery and a catalogue launch in summer 2026. Trommel says Yoyaku hopes to expand the plant into a full manufacturing facility.

The press dossier sums up the structure as curation and distribution in Paris, pressing and logistics in Berlin and a public address in Barcelona. For why pressing capacity matters, read our [vinyl pressing capacity report](/blog/vinyl-pressing-plant-capacity-crisis) and the [independent label survival study](/blog/independent-label-survival-study).

## How should you visit Yoyaku?

Plan around the two addresses and what each does best.

- **Paris:** go for the full catalogue of Yoyaku's own labels and the distributed records that few other shops hold. Allow time to find the third courtyard at the back of 14 Boulevard La Chapelle.
- **Barcelona:** go for second-hand vinyl and the local and Spanish shelves, which the press release treats as central.
- **Events:** Paris in-store sessions run through an RSVP link on the official site, and Barcelona sessions around Off Week were reserved for ticket holders. Many sessions are filmed and published on YouTube; see the [top 10 instore sessions on YouTube](/blog/top-10-yoyaku-instore-sessions-youtube).
- **Online:** per the contact page, reserve items are not paid at checkout while pre-orders are, so read the terms on each release page.
- **Pair it with other shops:** in Paris, Yoyaku appears with Syncrophone and Techno Import on our [Europe list](/blog/best-record-shops-in-europe-2026), and the [world guide](/blog/best-record-shops-in-the-world-2026) places it in a global context. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Yoyaku?

Yoyaku is a Paris record label, record shop and vinyl distributor founded in 2015 by Benjamin Belaga. It now also has a shop in Barcelona and owns the assets of a Berlin pressing plant.

### Where is the Yoyaku record shop in Paris?

It is at 14 Boulevard La Chapelle in the 18th arrondissement of Paris, in the third courtyard at the back, according to the official site. The same address was reported as the Chapelle XIV space in 2020.

### Does Yoyaku have a shop in Barcelona?

Yes. Yoyaku Barcelona Store opened on 15 June 2026 at Annex Llull, Carrer de Llull 48, 08005 Barcelona, inside the Bridge 48 hub in Sant Martí. It is the company's first shop outside Paris.

### What does the Yoyaku Barcelona shop sell?

According to its press release, it sells new and second-hand vinyl, Yoyaku's Paris catalogue and independent labels, with shelf space for local labels, distributions and Spanish records. Genres include house, tech house, techno, electro, minimal and ambient.

### What are Yoyaku's opening hours?

The official site lists Paris as 10:00 to 20:00 Monday to Friday and 11:00 to 19:00 on Sunday, with Saturday printed as 12:00 AM to 8:00 PM. We could not confirm hours for Barcelona.

### Is Yoyaku vinyl only?

In 2025 it announced it was going 100 percent vinyl and removed its digital store and streaming catalogue, but the shop's website still sells vinyl online, plus merchandise and DJ equipment.

### Who owns Yoyaku?

Benjamin Belaga is the founder and CEO, according to Doors Open and Resident Advisor. We found no public ownership structure beyond that.

### What is Kurtezy?

Kurtezy is a Berlin-based physical distribution and logistics platform that operates inside the former OBJECTS Manufacturing pressing plant, per Resident Advisor. Jamie Fry and Sammy Dee lead it.

### Does Yoyaku press its own records?

Since 2026 it has had access to a Berlin plant. Resident Advisor reported on 18 May 2026 that Yoyaku acquired the assets of OBJECTS Manufacturing, which runs Alpha Phoenix presses.
