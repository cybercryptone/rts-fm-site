For the better part of a decade, Belgian techno's two most-searched names have shared festival posters, magazine covers, and an entire generation's imagination, but not a studio session. That changed with "One Mind," the debut collaborative EP from Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte, released to coincide with a run of sold-out back-to-back shows at Flanders Expo in their home country. It is, by any measure, one of the biggest moments Belgian dance music has produced this decade, and it says as much about where techno is heading as it does about the two women who made it.

## What Is the "One Mind" EP, and When Was It Released?

"One Mind" is a two-track EP released on February 6, 2026, credited jointly to Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte and issued not through either artist's own label, but through a newly created joint imprint called B2B, a nod to the back-to-back format the pair are known for playing live. The release was confirmed and detailed by [Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/818006-amelie-lens-charlotte-de-witte-debut-joint-ep-one-mind), with the catalog entry and vinyl pressing (catalog number B2B001R) later listed on [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/33043215-Amelie-Lens-Charlotte-De-Witte-One-Mind) and covered by [Electronic Groove](https://electronicgroove.com/listen-charlotte-de-witte-and-amelie-lens-one-mind/). The choice to launch a standalone label rather than fold the record into Lens's Lenske or de Witte's KNTXT catalog is itself a statement: this was conceived as neither artist's project, but as a genuine third thing built by both of them.

The two tracks are the title cut "One Mind" and "Where Do We Go." According to Beatportal's rundown, "One Mind" opens with a chilling vocal whisper before dropping into a heavy, vibrating kick, hard-edged hats, and a striking acid line, while "Where Do We Go" poses what the outlet described as an almost existential question about where ravers go once the night ends, riding hypnotic acid-driven production, ghostly melodic loops, and a dramatic final drop. Reviewing the record, [EDM Identity](https://edmidentity.com/2025/02/07/amelie-lens-charlotte-de-witte-one-mind/) called it a case of two producers successfully fusing their signature sounds into one voice, noting that both artists' vocals are subtly layered together across the EP, an audible embodiment of the title.

## Why Did They Play Ghent, and How Did 69,000 Tickets Sell Out?

The EP's release was timed deliberately around three sold-out nights at Flanders Expo in Ghent, the pair's first-ever run of back-to-back sets together, which combined drew roughly 69,000 attendees. The first two dates, January 31 and February 1, sold their initial allocation (around 46,000 tickets) within minutes of going on sale, according to reporting at the time from outlets including [Electronic Groove](https://electronicgroove.com/amelie-lens-and-charlotte-de-witte-announce-special-b2b-shows/); organizers then added a third date, February 8, which also sold out immediately. Beatportal put the average crowd at roughly 23,000 dancers per night, filling the cavernous exhibition halls that Ghent regularly converts into one of Belgium's largest dance floors.

Speaking to Beatportal about the speed of the sellout, Lens said, "When it sold out so fast, you know it's the core fans. People really know what they buy a ticket for." De Witte, reflecting on how little conventional promotion the run required, added, "There was a whole marketing plan and budget for these events that was not necessary." That the shows sold out on reputation and anticipation alone, in the artists' own home country, underlines just how much cultural weight these two names now carry in Belgian dance music.

## Who Are Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte, and Why Do Their Labels Matter?

### Amelie Lens: Antwerp's Acid Techno Export

Born in Vilvoorde in 1990 and raised in Antwerp, Amelie Lens came up through Belgium's club and festival circuit before her breakout 2016 debut EP, "Let It Go," established her as a rising force in a sound built on hypnotic acid lines and relentless low end. In 2018 she founded her own label, Lenske, a platform that has since released underground techno from artists beyond her own catalog, alongside her EXHALE project, a collective, label, and touring event series she built to showcase forward-leaning techno on her own terms.

### Charlotte de Witte: Ghent's Techno Institution

Charlotte de Witte, born in Ghent in 1992, grew her name out of a club night she launched at Fuse in Brussels in 2015 before formalizing it into the label KNTXT in September 2019, according to [DJ Mag's coverage of the launch](https://djmag.com/news/charlotte-de-witte-launch-new-label-kntxt). KNTXT has since grown from club nights into full stage takeovers at Awakenings and Tomorrowland and a release roster that extends well beyond de Witte's own productions, a trajectory detailed in Beatportal's [Label of the Month feature](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1145641-label-of-the-month-kntxt) on the imprint. De Witte has repeatedly topped DJ Mag's techno-specific rankings in recent years, cementing her as one of the genre's most consistently dominant headliners worldwide.

## Why Does a Lens and de Witte Collaboration Matter So Much?

Part of what makes "One Mind" newsworthy is the narrative it quietly dismantles. For years, both artists have been positioned by press, promoters, and fans as rivals, two Belgian women who rose through the same scene at roughly the same time and were constantly measured against each other, often in ways that had less to do with their music than their gender. Both have pushed back on that framing directly. In a joint conversation at Amsterdam Dance Event covered by [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/music/amelie-lens-charlotte-de-witte-comparison-please-stop/), Lens and de Witte asked fans to stop pitting female DJs against one another, describing the constant comparisons as, in their words, emotionally and creatively draining.

Seen against that backdrop, "One Mind" reads less like a marketing stunt and more like a pointed answer. Two artists who could easily have kept competing for the same headline slots instead built something together, under a shared name, on a label that belongs to neither of their existing empires. It is a small, symbolic act with a large audience watching, and it arrived after [DJ Mag reported the pair had been quietly teasing a collaboration](https://djmag.com/news/amelie-lens-and-charlotte-de-witte-tease-collaboration-something-big-coming) for months beforehand, letting the anticipation build organically rather than forcing a rollout.

## Is This Part of Techno's Broader, Harder Turn?

Both Lens and de Witte are regularly cited as reference points whenever writers discuss techno's harder, faster, more mainstream-facing surge over the past few years, the same acceleration RTS.FM examined in its piece on [hard techno's American boom and a scene pushing tempos past 180 BPM](/blog/hard-techno-american-boom). "One Mind" doesn't chase that extreme end of the tempo spectrum; it sits closer to the acid-and-trance-inflected techno both artists built their reputations on. But the sheer scale of the Ghent shows, tens of thousands of people converging on an exhibition hall for a genre that started in warehouses and basements, is itself evidence of how far techno's commercial gravity has shifted in a short span of years, and how central Belgium's biggest names have been to that shift.

If that scale of techno feels like a lot, it's worth remembering the genre still has a healthy underground running in parallel to it. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the name of Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte's joint EP?

The EP is called "One Mind," released February 6, 2026. It contains two original tracks, "One Mind" and "Where Do We Go," and marks the first time the two Belgian techno artists have released music together.

### How many people attended their Ghent shows?

Across three sold-out nights at Flanders Expo in Ghent, Belgium, the two artists drew a combined total of roughly 69,000 attendees, with reports putting the average crowd at around 23,000 per night.

### What labels do Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte run?

Amelie Lens founded Lenske in 2018 and also runs the EXHALE collective, label, and event series. Charlotte de Witte founded KNTXT, which began as a Brussels club night in 2015 and became a full record label in September 2019.

### Is "One Mind" out on Lenske or KNTXT?

Neither. The EP was released through B2B, a new joint imprint created specifically for this collaboration, underscoring that the project belongs equally to both artists rather than sitting inside either one's existing catalog.

### Are Amelie Lens and Charlotte de Witte rivals?

Despite years of media framing that pitted them against each other, both artists have said the comparisons are exhausting and not reflective of their actual relationship. "One Mind" and their joint Ghent shows are widely read as a direct response to that narrative.

Whatever comes next for the pair, "One Mind" has already done its job: it turned two careers that were constantly discussed in relation to each other into a single, shared body of work, on their own label, in their own city.
